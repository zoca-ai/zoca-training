TL;DR

Zoe answers every inbound call and text, 24/7, in the salon's chosen voice. She books, reschedules, and cancels real appointments; answers questions about services, prices, hours, and staff; recognizes returning callers by phone; transfers to a human when needed; and filters out spam. What makes her trustworthy is what she won't do: she never invents a service, price, or time slot, never pretends to be human, and never takes payment. Pitch it as: "Answers every call — and never makes one up."

1. What it is

Zoe is an AI receptionist that picks up the salon's phone and texts around the clock. She holds a natural conversation with the caller, figures out what they want, and acts on it — usually by booking an appointment into the calendar the salon already runs on, or by answering a question and texting a confirmation.

She's not a phone tree ("press 1 for hours"), and she's not voicemail. She's a conversation: the client talks the way they would to a person, and Zoe responds the way a good front-desk person would.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"Think of Zoe as the receptionist who never goes home, never takes a lunch break, and can answer two calls at the same time — and she only books what's actually on your calendar."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

A small salon misses calls all day long, and every missed call is a client who might book down the street instead:

  • You're with a client. Your hands are busy; the phone rings out to voicemail. Most people don't leave one — they call the next salon.
  • It's after hours. A client decides at 9 PM to book a haircut for the weekend. Nobody's there. By morning, they've booked elsewhere.
  • Two calls come in at once. A human can only take one. Zoe takes both — every call is its own separate conversation, so there's no "line busy."
  • It's a robocall. Your time gets wasted on a warranty scam instead of a paying client.
VALUE LINE

"Every missed call is a client you already paid to attract — through ads, your website, word of mouth — walking away at the last second. Zoe's whole job is to make sure that call gets answered."

RULE OF THUMB

Three numbers carry the voice pitch: pickup in under one second, the missed-call rate drops from a typical ~22% to about ~3% on the Zoca network, and Zoe handles 12 languages from day one. Lead with those when a prospect's pain is the phone.

3. How it works — the anatomy of a call

Every call follows the same six beats. Knowing them lets you narrate a demo confidently.

StageWhat happens
RingThe call (forwarded from the salon's line) reaches Zoe's dedicated number.
AnswerZoe picks up in the salon's chosen voice with a warm greeting.
IdentifyIf the caller is a returning client, Zoe recognizes them by their phone number and can greet them by name.
UnderstandZoe figures out the intent — book, reschedule, cancel, ask a question, or reach a human.
ActShe looks up real services, checks real availability, books or modifies the appointment, or answers the question.
Wrap-upThe call ends, an AI summary is written, the call is tagged (booked, spam, sales call, etc.), and any follow-up is scheduled.

A standard booking call runs about 60–120 seconds; a quick "what are your hours?" is 30–60 seconds; a messy reschedule can take 4–5 minutes.

GOOD TO KNOW

Returning callers are matched on the phone number they're calling from — pulled from the salon's client list and booking history. That's how Zoe can say "Hi Maria, welcome back" without anyone typing anything. (Recalling what Maria booked last time, or her preferences across calls, is on the roadmap — see Training & memory.)

OBJECTION

"What if it just makes stuff up like AI does?" — That's exactly the line we drew. Zoe only books what's actually on your calendar, only quotes prices from your real service list, and only offers times you actually have open. If a caller asks something she doesn't know, she doesn't guess — she says she'll have someone follow up, or she transfers. Honest answer: she's very good, not perfect, and she'll occasionally make a mistake — but she's built so the mistakes aren't "invented a fake appointment."

5. Where it lives + demo path

Zoe lives behind the salon's phone number — there's no app the client downloads; they just call. On the owner's side, everything Zoe did shows up in the Front Desk dashboard (see Dashboard & lead recovery).

To demo "what Zoe does," place a real call to a demo number — this is the single most convincing thing you can do:

  1. Have a Front Desk demo account connected to a scheduler.
  2. Call the demo number on speaker. Let the prospect hear Zoe greet, answer a question, and book.
  3. Then open the Activity feed on the dashboard and show the call you just made — transcript, summary, and the booking that landed on the calendar.
DEMO TIP

A live call beats any slide. Pre-plan a tight script: ask a price question, then book an appointment, then ask "are you a real person?" so the prospect hears Zoe disclose she's AI. Test calls are free and don't count against the 100-minute quota, so rehearse it beforehand. Confirm button labels in your own account before the call.

DEMO TIP

Beware of placing two calls into the same demo account at once — the system can mix the summaries together. Use one call at a time during a live demo.

7. FAQ & objection handling

"How does it know it's a returning client?" By the phone number they're calling from. Zoe matches it against your client list and booking history, so she can greet regulars by name. (Pulling up what they got last time is coming; recognizing them is live.)

"What's the spam shield — does it block calls before they ring?" Not pre-answer. Zoe answers, and then very short calls (under about 10 seconds) and obvious robocalls get tagged as spam/abandoned after the call, so they don't clutter your activity feed or trigger follow-ups. There's no third-party caller-reputation database today — it's based on how the call actually went. Be honest: it filters the obvious junk, it's not a perfect spam wall.

"What happens if Zoe can't handle something?" She transfers to a human during your forwarding hours, or — if it's after hours — she takes a message and can fall back to texting the caller. She doesn't bluff.

"Will it pretend to be me or my receptionist?" No. If a caller asks whether they're talking to a person, Zoe discloses she's the AI assistant. That's a deliberate design choice for trust and compliance.

"Can it take payment over the phone?" No — Zoe doesn't collect payment or deposits. She books the appointment; you take payment your normal way. (More on why in Booking & platforms.)

"What are these 'dispositions' I see on calls?" Just plain-English tags for what a call was: a new appointment, a reschedule or cancel, a spam call, a sales call, a job inquiry, a complaint, a general question handled, or one that needs a callback. They make the activity feed easy to scan.

8. Key terms

  • Inbound call — a call coming into the salon that Zoe answers.
  • Intent — what the caller wants (book / reschedule / cancel / ask / reach a human).
  • Returning-caller recognition — matching the caller's phone number to an existing client.
  • Transfer / handoff — Zoe passing a live call to a human during forwarding hours.
  • Spam shield — post-call tagging of very short calls and robocalls so they're filtered out.
  • Disposition — the plain-English category a call gets tagged with afterward.
  • Fall through to SMS — when a call can't be completed, Zoe follows up by text.
  • Disclosure — Zoe stating she's AI when a caller asks.
  • Multilingual — Zoe speaks 12 languages from day one, in the caller's language, with the same booking ability.
  • Pickup latency — time from ring to answer; spec is sub-1-second. Missed-call rate moves from a typical ~22% to ~3% on the Zoca network.
