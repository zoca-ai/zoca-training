TL;DR

The Front Desk dashboard has six tabs (Overview, Analytics, Activity, Bookings, Training, Settings). The Activity feed is the inbox — every call and text Zoe handled, with transcripts and recordings (read-only in v1). L2B (Lead-to-Booking) is the lead-recovery engine: when someone abandons a web booking, Zoe texts them back and re-books them right in the thread. The Overview shows an estimated impact (revenue saved, time saved) — present those as estimates, not audited numbers. Pitch it as: "See every call she caught — and the ones she won back."

1. What it is

The dashboard is where the owner watches Zoe work. They can read every call and message, see how she's performing, and review the appointments she booked. Alongside it, L2B is the quiet money-maker: it chases the people who started booking online and gave up, and turns a chunk of them back into appointments.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"Everything Zoe does shows up in one place — every call, every text, every booking — so you're never wondering what happened while you were busy. And the leads who almost booked on your website? Zoe texts them back to finish the job."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

Owners want two things from a tool like this: proof it's working and money they wouldn't have gotten otherwise. The dashboard gives the proof; L2B gives the found money.

  • Proof. Read the actual transcripts. Hear the recordings. See the bookings on the calendar. No black box.
  • Found money. Most people who start an online booking don't finish. L2B texts them and re-books a meaningful share of them — appointments the salon would simply have lost.
VALUE LINE

"Half the people who start booking on a salon website never finish. That's not a website problem — it's life: they got distracted, a kid yelled, the bus came. L2B is Zoe tapping them on the shoulder a few minutes later: 'still want that Saturday slot?' — and a good number say yes."

3. How it works — the six tabs

TabWhat's in it
OverviewStatus (Ready to go live / Live / Paused), six metric cards (Calls Answered, Bookings Made, Leads Engaged, After-Hours Calls, Spam Filtered, Avg Call Length), and an Impact section (estimated revenue saved $ + staff time saved hrs).
AnalyticsA rolling last-30-days view with widgets: Call Breakdown, Spam Shield, After-Hours (with a heatmap), Lead Recovery, Staff Time Saved, Conversion Trend.
ActivityThe chronological feed of every call and SMS Zoe handled — this is the inbox. Expand any item for the transcript, an AI summary, and an inline recording player. Filter by Today / This week / This month, or search a client.
BookingsThe appointments Zoe created (mostly Confirmed; some awaiting payment, awaiting approval, declined, or failed).
TrainingTeaching Zoe — see Training & memory.
SettingsConnect/resync the scheduler, the forwarding wizard, booking preferences (deposits off), and agent settings (voice, name, transfer hours).
GOOD TO KNOW

The Activity feed is the inbox — there's no separate "Inbox" tab. In v1 it's read-only: the owner can read a full SMS thread but can't jump in and reply as themselves from the feed. To reach a customer directly, they use their normal channel. (Human take-over of a live SMS thread — owner steps in, Zoe pauses, Zoe resumes — is on the roadmap.)

GOOD TO KNOW

One inline action that matters: "Save to Brain." From any transcript or chat line, the owner can push something straight into Zoe's knowledge — turning a real conversation into a permanent answer (see Training & memory).

4. L2B — lead-to-booking recovery

When someone uses the salon's website to book and stops before finishing (picked services, chose a time, never hit confirm), that creates a lead record — and Zoe follows up. The recovery happens by SMS today, and the magic is that the booking tools live inside the text thread: the customer can confirm right there without calling back.

The follow-up cadence. Zoe sends a sequence of gentle nudges with growing gaps between them. A typical sequence is roughly 2 hours → 1 day → 3 days → 1 week → 2 weeks → ~1 month after the drop-off. The exact timing is configurable — you decide — and the first nudge is "minutes, not hours" out for the freshest leads.

  • Throttled — no more than a couple of texts per hour per customer, so it never feels like spam.
  • Opt-out — a "stop" reply opts the customer out automatically (compliance handled).
  • Spam and sales calls get no follow-up — only real leads get chased.
GOOD TO KNOW

Abandoned-lead follow-ups surface in the Activity feed — there's no separate "Enquiries" tab inside Front Desk. The broader lead pipeline lives in Zoca's Discovery product; in Front Desk, the Lead Recovery widget in Analytics is the at-a-glance view (enquiries received, how fast Zoe engaged, how many converted).

ROADMAP

L2B is SMS-first today. An outbound voice callback to abandoned leads is on the roadmap; for now, voice abandonment flows into the SMS follow-up. Group bookings are also roadmap (single-client bookings only today).

5. The impact / ROI framing — handle honestly

The Overview's Impact section and Analytics put numbers on Zoe's value — revenue saved, staff time saved, recovery rates. These are helpful for the story but they are estimates, not audited results.

OBJECTION

"Is this 'revenue saved' number real?" — Be straight: it's an estimate the system calculates to show the kind of value Zoe drives, not an audited accounting figure. Use it directionally ("here's the scale of what she's catching"), never as a guarantee.

VALUE LINE

When you cite abandonment or recovery figures — e.g. ~40–60% of web bookings get abandoned and a recovery target of ~25–40% — always flag them as illustrative. Say: "industry-typical ranges, not a promise about your numbers." These are examples in our training material, not measured Zoca metrics.

8. FAQ & objection handling

"Where do I see what Zoe actually said?" The Activity feed — every call and text, with the full transcript, an AI summary, and the recording. Nothing hidden.

"Can I reply to a customer's text from the dashboard?" Not in v1 — the feed is read-only, so you'd reach them through your normal channel. Stepping into a live SMS thread (Zoe pauses, you take over, Zoe resumes) is on the roadmap.

"How does the lead recovery work — does it call them?" Today it texts them, and they can finish booking right in the thread. It's throttled so it's never spammy, and a 'stop' reply opts them out. Voice callbacks are on the roadmap.

"Is the 'revenue saved' number guaranteed?" No — it's an estimate to show the scale of value. The bookings and calls themselves are real; the dollar figure is directional.

"What if the abandonment/recovery percentages don't match my results?" Those are industry-typical illustrative ranges, not a promise about your salon. Your actual numbers will be your own.

"Does it chase spam or sales calls too?" No — only real leads get follow-up. Spam and sales calls are filtered out.
