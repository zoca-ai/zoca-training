1. What it is

Front Desk is sold as a flat monthly subscription. The headline price gets the salon Zoe (voice + SMS + lead recovery), a dedicated number, one voice, the full Training tab, one scheduler connection, the dashboard, and a pool of included voice minutes. It can be bought on its own or bundled with Zoca's Discovery product.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"Ninety-nine bucks a month, flat. That's your AI receptionist answering calls and texts around the clock — for less than four hours of a human receptionist's wages."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

The comparison that closes deals isn't against other AI tools — it's against the cost of a human and the cost of missed calls.

  • A human receptionist runs roughly $25/hour, which is easily a few thousand dollars a month — and they go home at five, take lunch, get sick, and can only take one call at a time.
  • Missed calls cost real bookings the salon already paid (in ads and effort) to generate.
  • Flat, predictable pricing beats the per-minute or per-caller-tier billing most competitors use — the owner knows exactly what they'll pay.
VALUE LINE

"You're not choosing between Zoe and nothing — you're choosing between Zoe at ninety-nine a month and the calls you're losing right now. She pays for herself the first time she books an after-hours client you'd have missed."

4. The trial and the pause offer

  • 7-day free trial — fully free, but a card on file is required. The clock starts once onboarding is complete and the subscription is set up; the first $99 charges automatically at the end unless they cancel.
  • Pause offer — if a seasonal salon or a wavering customer wants to step back, there's a $29/mo pause capped at 60 days, one time per account, and the number is held so they don't lose it. Use this as a save, not a lead opener.
DEMO TIP

The trial is your best closing tool: "Try her free for a week — make her answer your phone, listen to the recordings, see the bookings land. If she's not earning her keep, cancel before day seven and pay nothing." Low risk for them, high conviction for you.

6. Objection handling (the big four)

OBJECTION

#10 — "I don't want AI to answer my calls." Reframe to "never miss a call." Zoe isn't replacing you — she catches the calls you'd otherwise lose: after-hours, when you're with a client, when two ring at once. You stay in control; she transfers to you during your hours. Start her in "after hours" or "no answer" mode so she only takes the calls you're missing anyway.

OBJECTION

#11 — "My clients don't like AI." Most of the calls Zoe catches are ones that would've gone to voicemail — and most people hang up on voicemail. Returning clients are recognized by their number, she books fast, and she texts a confirmation. If a caller asks, she's honest that she's the AI assistant. Try the trial and listen to the recordings — owners are usually surprised how natural it is.

OBJECTION

#12 — "I already have a receptionist. Why do I need this?" It's coverage, not replacement: 24/7, simultaneous calls, after-hours, spam filtering, plus winning back leads that didn't book. A human is ~$25/hr — a few thousand a month — and can't be in two places at once. Zoe is $99/mo and backs up your front desk on everything they can't physically catch.

OBJECTION

#13 — "Why is your AI better than the other AI calling tools?" Because she actually books into your existing scheduler end-to-end on the call — most of them just send a booking link or only support Calendly/Google Calendar. Plus lead recovery, flat pricing, and salon-specific depth. See the table above; the difference is real and demoable.
