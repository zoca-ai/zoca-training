TL;DR

Owners ask "do you integrate with [X]?" on nearly every demo, and the answer they're really listening for is "do I have to switch?" The pitch is simple: we don't rip out your booking app — we slot in alongside it. Their platform owns the inside (calendar, payments, client records, staff scheduling); Zoca owns everything before a client arrives and after they leave (getting found, answering inbound, post-visit loyalty). Know the integration list cold, lead with "we work with it," and when a prospect's platform isn't on the list, treat it as a migration/integration conversation — never a hard no. The deal stays alive; you just set honest expectations about setup.

1. Why this is the conversation that unlocks the demo

Every owner who's been in business 2+ years has invested in a booking platform. They moved their clients onto it, trained staff on it, and built their policies around it. The idea of switching — even to something better — feels like a root canal. The good news for your sales cycle: we don't ask them to switch. How early and clearly you communicate that determines whether the rest of the demo even happens.

SAY IT SIMPLY

Open every demo with "we don't replace [their platform]" inside the first 90 seconds. Until that anxiety is cleared, the owner is half-listening — bracing for the migration ask that never comes.

TALK TRACK

Opening line. "First thing — you're on Booksy? Great. We don't replace Booksy. We work with it. Let's talk about what Zoca actually does so you understand where we fit."

KEY TERM

Payments — orchestrate, don't process. Zoca doesn't process payments itself; that stays inside the booking platform. When Front Desk Agent takes a deposit to hold an appointment, it calls back into the booking system's payment flow (Booksy, Vagaro, Square, etc.), sends the client a link, and confirms when it's paid.

4. What we DON'T integrate with (and don't claim we do)

Being honest about the edges builds more trust than overclaiming. If an owner names one of these, say so plainly.

  • Generic CRMs (HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive) — we're not a CRM; we don't sync contacts to one.
  • Email marketing platforms (Mailchimp, Klaviyo, ActiveCampaign) — Loyalty runs on SMS/DM primarily; email isn't our channel.
  • Yelp — read-only for review-visibility signals; no posting or response automation.
  • QuickBooks / accounting — out of scope.
FOR THE AE

The crisp answer format. When an owner asks "do you integrate with [X]?": "Yes — Front Desk Agent books directly into [X] and Loyalty reads visit history from it for rebook timing." If it's genuinely not supported yet: "Not directly today — the most common route with [X] is to pair Zoca with one of our live platforms, or use Zoca to collect the booking intent and route it to you. If [X] is a hard requirement, I'll flag it for our product team." If you're unsure: "Let me confirm the integration depth for that system after this call — I want to give you a real answer, not a guess."

5. The "we don't rip out your booking app" pitch

This is the heart of the lesson. The owner's fear isn't about Zoca — it's about loss. Name the fear, then dissolve it.

What they sayWhat they're actually worried about
"I just trained my staff on Vagaro"Training cost. Operational disruption.
"My clients are used to the Booksy app"Customer friction. Losing clients in the transition.
"I have 2 years of history in Square"Data loss. Analytics continuity.
"I don't want to pay for 2 systems"Double-billing. Justifying incremental cost.

Your opening line neutralizes the first three. The fourth — "why pay for two?" — is a real conversation, but it's the pricing/ROI conversation (a different lesson), not a switching conversation. Don't let it pull you back into defending the integration.

6. When a prospect WANTS to switch

Sometimes an owner is genuinely unhappy with their booking platform and asks if Zoca replaces it. Resist the temptation to say yes — we don't build booking, and saying yes sets up a bad fit.

TALK TRACK

"We're not a booking platform — we wouldn't be great at it, because that's not where we focus. If you're unhappy with what you've got, I'd honestly recommend looking at GlossGenius or Fresha separately — those are the two we run deepest with. Zoca sits next to whichever one you pick. And the good news — switching booking platforms is a one-time pain, and Zoca's the kind of tool you'd want either way."

This is a long-game move. You just saved them from buying the wrong thing, and they'll remember you for it.

7. When the platform isn't live yet — it's a migration conversation, not a no

This is the rule that matters most: a not-yet-integrated booking platform is never a hard "no." It's a migration/integration conversation. The prospect can still become a customer; your job is to scope the setup honestly, not to turn them away.

RULE OF THUMB

Best-fit → the integration is clean (today that's GlossGenius or Fresha), lead with confidence. Off-list / not-yet-live platform → still a real deal; you flag the extra setup step and let them decide. We don't say "we can't take you." We say "here's the setup path to get you onto a platform we run cleanly with."

TALK TRACK

Off-list platform (migration framing). "You're exactly the kind of business Zoca is built for. The one wrinkle is your booking platform isn't one we run deepest with yet — so there's a migration step to get you onto something like GlossGenius or Fresha. Totally doable; I just want you to know it's part of the setup, not a surprise later."

COMMON MISCONCEPTION

"If they're not on a supported platform, the deal's dead." Retired framing. A platform gap is a setup conversation — scope the migration, set expectations, and keep selling. The only true hard prerequisite anywhere in onboarding is a laptop or iPad in the shop; everything else flexes.

8. The five integration-friction patterns (and how to handle each)

Most integrations are clean. A few aren't. Knowing the friction patterns in advance saves you from mid-demo surprises and avoidable post-sale churn. In every case the move is the same: be honest, reshape the deal, and let them choose — not decline.

  1. Legacy platform with no public API. A few older systems (some regional booking tools, custom-built calendars) don't expose an API. Zoca can't book directly into the calendar, but still adds value through Discovery, Front Desk Agent on other channels (text, DMs), and Loyalty.

> "Honest answer — [X] doesn't give integrators calendar access. We can still handle Discovery, inbound response, and post-visit loyalty. For booking itself, we'd collect the intent and hand off to you. It's a different deal shape — let's see if it still makes sense for you."

  1. Locked-down enterprise deployments. Some multi-location brands run customized MindBody or Booksy Enterprise setups where admin access is controlled by corporate, not the owner you're selling to. Integrations need corporate sign-off.

> Handle it: scope a longer discovery that includes the corporate decision-maker. (5+ locations is enterprise territory — escalate rather than trying to close solo.)

  1. Dual-systems migration. Owner switched platforms recently — half their history is in the old one, half in the new one. Data is fragmented.

> "Loyalty Agent will start cleanly from your current system's visit data. Clients who only exist in your old system won't get re-engagement messages until they come back in. That's a 30–60 day transition. Workable for you?" — usually yes.

  1. Custom service catalog / variable pricing. Some salons price by stylist level, hair length, or time of day. If the booking platform doesn't expose that cleanly, Front Desk Agent can't quote exact prices.

> "For services with complex pricing, we'll train Front Desk Agent to say 'pricing starts at $X, final quote confirmed after we see you.' Most clients accept that. If exact AI-quoted pricing is a dealbreaker for you, tell me now so we set it up right."

  1. Integration requires the owner's technical setup. Some connections (Instagram Business Profile, Meta Page permissions) require the owner to complete OAuth flows. If they're not technical, onboarding can stall.

> "Day 1 onboarding includes connecting your Instagram and Google accounts — about 10 minutes, fully guided, someone from our team walks you through it. Just so you know it's a real step, not magic."

9. When to slow down (not walk away)

Some combinations are genuinely high-friction. They don't mean "decline" — they mean pause, re-scope, and set firmer expectations before you close, so you don't sell a setup that churns.

  • Legacy no-API platform + owner insists on calendar-level booking → reshape to the Discovery/Front Desk/Loyalty deal, or scope a migration first.
  • Enterprise setup + no access to corporate decision-makers → escalate and bring corporate into the room.
  • Complex variable pricing + owner insists on exact AI-quoted pricing → set the "pricing starts at $X" expectation in writing before signing.
  • Owner unwilling to connect Google/Instagram during onboarding → surface it now; the agents need those connections to deliver.
WHY IT MATTERS

These aren't disqualifiers — they're expectation-setting moments. A deal closed without scoping them creates a disproportionately expensive CX burden and churn. A deal closed with eyes open sticks. The honest scope protects both sides.

10. The 30-second pre-demo check

Before every demo, run this in your head from the qualifying-call notes:

  1. What booking platform are they on?
  2. Is it live today (GlossGenius / Fresha, or a confirmed near-live one)? If yes — lead with "we work with it." If not — prep the migration/integration framing (Section 7), not a no.
  3. Any friction patterns? (enterprise access, dual-systems history, variable pricing, technical hesitation) — have the honest-scope line ready.
