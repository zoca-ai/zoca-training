1. What Zoca is, in one line

SAY IT SIMPLY

"Zoca is the marketing + voice platform for hyperlocal beauty and wellness businesses." Read it again. Every word is load-bearing.

  • Marketing + voice — both, in one platform, sharing one brain. Not a marketing tool that bolted on voice. Not a voice tool that bolted on SEO. One system.
  • Hyperlocal — every location of every brand gets its own intelligence. A salon in DTLA doesn't market the same way as a salon in Nashville, because their customers don't behave the same way. We treat them differently on purpose.
  • Beauty and wellness — hair, med-spa, nails, lashes, brows, barbershops, wellness studios, and the in-vertical specialties (tattoo, piercing, permanent makeup). This is the vertical we're built for and the story we lead with. (Why the vertical is the moat comes later in the track.)

If a prospect asks "what is Zoca?" — that one line is the answer. Not "an AI agency." Not "an SMB SaaS." Not "the future of beauty." That line.

2. Say it cold, in one breath

This is the version you deliver when an owner picks up cold and asks what this is:

TALK TRACK

"Zoca is the marketing + voice platform for hyperlocal beauty and wellness businesses. We get you ranked on Google and inside ChatGPT for what you actually sell, and we pick up every call your front desk misses. One platform, one contract."

Practice it out loud until it lands in one breath, with no commas where you stop to think.

COMMON MISCONCEPTION

Burying the one-liner under three sentences of warm-up — "so what we do is, basically, we're kind of like an AI thing for…" Cut all of it. Lead with the line. Owners decide in the first 10 seconds whether you're worth their next 20 minutes.

There's also a 25-words-or-fewer version for when "what does Zoca actually do for me?" comes mid-call:

SAY IT SIMPLY

"We fill your chair. Every chair, every location. We handle Google, your phone, and your rebooks — automatically. You don't touch marketing again." (24 words.)

3. Why Zoca exists — fill the chair

Local beauty businesses run on full chairs. When a chair sits empty, the owner never gets that hour back. The whole company rolls up to closing four leaks that drain those chairs:

#The leakWhat it costs the owner
1New bookings / visibilityA new or quiet location nobody can find — great stylists, zero footprint.
2Missed callsThe phone rings, nobody picks up, the booking walks three doors down.
3Mid-booking drop-offClients start to book and bail before they finish.
4First-timer churnThe most expensive customer they'll ever pay for is gone after one visit.

Owners feel every one of these. But they can't fix them — they're cutting hair, doing facials, training staff, running payroll. No time, no marketing skill, no budget for a $2,000-a-month agency that takes six weeks to publish a blog post. So they live with the leak: empty slots, missed calls, lost rebooks, quiet weeks they can't explain.

WHY IT MATTERS

Zoca closes that leak — completely, automatically, with the owner learning nothing about marketing. That's the whole job. Every feature we ship, every call you take, every contract you sign rolls up to one outcome: fill the chair, at every location, every day.

4. Who Zoca is for — best-fit, not a gate

Zoca is sharpest for a beauty or wellness business that has crossed from "side hustle" into "real business." Best-fit shows up when at least one of these is true:

  • they run more than one location, or
  • they employ at least one person besides the owner, or
  • they do more than $100K/year in revenue.

That's the picture where the four leaks are biggest and Zoca closes them fastest — so it's where you spend your energy first and lead with the most confidence.

RULE OF THUMB

Best-fit → move fast, lead with confidence. Lower-fit → still a real, sellable conversation; just be honest about what success looks like at their stage. We don't say "you don't qualify." We say "here's what Zoca will and won't move for a shop like yours right now."

The solo operator working from a spare room at $40K — no staff, one location — is lower-fit, not a no. The wins are smaller and slower, and you say so. The fit lifts the day they add a chair, a second location, or cross six figures. Beauty & wellness is where we're sharpest, but it isn't a wall either — an out-of-vertical owner who wants in can buy, as long as you're honest that the category-specific intelligence is tuned for beauty. (The full best-fit picture and how to read it on a call is the next lesson.)

RULE OF THUMB

⛔ The one hard rule in the whole curriculum: a laptop or iPad must be in the shop. Onboarding and the demo can't run without it. If there's no device, the move is "let's get a laptop or iPad at the front desk first — usually a quick fix — then we onboard." Everything else flexes.

COMMON MISCONCEPTION

Treating the threshold as a velvet rope. It isn't. It tells you where to aim and how to set expectations — not who to turn away.

You'll be tempted to soften that. Don't. Owners who want to bet on a company that outlasts them respond to ambition. Owners who want a comfortable mid-tier vendor were never our fit anyway. And note where you're joining: the product is six months old in its current form, there's no senior comparable in India to copy, and the customers we sign this quarter are setting the blueprint the next 100 reps will run. We're figuring it out as we go — that's the job, not a bug in it.

7. What the job really is, on day 1

You're an AE or an AM. Either way, you are the surface area between Zoca and the customer. The product is good, the market is real, the dream is big — and none of it matters if you can't do four things:

  1. Fill chairs. The job is not selling software. It's putting bookings in the chair. Every conversation traces back to that outcome.
  2. Tell the story in one breath. By the end of day 1 you can explain what Zoca is, who it's for, and why it works in under 60 seconds. Owners don't have time for a 40-slide deck.
  3. Hear the actual problem. Owners say they want "more bookings." That's rarely the real issue — it's usually one of the four leaks. Your job is to find which one and name it back to them.
  4. Be honest about fit. If we're not their sharpest fit, you say so — and you still sell them honestly if they want in. That's value 04: be honest, full stop. You'll lose deals by promising things that aren't true; you'll keep customers for years by being the rep who leveled with them.
OBJECTION

"Are you guys an agency?" — "No, the opposite. Agencies sell you marketing labor — junior staff, monthly PDFs, $2,000 a month. We sell you the outcome: bookings in the chair. Different business."

What worked: led with the one-liner cold, no buildup; named the leaks by number before pitching features; read fit inside three minutes without it feeling like an interrogation; and closed to the demo, not the price. Price comes after the dashboard.

9. Selling honestly when they're not your sharpest fit

Best-fit isn't a wall, and "lower-fit" isn't a brush-off. When an owner is below the threshold or outside the sharpest vertical, you don't refuse them — you tell the truth about what Zoca moves at their stage and let them decide.

TALK TRACK

Lower-fit, said cleanly: "The platform really hits its stride once you've outgrown doing it all yourself — multiple locations, staff, missed calls piling up. At where you are right now, some of what you'd pay for is capacity you don't need yet. If you want in, I'll set you up straight and we'll be honest about the timeline — and the day you add a chair or cross six figures, the value steps up fast. If you'd rather wait, I'll keep your contact and we pick it up then."

COMMON MISCONCEPTION

Saying "you don't qualify" or "you're too small." Never. Frame everything as the product's fit and stage — "here's what it'll move for you right now" — not a verdict on the customer. (The full best-fit playbook, including out-of-vertical owners, is the next lesson.)
