TL;DR

One product, two numbers, one math story — and a billing model you can explain in a sentence. Every new logo buys Discovery Neo (Discovery Agent + Front Desk Agent, bundled, can't be split). The reveal is two prices: [annual plan price]/month on annual and [monthly plan price]/month monthly — pending final confirmation, and you reject every stale number you see anywhere else. 1. What you're selling: Discovery Neo + add-ons

Every new customer buys the same core. Memorize the packaging before you memorize the price.

KEY TERM

Discovery Neo — the bundled core product: Discovery Agent + Front Desk Agent, sold together, can't be unbundled. This is what every new logo gets. Same rate per location.

Everything else is an add-on — sold as upside, turned on when the customer wants it, never bundled into the base and never sold with a launch date.

2. The two numbers — say them like this

SAY IT SIMPLY

"[annual plan price]/month on a 1-year contract. [monthly plan price]/month month-to-month. Discovery Agent and Front Desk Agent — bundled, can't be unbundled. Same per location."

That's the whole reveal. No qualifying, no "well, it depends." Two numbers, then stop talking. Reps who fumble pricing fumble closes — drill it until "what does this cost?" gets an automatic answer.

SOURCE NOTE

Pricing is pending final confirmation. Until Sales Ops confirms, use the literal placeholders [annual plan price] and [monthly plan price] in every role-play and quote.

COMPLIANCE NOTE

Stale numbers — reject on sight. $179, $249, $269, $279, $290 are all dead. They appear in old modules, old settings panels, prior product docs, and old meeting minutes — every one is wrong. If a product UI surface still shows one, ignore it; RevOps is updating those screens. The only correct numbers are [annual plan price] (annual) and [monthly plan price] (monthly).

3. The setup fee — your reason to go annual

There is a setup fee. It is waived only for customers who choose the yearly plan — the waiver is baked into annual pricing.

FOR THE AE

Never say "no setup fee" — that's stale wording. The setup fee exists; it's simply waived on annual. The amount is TBD/pending — never state a number. Frame it as a concrete, money-on-the-table reason to commit to the year.

TALK TRACK

"One more thing that makes annual the easy call — there's a setup fee on monthly, and it's waived entirely when you go annual. So annual locks your rate, skips the setup fee, and gets you the better monthly number. Three reasons, one decision."

4. Annual vs. monthly — read the signals, pick the close

Two prices, two close paths. There is no discounting beyond this — the annual rate is the discount, full stop.

COMPLIANCE NOTE

No discounts, no floor. AEs have no pricing floor. No founding-customer pricing, no cohort discounts, no time-pressure framing. The annual plan is the discount vs. monthly. There is no second discount to give.

Push annual when…Push monthly when…
Multi-location, or owner-operator with steady revenueCash-tight, or wants flexibility for the first 90 days
Comfortable with annual commitments (e.g. ex-agency)Brand-new to AI tooling and wants the off-ramp
Asked about "discount" or "best price" (annual is the discount)Explicitly asked "can I cancel?"
Discovery answers already point to a 6+ month commitmentBusiness under ~6 months old / intent still unclear
TALK TRACK

The annual close. "[annual plan price]/month on annual is the price I'd want for you. It locks you at this rate — when new agents ship and pricing moves up, you stay at [annual plan price]. It's basically the discount, but you keep it. Plus the setup fee's waived. I'll send the signature link, you sign now, we onboard tomorrow."

TALK TRACK

The monthly close. "[monthly plan price]/month if you want the off-ramp. Cancel any time with 30 days' notice. Most folks switch to annual after 60 days once they see the numbers — and we re-rate you to [annual plan price] at that point. Start safe today, get the better rate when you're confident."

COMMON MISCONCEPTION

Saying "no contracts." Public-facing copy is "no long-term contracts" — annual is a 1-year service agreement, monthly is a 30-day notice. Don't say "no contracts"; it contradicts the actual agreement.

5. The agency-cost math — the close that does the work

This is the cudgel. Run the line items in order; the savings number lands the deal.

VALUE LINE

"What you'd pay an agency to get what Zoca delivers in 12 months: content writer (96 blog posts at $150) — $14,400; GBP manager ($300/mo) — $3,600; citation service — $500/yr; web designer — $3,000 one-time; SEO specialist ($1,500/mo) — $18,000; review management tool ($100/mo) — $1,200. That's $40,700 in agency equivalents. Zoca on annual is [annual plan total/yr] — about 91% less. And that's before the Front Desk Agent, which has no agency equivalent, because nobody else does it."

SOURCE NOTE

Don't reuse the stale self-comparison number from old product docs (it assumed $279/mo). Recompute against our real numbers: [annual plan price] × 12 = [annual plan total/yr] and [monthly plan price] × 12 = [monthly plan total/yr], both ~91% under $40,700.

When a prospect pushes on price, don't defend the number — convert their pain into dollars.

TALK TRACK

The leak-math reframe. "[annual plan price] sounds like the price — let me show you the cost. You said you're missing 18 calls a week. That's 72 a month, ~30% would've booked, $85 ticket — that's $1,836/month walking past you. We charge [annual plan price]. Even halving that math, we're positive by month one."

FOR THE AE

Two common mistakes: (1) Defending Zoca — listing everything it does. The prospect doesn't care what Zoca does; they care what their leak costs. (2) Accepting the objection's premise. Reject it cleanly, then reframe. Move every price conversation from price-of-Zoca to cost-of-leak.

6. When and how to reveal price

Reveal at minute 18 of the demo — not minute 4, not minute 12. The prospect needs the value loaded (audit reveal, the four promises, the Test Call) before the number hits, or they spend the rest of the demo pushing on it.

TALK TRACK

Early-price hold (asked at minute 4). "Fair question — I'll get to it in about 12 minutes. I want you to see what you're paying for first. Told now without context, it'll feel high. Told after the Test Call, it'll feel like a no-brainer. Stick with me."

After you reveal, stop talking for 4–6 seconds. The deal turns in the silence — don't fill it.

OBJECTION

"Is this all in?" — "Base bundle is [annual plan price] or [monthly plan price] — Discovery Agent and Front Desk Agent, all in. Two things on top, only if you want them: Loyalty Agent, $49/mo; Conversion Agent, $49 plus 10% of recovered bookings, upside-only, needs the free Zoca payment rail. Ads pricing's still landing. Everything else is in the base."

7. The close mechanics — checklist, recap, contract

RULE OF THUMB

The end-of-call checklist runs in the last 3 minutes of every demo. No exceptions. Miss one item and you've ended the call, not closed it.

  1. Named the deal? [annual plan price] annual / [monthly plan price] monthly + which add-ons (B&P / Loyalty $49 / Conversion $49 + 10%). Said out loud and written in the recap.
  2. Set the next action? Signature link sent, second call calendared, or AM handoff scheduled. Pick one.
  3. Captured the right contact? Decision-maker email and phone — not the front desk's.
  4. Agreed the timing? Start date, billing cycle, onboarding slot.
  5. Surfaced blockers? Legal review, partner approval, IT audit — name them so they don't blindside you.
FOR THE AE

The recap email is a work order, not a sales letter — four lines, plain text: (1) the leak you'd fix first, (2) pricing as quoted ([annual plan price]/mo annual or [monthly plan price]/mo monthly; bundle = Discovery + Front Desk), (3) the next step with a date, (4) "Anything I missed?" No marketing copy, no second pitch, no "no contracts," no number different from what you said live.

OBJECTION

"Send me a contract to review." Bimodal. Real if they named a legal reviewer, asked about specific terms, or were ready to sign. Stall if they couldn't name a reviewer, asked about no specific term, or it surfaced the second you said the price. Real → send within 24 hours with a follow-up calendared. Stall → don't send; triage: "What specifically do you want to see in it? Term length, exit, something else?"

8. The billing & subscription model (authoritative)

This is how the account actually bills. It's deliberately simple — explain it the same way every time.

KEY TERM

One subscription, one billing cycle. Each user has a single subscription on a single cycle. Adding agents joins that same subscription on the same cycle; renewals consolidate all active agents into the one renewal. There are no separate per-agent subscriptions — ever.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"You don't end up with five bills. Everything you turn on — Loyalty, Conversion, whatever — rides on the one subscription, renews on the one date. One account, one cycle."

Upgrade (monthly → yearly). One-click and low-friction. The customer chooses when it takes effect:

  • At the end of the current cycle — finish the month already paid for, then start annual.
  • Immediately — switch to annual right now.

After the upgrade, the user lands on the subscription page.

TALK TRACK

The annual upgrade move (~90-day mark). "Sixty days in you've got real numbers — call me, I'll move you to annual at [annual plan price] and reset the lock. It's one click; you choose whether it starts now or at your next renewal. You stayed safe today, you get the better rate now that you're confident."

Downgrade. The customer can turn an agent off or keep it — their call. Key facts: no mid-cycle refund, the subscription structure is unchanged, and no new subscription is created. They simply stop paying for that agent at the next cycle.

FOR THE AE

Downgrade isn't churn — it's a customer right-sizing. There's nothing to rebuild on the back end: same subscription, same cycle, one agent toggled off. Be straight that turning something off mid-cycle doesn't refund the remainder of the period.

Cancellation. Cancellation lives in its own dedicated page/flow, separate from upgrade/downgrade. The flow is broadly the same for everyone, with monthly vs. yearly handled distinctly (monthly is a 30-day-notice termination; annual is the 1-year service agreement, handled per its terms).

FOR THE AE

This lesson owns the cancellation mechanics — where it happens and how monthly vs. yearly differ. The retention / churn-prevention play — how an AM saves the account before it cancels — lives in the AM lifecycle lesson. Know the mechanics here; run the save-play there.

9. Multi-location pricing — flag and route

For 2–4 locations, it's per-location at the same base rate. For 5+ / franchise networks, the standard number is the wrong shape — escalate, never quote.

TALK TRACK

"For 2–4 locations it's per-location at the same rate — [annual plan price] each on annual, [monthly plan price] each on monthly. We don't charge per chair, per stylist, or per call. For 5+ locations or franchise networks, we structure it differently — let me get our enterprise lead on a 30-minute call this week and we'll tailor it to your network."

FOR THE AE

Even if you've heard a multi-loc number from another rep, don't quote it — it changes by network size, contract length, and which add-ons are bundled in. Escalate to your AE manager / enterprise lead.
