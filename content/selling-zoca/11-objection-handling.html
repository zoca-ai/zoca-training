TL;DR

An objection is almost never the thing the prospect just said. It's the surface form of one of three real concerns: they don't see the value yet, they don't have the authority/alignment to say yes, or they've been burned before. Your job is not to defend Zoca — it's to figure out which one is underneath and address that one directly. You don't accept the objection's premise, and you don't bulldoze it either. You concede the real point first, then redirect (Cole's rule). This lesson consolidates the top-five plays AEs run every week — price, incumbents, "Groupon leads," AI mistakes, and "let me think about it" — with the full field objection bank by product. Memorize the verbatim lines; the numbers flex, the structure doesn't.

1. What an objection actually is

Before you reach for a script, diagnose. Every objection maps to one of three underlying concerns, and each is handled differently:

The surface lineWhat's usually underneathThe category
"It's too expensive""I don't yet see what I'd get for the money."Don't believe the value
"Let me think about it""My partner/franchisor isn't on board" or "I don't decide alone."No authority / alignment
"These are just Groupon leads""I tried a deal platform, got bad customers, and I'm pattern-matching you to that."Been burned before
RULE OF THUMB

Cole's rule — concede the real point first, then redirect. Don't defend. Don't bulldoze. Find which of the three concerns is live, agree with the legitimate part of it, and reframe from there.

DEMO TIP

When you hit a reframe, make it visible. Pull up the preview or the deck mid-objection and let the prospect see the answer — the deal-platform comparison table, the day-state toggle, the audit. Heard beats unheard; seen beats heard.

2. Play 1 — "It's too expensive"

What's underneath: they haven't translated the monthly cost into their own math yet. They're comparing it to a single tool ("Vagaro is $99") instead of the bundle of leaks it closes.

The reframe: move the conversation from price-of-Zoca to cost-of-the-leak.

TALK TRACK

The leak-math reframe (verbatim structure — plug in their real numbers) "[annual plan price] sounds like the price. Let me show you the cost. You said you're missing about 18 calls a week — that's roughly 72 a month, conservatively 30% would have booked, average ticket $85. That's $1,836 a month walking past you on the phone alone. We charge [annual plan price] to fix it. And there are three other leaks I haven't even priced — visibility, drop-offs, and rebooks. Even if we only fixed the phone, the math runs in your favor by the end of month one."

If they fight the math ("you're assuming 30% would have booked"), concede the assumption and make a smaller one:

TALK TRACK

The math fallback "Fair — let's halve it. 15% conversion, $85 ticket, 72 missed calls. That's $918 a month. We're [annual plan price]. The math still runs."

FOR THE AE

If they still won't engage with the math, this was never a price objection — it's "let me think" wearing a price mask. Skip to Play 5. And don't hide the number: say "[annual plan price] on annual, [monthly plan price] monthly — that's it," then stop talking. We tested this — prospects respect the rep who says it cleanly.

COMPLIANCE NOTE

Pricing is pending final confirmation. Always use the [annual plan price] / [monthly plan price] placeholders. Reject any stale number you've heard — $179, $249, $269, $279, $290 are all dead; never quote them. The setup fee is real and is waived only on the yearly plan (baked into annual pricing) — frame the waiver as a concrete reason to go annual; never say "no setup fee" and never state the fee amount.

3. Play 2 — "We already have an agency / Vagaro / a point tool"

What's underneath: they don't want to fire someone. There's a relationship, a contract, or a sunk cost. They're defending the change cost, not the tool. Never badmouth the incumbent — the prospect picked them. Show what Zoca does that the incumbent can't.

IncumbentThe reframe (verbatim)
Agency"Agencies are great at strategy decks. We don't compete with strategy — we compete with execution at scale. Your agency is one human running 12 accounts. Zoca produces blog posts, GBP posts, and service pages continuously for one customer. That's not anti-agency — that's just the output math."
Vagaro / Mindbody / Square (booking)"Vagaro's a great booking tool. We don't replace your booking — we sit in front of it. Vagaro is what happens after the customer finds you. We're how they find you in the first place, and how they get answered when they call. We integrate, we don't replace."
Voice-AI receptionist"Two questions. One — does it know the customer's full visit history when they call back? Two — when it books, does it write to your scheduler, your reviews flow, and your rebook reminders? Most voice tools answer no on both — they sit in their own bubble. Ours is one brain across phone, web, and chat, and it talks to your booking platform. Your customer never repeats themselves, and you don't run two systems."

The unifying message across all four: one brain beats a bolt-on. Discovery Agent + Front Desk Agent ship together as Discovery Neo — the bundled core every new customer gets. Conversion, Booking, Loyalty, and Ads are add-ons / coming-soon — sell them as upside, never with launch dates.

4. Play 3 — "These are just Groupon leads"

This is the objection prospects feel most strongly, because they've been burned. Run the branded-vs-non-branded reframe verbatim — escalate through the lines as they push.

TALK TRACK

Branded vs. non-branded "I hear that concern a lot — and it makes total sense if you've been burned by Groupon. Here's the key difference. A Groupon customer found you by searching your business name after seeing a deal. A Zoca lead found you by searching 'facial near me.' That customer had no platform in mind — they were looking for the best local option, and you showed up. That's a completely different buyer."

TALK TRACK

Source vs. experience (if they say "they feel the same to me") "The experience at the appointment might feel similar — but the source tells you everything about future behavior. Ask your last Groupon client if they'd rebook at full price. Then ask the Zoca lead. The Zoca lead never expected a discount — they booked because you showed up as the best nearby option on Google."

TALK TRACK

The proof move (when they still won't buy it) "Last thing — Zoca is not listed on Groupon. We're not a marketplace. If a customer found you through Zoca, they found you on Google. That is, by definition, a non-Groupon customer."

DEMO TIP

Share your screen and show the platform-comparison table: the Zoca row — Search type: Non-Branded · Customer owns relationship: ✓ You do · Discount required: ✗ Never. They need to see it, not just hear it.

TALK TRACK

Honest concession (some markets really are deal-heavy) "You might be in a market where deal-seekers dominate — that's real. What we do still works there, because we move you up the non-branded rankings and get you the active buyers your competitors are missing. But I won't pretend discount-seekers don't exist. The question is: do you want both audiences, or just the deal one?"

FOR THE AE

5. Play 4 — "What if the AI makes a mistake?"

What's underneath: two blended fears — AI-hallucination horror stories, and looking bad to their own customers if a booking gets botched. Don't promise perfection. Concede, then contain.

TALK TRACK

The honest answer "It will make mistakes. Every AI does — so does the human you have right now. The question isn't whether, it's how you contain them. Three things. One — every call is recorded and transcribed in your dashboard, so you see exactly what happened. Two — the agent is trained on your services, pricing, and policies, and you can correct it from the dashboard in 30 seconds. Three — you can route any call to a human as a fallback if the agent doesn't recognize the request. We don't pretend it's perfect. We make it auditable."

TALK TRACK

The Test Call confidence move (run it live if you haven't yet) "Want to hear it? Pick up your phone — I'll text a number. Try to break it. Ask for something weird, or a service it doesn't offer. The best way to trust this is to test it for 90 seconds, not for me to tell you it's good."

Even if it stumbles, they trust it more afterward — because you didn't lie about it being perfect.

DEMO TIP

If the Front Desk Agent fumbles on a training instance: "This is a training model — it doesn't have a real booking system connected. On a live instance it pulls availability straight from your booking platform. The slip is fixable; what matters is the voice and how it handled the question."

TALK TRACK

Human-fallback recovery (when they insist on a human) "The agent picks up first. If it doesn't know the answer, it routes to your human — so nothing is lost. Customers don't sit on hold, and you don't lose a booking because the front desk was busy. The agent is a buffer, not a replacement. You still have your team."

6. Play 5 — "Let me think about it"

The most underrated objection. New AEs hear a soft no. It isn't — it's the surface form of three different things, each handled differently. Triage first.

TALK TRACK

The triage "Cool — what specifically do you want to think on? If it's pricing, we should talk now. If it's whether the phone thing actually works, let's redo the Test Call with a real number. If it's about your partner — let's get them on the next call. Which one is it?"

Whatever they name is what you address:

  • A — Internal alignment (spouse / partner / franchisor): "Let's not lose momentum. Two options — I jump on a 30-minute call with both of you Tuesday or Thursday so I answer their questions live, or I send a 4-line written recap with pricing tonight and you walk them through it. Which works better for them?" Get a calendar invite or a recap with a deadline. Never end on "I'll think about it."
  • B — Stalling (liked it, not in enough pain today): "Honest question — if I gave you 90 days free, would you start tomorrow? If yes, the issue is pricing and we should talk. If no — what's the real reason?" The free-90-days line is hypothetical; you're diagnosing, not offering. A "no, even free" means not this quarter — move them to a 60-day follow-up and free your calendar.
  • C — Not the sharpest fit, and they didn't say so: handle with the soft best-fit framing below.
OBJECTION

"…actually, it's probably overkill for a shop my size." Never say "you don't qualify" or "you're too small." Read fit honestly and let them decide: "Tell me straight — is it owner-as-everything right now, all the marketing, booking, and calls on you? Because Zoca pays off fastest once a shop has hit the ceiling on that. If you're not there yet, it still helps — the wins are just smaller and slower, and the day you add a chair or a second location, the value steps up fast. I'd rather you go in knowing exactly what it'll move for you now." (Zoca Value 04 — be honest, full stop.)

FOR THE AE

The only hard rule in the whole curriculum is a laptop or iPad in the shop — onboarding can't run without it. Everything else flexes. Beauty & wellness is our sharpest fit, not a wall: a non-beauty owner who wants in isn't refused — you're honest the product is tuned for beauty and some category intelligence won't apply, then let them choose.

The five plays above are the weekly heavyweights. Below is the broader bank of real client objections, grouped by the product each touches. Discovery Agent — content & SEO

OBJECTION

"I can write my own content from AI — why do I need you?" You're buying productized, at-scale, best-practice execution personalized by the Brain — content that sounds like you, published across website + Google profile + blogs continuously. "You can write your own blog… or buy the product that does so many jobs it would've cost thousands a month."

OBJECTION

"How are the keywords selected?" By demand and opportunity per service (bucketed and ranked), not a flat cap. "We qualify your services by the higher-demand opportunity."

OBJECTION

"Why should I take your landing page?" (author — no scripted line) It's an SEO engine, not just a page: a page per service, FAQ/Q&A content, schema + sitemap + an AI-readable summary file, kept current from the Brain, with Call + Book buttons — the substrate that ranks on Google and gets cited by AI engines.

OBJECTION

"How does AI SEO work?" Structured, FAQ-rich, business-specific content (service pages + Q&A blogs) that AI engines crawl and cite. The audit measures mentions and citations across ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI Overview, and Perplexity.

OBJECTION

"How do you rank on AI SEO?" (this is a trick — there is no ranking) There is no "ranking" in AI search. GEO/AEO is about being recommended and cited, not placed at a position. We optimize for mentions, citations, and trust signals — not a rank number. Knowing this distinction signals you actually understand the space.

OBJECTION

"Will AI-generated content get me penalized by Google?" (author — no scripted line) The content is original, structured (Q&A/FAQ), service-specific, and grounded in the business's real facts via the Brain — not spun duplicate filler. That grounding is exactly what makes it rank, not what gets it penalized.

OBJECTION

"How fast do I get recommended on GEO/AEO?" No deterministic timeline. AEO indexing runs roughly 7–30 (up to ~60) days for Google; results often show about a month out. The lever is doing the best practices consistently — there's no instant switch.

Front Desk Agent — voice

OBJECTION

"I don't want AI to answer my calls." (author — reframe) Reframe to "never miss a call." It catches the calls you'd otherwise lose — after-hours, when you're with a client, simultaneous ringers — and transfers to you during your hours. You stay in control.

OBJECTION

"My current clients won't like AI." (author — no scripted line) Returning clients are recognized by phone; the agent discloses it's AI if asked, books fast, and texts a confirmation. Most of the calls it catches would've gone to voicemail anyway.

OBJECTION

"What about my receptionist — why do I need you?" It's coverage, not replacement — 24/7, simultaneous calls, after-hours, spam filtering, plus outbound recovery. A human receptionist at ~$25/hr is a few thousand a month; the Front Desk Agent isn't.

OBJECTION

"Why is your AI front desk better than other AI calling tools?" (author — no scripted line) It actually books into your existing scheduler end-to-end on the call — most competitors just send a link or only support a single calendar tool — plus lead-to-booking recovery, flat pricing, and genuine salon depth.

9. Common rep mistakes

  • Defending instead of diagnosing. You argue the objection's premise. Instead, find which of the three concerns is live and address that.
  • Bulldozing. You talk over the legitimate part. Concede it first (Cole's rule), then redirect.
  • Hiding the price. Nerves make you say "there are a few options, depending on volume…" No. Say the number, then go quiet — whoever talks first loses leverage.
  • Hearing "let me think" as a no. It's a triage prompt. Force the real concern to the surface.
  • Overselling a lower-fit shop. Be honest about smaller/slower wins now and the lift later. Honesty at the top of the funnel is the long game — Value 04.

10. Key terms

  • Cole's rule — concede the real point first, then redirect. The spine of every play.
  • The three concerns — don't-believe-the-value, no-authority/alignment, been-burned-before. Diagnose which before reaching for a script.
  • Leak math — translating the monthly price into the dollar cost of the prospect's missed calls / drop-offs, using their own numbers.
  • Branded vs. non-branded — the Groupon reframe: a deal customer searched your name; a Zoca lead searched "[service] near me." Different buyer.
  • The Test Call — a live 90-second test of the Front Desk Agent; the highest-trust moment in the cycle, run especially against "what if the AI makes a mistake?"
  • Triage line — the question that forces "let me think about it" to name its real concern (pricing / does-it-work / partner).
  • Soft best-fit framing — handle "too small / overkill" as product fit-and-stage ("here's what it'll move for you now"), never as a verdict on the customer. Laptop/iPad is the only hard rule.
  • Scripted vs. author — "scripted" lines came verbatim from field transcripts; "author / adjacent framing" lines are best-starting-points worth refining with field experience.
  • The trick objection — "how do you rank on AI" has no answer because AI search recommends, it doesn't rank; recognizing that is the point.
