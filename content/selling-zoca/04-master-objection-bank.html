1. Discovery — content & SEO

OBJECTION

#1 — "I can write my own content from AI, why do I need you?" You're buying productized, at-scale, best-practice execution personalized by the Brain — content that sounds like you, published across website + Google profile + blogs continuously. "You can create your own blog… but buy the product that does so many jobs for you that would've cost thousands a month."

OBJECTION

#2 — "How are these keywords selected?" By demand and opportunity per service (bucketed and ranked), not a flat keyword cap. "We qualify your services by the higher-demand opportunity."

OBJECTION

#3 — "Why should I take your landing page?" (author — no scripted line) It's an SEO engine, not just a page: a page per service, FAQ/Q&A content, schema + sitemap + an AI-readable summary file, kept current from the Brain, with Call + Book buttons — the substrate that ranks on Google and gets cited by AI engines.

OBJECTION

#4 — "How does AI SEO work?" Structured, FAQ-rich, business-specific content (service pages + Q&A blogs) that AI engines crawl and cite. The audit measures mentions and citations across ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI Overview, and Perplexity.

OBJECTION

#5 — "How do you rank on AI SEO?" (this is a trick — there is no ranking) There is no "ranking" in AI search. GEO/AEO is about being recommended and cited, not placed at a position. We optimize for mentions, citations, and trust signals — not a rank number. (Knowing this distinction signals you actually understand the space.)

OBJECTION

#7 — "Will AI-generated content get me penalized by Google?" (author — no scripted line) The content is original, structured (Q&A/FAQ), service-specific, and grounded in the business's real facts via the Brain — not spun, duplicate filler. That grounding is exactly what makes it rank, not what gets it penalized.

OBJECTION

#8 / #9 — "How fast do I get recommended on GEO/AEO / by AI?" No deterministic timeline. AEO indexing runs roughly 7–30 (up to ~60) days for Google; results often show about a month out. The lever is doing the best practices consistently — there's no instant switch.

2. Front Desk — voice

OBJECTION

#10 — "I don't want AI to answer my calls." (author — reframe) Reframe to "never miss a call." It catches the calls you'd otherwise lose — after-hours, when you're with a client, simultaneous calls — and transfers to you during your hours. You stay in control.

OBJECTION

#11 — "My current clients don't like AI." (author — no scripted line) Returning clients are recognized by phone; the agent discloses it's AI if asked, books fast, and texts a confirmation. Most of the calls it catches would otherwise have gone to voicemail anyway.

OBJECTION

#12 — "What about my receptionist? Why do I need you?" It's coverage, not replacement — 24/7, simultaneous calls, after-hours, spam filtering, plus outbound recovery. A human receptionist at ~$25/hr is a few thousand a month versus ~$99/mo.

OBJECTION

#13 — "Why is your AI front desk better than other AI calling tools?" (author — no scripted line) It actually books into your existing scheduler end-to-end on the call — most competitors just send a booking link or only support Calendly/Google Calendar — plus lead-to-booking recovery, flat pricing, and genuine salon depth.

3. Command Center

OBJECTION

#14 — "I still have to do a lot of work, like…?" This is the cue to nudge toward Command Center — it does the work and answers the questions so the owner doesn't have to dig through the dashboard. "Just ask it."

4. Brain

OBJECTION

#6 — "Why do I need to share information with the Brain?" So the content and the agents have real context — which produces differentiated, non-generic output that makes the business stand out instead of blending in.

OBJECTION

#15 — "The content doesn't sound like me. It's generic." The cure for generic content is feeding the Brain. "If they give their relevant context to the AI agent, the content generated will be very different." Turn the complaint into a Brain-building conversation.
