TL;DR

ICP is our best-fit picture — the kind of beauty or wellness business where Zoca creates the most value, fastest. It is a targeting and expectation-setting tool, not a gate that turns paying customers away. Know who you're talking to (three buyer personas), read the fit out loud on the call, and when a prospect isn't our sharpest fit, sell honestly — set the right expectations and point them at the version of success that's real for them. The one true prerequisite is operational: a laptop or iPad in the shop, because the onboarding demo can't run without it.

1. Who you're talking to

A beauty or wellness owner is running an extraordinarily complex operation — often nearly alone. They handle the service, the marketing, the calendar, accounting, reviews, and the website, in a craft that is hyper-specialized (a laser tech is not a facialist; "hair removal" alone splits into electrolysis, laser, waxing, and sugaring). Before any pitch, earn the right to it by showing you understand their world.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"Before we get into what Zoca does, let's talk about why it matters for a shop like yours." The product lands far harder once the owner feels understood.

Every cold conversation is with one of three people. Diagnose which fast — the wrong opening loses them.

Type C is the hardest convert because they feel fulfilled — they're paying for lots of tools. Ask "what are you using, and where's the spend?" Their total almost always exceeds Zoca. Then sell simplicity, cohesiveness, and context — one brain instead of five vendors — not features.

2. What "ICP" means here — and what it doesn't

ICP (Ideal Customer Profile) is the shape of business where the four leaks are biggest and Zoca closes them fastest, so the customer sees value early and stays for years. That's it. It tells you where to spend your energy first and how to set expectations — it is not a list of people we refuse.

RULE OF THUMB

Best-fit → lead with confidence and move fast. Lower-fit → still a real conversation; just be honest about what success looks like at their stage and how soon it shows up. We don't say "you don't qualify." We say "here's what Zoca will and won't move for a shop like yours right now."

COMMON MISCONCEPTION

"Not ICP" used to read as "disqualify / walk away." That's retired. A lower-fit prospect who wants to buy can buy — your job is to make sure they go in with eyes open, not to turn them away.

3. The best-fit picture

Read fit in two layers. None of these (except the laptop) is a hard stop — they're signals that tell you how strong the fit is and what to set expectations around.

Layer 1 — operational readiness

  • A laptop or iPad in the shop. Zoca is desktop-first and the onboarding demo runs on a real screen. ⛔ This one is a genuine hard requirement — without a device in the shop, we can't onboard. (See the callout below.)
  • Healthy demand. Best-fit shops are projected to generate at least ~60 new leads over the next six months. Thinner demand isn't a no — it means slower, smaller wins, and you say so.
  • Booking platform. We integrate cleanest today with Gloss Genius, Square, Mindbody, Fresha, and Vagaro. A shop on a custom site, paper, or no link isn't turned away — it's a migration conversation, and you flag the extra setup honestly.
RULE OF THUMB

⛔ Hard rule — the only one. A laptop or iPad must be in the shop. The onboarding demo and daily use depend on it. Everything else on this page flexes; this does not. If there's no device, the move is "let's get a laptop or iPad at the front desk first — it's usually a quick fix — then we onboard."

Layer 2 — lead shape (how big the leak is)

Lead shapeFitWhat to know
Multi-location (2+)Best-fitAny category, no revenue floor. The moment there are two locations the leak is structural — the owner can't be in both places.
Single location + staffStrong fitAny category is a green light. A few low-ticket single-location categories (threading-only, nails-only, eyelash-only, braiders) are lower-fit — sell with honest expectations.
Single location + solo (owner only)Workable fitStrongest when revenue is $100K+ and the category isn't one of the four low-ticket ones. Below that, it's still sellable — just set modest, honest expectations.
KEY TERM

The four low-ticket categories — threading-only, nails-only, eyelash-only, braiders. At a single location the cost-to-serve / LTV math is thinner, so they're lower-fit, not excluded. The fit lifts the day they add a second location or a second service line. Tattoo, piercing, and permanent makeup are in-vertical, best-fit beauty categories.

4. Lower-fit signals (set expectations, don't slam the door)

These used to be "disqualifiers." Reframe them as honesty flags — things that make the value slower or smaller, which you name out loud so the customer isn't surprised.

  • No GBP, or a brand-new one (< ~3 months). The Discovery Agent ranks from an established Google Business Profile. New GBP → "let's let it season a few weeks; here's what we'll do in the meantime."
  • Fewer than ~20 reviews, or under 4 stars. Reviews are the conversion lever — thin review signal means ranking gains convert more slowly. Name it; pair Zoca's review engine with the ranking story.
  • Part-time cadence (owner in 1–2 days/week). The platform rewards a real operating rhythm (daily approvals, responding to inquiries). Lighter cadence → lighter results; say so.
  • Mobile / no fixed location. The Discovery Agent is geo-anchored, so a fixed address makes ranking and routing work. Without one, set expectations on what still helps (Front Desk, reviews).
  • Thin trade-area demand. If local search volume for the services is low, ranking produces fewer bookings. Be straight about the ceiling.
  • Winding down. If they're closing, no platform investment pays back — the honest move is to say "this isn't the right time to spend."
  • Outside beauty & wellness (e.g. dentists, gyms, restaurants), or product/MLM/pure-online businesses. Beauty & wellness is where Zoca is sharpest — it knows balayage, hydrafacial, box braids, lash fills, and the rebook windows for each, so in-vertical shops get the most tailored results. That's a fit signal, not a wall: if a non-beauty owner wants in, you don't refuse them — you're honest that the product is tuned for beauty and some of that category intelligence won't apply to their space, then let them decide. Set the expectation; don't slam the door.

5. Reading fit on the call

Run it as natural scoping, not an interrogation. Order matters — lead with operational and shape questions; let revenue surface on its own.

  1. Device (the one hard check). "Quick scope question — is there a laptop or iPad at the front desk the team uses day to day?"
  2. Booking platform. "What are you on for booking — Gloss Genius, Square, Mindbody, Fresha, Vagaro, or something else?"
  3. Demand. "Roughly how many new client inquiries are you getting in a typical month?" (gauges the ~60-leads/6-months picture).
  4. Lead shape. "How many locations are you running, and is it just you or do you have folks working with you?"
  5. Category (if single-location). "What service do you mainly do?"
  6. Revenue (only if single-location + solo, and only after the above). "Under $100K, low six figures, more?"
  7. Fit sweep. "Couple of operational things — how long has your Google profile been live, and roughly how many reviews are you at?"
COMMON MISCONCEPTION

Opening with the revenue question. Don't — owners get defensive when a stranger leads with "what's your revenue?" By the time you reach it, the location/staff answers have already told you the size, and the question feels like normal scoping.

6. Selling honestly when they're not your sharpest fit

A solo eyelash artist at $58K from a home studio is excited and ready to buy. We don't hard-no her — and we don't oversell her either. We tell the truth about what Zoca will move at her stage, and we let her decide.

FOR THE AE

Honesty at the top of the funnel builds the brand. The owner you leveled with — even when you flagged a lighter fit — refers the multi-location owner who's a perfect fit. Every time. Being straight is the long game, and it's value 04: be honest, full stop.

Lower-fit, said cleanly (in-vertical):

"You're in our world for sure. I want to be straight on fit: at your stage — [solo / lighter demand / brand-new GBP] — Zoca still helps, but the wins are smaller and slower than for a multi-chair shop. If that's worth it to you, I'll set you up right and we'll be honest about the timeline. And the day you [add a second chair / open a second location / your reviews build], the value steps up fast."

Booking-platform gap (a setup conversation, not a no):

"You're our exact kind of business. The one wrinkle is you're on [custom site / paper] — we integrate cleanest with Gloss Genius, Square, Mindbody, Fresha, or Vagaro, so there's a migration step. Totally doable; I just want you to know it's part of the setup."

Outside beauty & wellness (honest, not a refusal):

"Fair warning so you go in with eyes open — Zoca is built specifically for beauty and wellness, so a lot of the smarts (the service vocabulary, the rebook timing) are tuned for that world and won't map perfectly onto [dental / a restaurant / a gym]. The core — getting found, answering your calls — still works. If that's the part you want, I'm glad to set you up; I just want you to know what's optimized for you and what isn't."

COMMON MISCONCEPTION

Saying "you don't qualify" or "you're too small." Never. Frame everything as the product's fit and stage — "here's what it'll move for you right now" — not a verdict on the customer.

7. Edge cases — read the fit, set the expectation

  • 2-location threading studio on Square. Best-fit. Multi-location — any category, no floor. The low-ticket caveat doesn't apply once there are two locations.
  • Single-location nail salon, 4 staff, $400K. Lower-fit (nails-only at single location). Sell with honest expectations; fit lifts at a second location.
  • Solo hairstylist, $200K, on Vagaro, established GBP. Strong fit — hair isn't a low-ticket category and revenue clears $100K.
  • Solo eyelash artist, $200K, on Gloss Genius. Workable but lower-fit (eyelash-only at single location). Still sellable; name the smaller/slower wins and the lift at a second chair.
  • 6-location barbershop chain on Square. Best-fit. Multi-location, any category. (Barbershops are not a low-ticket caveat category.)
  • Multi-location chain on a custom in-house booking site. Best-fit on shape — migration conversation. "You're exactly our customer; we just need to get you onto one of our five platforms."
  • Med-spa, 1 nurse practitioner, $90K, established GBP. Strong fit — single-location + staff, med-spa isn't a low-ticket category.
  • Solo med-spa, $180K, on Mindbody, GBP only 6 weeks old. Strong fit, with a timing flag — let the GBP season a few weeks, then go.
  • Tattoo / piercing / permanent-makeup studio. In-vertical, best-fit beauty categories — sell them like any other beauty shop.
  • Dentist, gym, or restaurant with budget. Outside our sharpest fit — beauty-specific intelligence won't all apply. Don't refuse them: be honest that the product is tuned for beauty, name what still works (getting found, answering calls), and let them choose.

8. Why beauty & wellness is our sharpest fit

Two reasons the vertical is where Zoca wins biggest — so it's where you lead, even though it isn't a wall:

  • The product speaks the language. Balayage, hydrafacial, box braids, skin fade, microneedling, lash fill — the Discovery Agent knows these and their rebook windows. That depth is exactly what a beauty shop gets and a dental office wouldn't fully use.
  • The vertical is the moat. A horizontal platform serving 50 industries can't beat a vertical one serving a single craft deeply. Focus is the edge — which is why beauty & wellness is the story you lead with and where you spend your pipeline energy.
OBJECTION

"You'll really take a non-beauty business?" — "If you want in, yes — I'm just going to be straight with you. The product is tuned for beauty and wellness, so the service-specific smarts are built for that world. The fundamentals — getting you found and answering your calls — still work for you. I'd rather you know exactly what's optimized for your space and what isn't, and then it's your call."

9. Key terms

  • ICP (best-fit) — the business shape where Zoca's value lands fastest. A targeting/expectation tool, not a gate.
  • The one hard rule — a laptop or iPad in the shop; onboarding can't run without it.
  • Lead shapes — multi-location (best-fit), single-location + staff (strong), single-location + solo (workable; $100K+ is the strong line).
  • Low-ticket categories — threading-only, nails-only, eyelash-only, braiders: lower-fit at single location, not excluded; lift at a second location/service line.
  • Lower-fit signals — GBP missing/new, thin reviews, part-time cadence, mobile, thin demand, winding down: honesty flags, not refusals.
  • In-vertical — beauty & wellness, including tattoo, piercing, and permanent makeup.
