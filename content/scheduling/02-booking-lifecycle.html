TL;DR

A booking is the heart of the product — the appointment a client has reserved. Zoca lets bookings be created three ways (the client books themselves online, the front desk books them in-app, or Zoca's AI books them outbound), and then handles the whole life of that booking — editing, rescheduling, cancelling, and refunding — with the policy and payment handled automatically. Pitch it as: "However the appointment comes in, it lands in one place and follows your rules."

1. What it is

A booking is a confirmed appointment: a client, one or more services, a stylist, and a time. Around it the system tracks payment (deposit and balance), notifications, and status as the appointment moves from "just requested" to "paid and done."

The lifecycle is: a booking is created, it can be edited or rescheduled if plans change, and it ends as completed, cancelled, or a no-show.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"It's the appointment — but a smart one. It knows what was booked, what's been paid, who's coming, and what to do if they cancel."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

  • Bookings come from everywhere. A client books online at midnight, a regular calls the front desk, and Zoca's AI fills a cancellation by reaching out to leads. Without one system, those live in three places. Here they all become the same kind of booking on the same calendar.
  • The rules apply themselves. Deposits, cancellation windows, and refunds are enforced automatically, so the front desk isn't negotiating policy or doing math by hand.
  • Changes are painless. Rescheduling is as simple as editing the appointment and changing the start time; cancelling triggers the right refund (if the deposit is refundable) and frees the slot for someone else. These actions can also be done via the Command Center with just some instructions.
VALUE LINE

"You set your policy once. Every booking — online, phone, or AI — follows it automatically. That's fewer arguments at the desk and less revenue slipping through the cracks."

3. How it works

Creating a booking — three paths

A. Client books online (self-service). The client enters their name and number, selects a service, picks an available slot, enters a card or pays the deposit, and confirms. The appointment is created in a pending payment state; the deposit is charged; the appointment becomes confirmed. Automated reminders go out before the appointment.

B. Front desk books in-app. From the Calendar, staff click a slot, pick the client, choose the service(s) and stylist, set the time, and configure a deposit if the policy requires one. Same result — a confirmed booking on the calendar.

C. Zoca's AI books outbound. Zoca's AI can work a list of leads (a marketing list, past clients) — reach out by call or text, offer an open slot, send a secure payment link, take the deposit, and book the appointment. The lead is then marked converted. This is a differentiator: the system doesn't just hold appointments, it can go fill them.

GOOD TO KNOW

Online checkout uses a short-lived cart (about 30 minutes) and a ~10-minute hold on the chosen slot, so a client has time to finish paying without the slot being grabbed by someone else — and an abandoned cart frees the slot back up.

What's captured on a booking

Client (name, email, phone), the service(s) and stylist, the start/end time, any notes, the deposit and the full price (plus any retail products added at checkout, once that launches — see Checkout / POS).

Booking statuses

A booking moves through states the team can see and act on: it starts as pending payment (created, deposit not yet charged) and becomes confirmed once the deposit is taken, then completed after the visit, with branches for cancelled, rescheduled, and no-show (which itself splits into fee collected vs fee waived — see the No-shows doc). Some flows can also start as pending approval when the owner wants to approve incoming requests before they're locked in. (When there's no deposit — a "no payment required" booking — it's confirmed straight away.)

Editing

From a booking's detail panel the front desk can update the appointment — change the client, the service(s), the deposit configuration, and the time slot. Edits stay on the same booking. This can also be done via the Command Center with just some instructions.

Rescheduling

To reschedule, open the appointment and edit the start time (or change the stylist). The system keeps a record of the reschedule, frees the original slot, and resets the client's confirmation (so they reconfirm the new time). Rescheduling can also be done via the Command Center with just some instructions.

Cancelling

A booking can be cancelled by the client (from their confirmation/portal) or by the front desk. Cancelling can also be done via the Command Center with just some instructions. When it's cancelled:

  • the system evaluates the cancellation policy — if the cancellation falls within the policy fee range, the business still has the option to charge or not charge the client on a case-by-case basis,
  • if a refundable deposit was collected, the refund is processed automatically (back to the original card); if the deposit is non-refundable (configurable by the business), it is not refunded,
  • the slot is released back onto the calendar, and
  • the client and stylist are notified.
POLICY IS CONFIGURABLE

A common policy shape looks like: cancel more than 24h out → no fee; inside 24h → cancellation fee applies. Those exact windows and fees are set by the salon, not fixed by Zoca — so present them as "you decide your policy." Even when the policy says a fee applies, the business can choose on a case-by-case basis whether to actually charge or waive it. A refund is only possible when the client has paid a deposit that is set as refundable. When the salon cancels (e.g., a stylist is out sick), the client typically gets a full refund regardless of timing.

4. Where it lives + demo path

  1. Create (front desk): Calendar → click a slot → New Appointment → client, service, stylist, time → save.
  2. Create (online): open the salon's public booking page in another tab → name and number → service → slot → card/deposit → confirm, and show it appear on the calendar.
  3. Edit: click the appointment → open details → change a service or time.
  4. Reschedule: open the appointment → edit the start time. You can also do this via the Command Center.
  5. Cancel: open the appointment → Cancel → show the policy/refund message.
  6. Approvals: point to the Pending Approvals list in the calendar sidebar for the "approve before it's locked" flow.

6. FAQ & objection handling

"Can clients book themselves, or does my staff have to enter everything?" Both. Clients self-book online; staff book in-app for calls and walk-ins. Everything lands on the same calendar.

"What happens to the money when someone cancels?" The system applies your cancellation policy. If the deposit is refundable, the refund goes back to the original card automatically. If the deposit is non-refundable (configurable by the business), it is not refunded. Even when a fee applies, the business can choose on a case-by-case basis whether to charge or waive it.

"If I have to cancel on a client, do they get charged?" No — salon-initiated cancellations refund the client in full if a refundable deposit was collected.

"Do I have to accept every online booking, or can I approve them first?" You can run an approval step — incoming requests sit in a Pending Approvals list for you to approve or decline.

"Is rescheduling a hassle?" Just open the appointment and change the start time. The old slot frees up and the client is asked to reconfirm the new time. You can also reschedule via the Command Center.

"What if a client starts booking online but doesn't finish?" The cart and slot hold expire on their own (about 30 minutes / ~10 minutes), so abandoned bookings free the slot back up automatically.

"Can the AI really book for me?" Yes — it can reach out to leads, offer a slot, take the deposit via a payment link, and book the appointment. (Scope of outbound campaigns is set up with your team.)

7. Key terms

  • Booking / appointment — a reserved service for a client at a time with a stylist.
  • Pending payment — created but the deposit hasn't been charged yet.
  • Confirmed — deposit taken, appointment locked in.
  • Pending approval — waiting for the owner to approve before it's confirmed.
  • Rescheduled — moved to a new time; original slot released, reschedule recorded.
  • Cart — the short-lived online checkout session (~30 min).
  • Hold — a ~10-minute reservation on a slot during checkout.
  • Lead → converted — an outbound prospect that the AI (or staff) turned into a booking.
