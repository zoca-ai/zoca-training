TL;DR

A booking is the heart of the product — the appointment a client has reserved. Zoca lets bookings be created three ways (the client books themselves online, the front desk books them in-app, or Zoca's AI books them outbound), and then handles the whole life of that booking — editing, rescheduling, cancelling, and refunding — with the policy and payment handled automatically. Pitch it as: "However the appointment comes in, it lands in one place and follows your rules."

1. What it is

A booking is a confirmed appointment: a client, one or more services, a stylist, and a time. Around it the system tracks payment (deposit and balance), notifications, and status as the appointment moves from "just requested" to "paid and done."

The lifecycle is: a booking is created, it can be edited or rescheduled if plans change, and it ends as completed, cancelled, or a no-show.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"It's the appointment — but a smart one. It knows what was booked, what's been paid, who's coming, and what to do if they cancel."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

  • Bookings come from everywhere. A client books online at midnight, a regular calls the front desk, and Zoca's AI fills a cancellation by texting the waitlist. Without one system, those live in three places. Here they all become the same kind of booking on the same calendar.
  • The rules apply themselves. Deposits, cancellation windows, and refunds are enforced automatically, so the front desk isn't negotiating policy or doing math by hand.
  • Changes are painless. Rescheduling is a drag on the calendar; cancelling triggers the right refund and frees the slot for someone else.
VALUE LINE

"You set your policy once. Every booking — online, phone, or AI — follows it automatically. That's fewer arguments at the desk and less revenue slipping through the cracks."

3. How it works

Creating a booking — three paths

A. Client books online (self-service). The client browses services and open times, enters their contact details, enters a card, and confirms. The appointment is created in a pending payment state; the deposit is charged; the appointment becomes confirmed. Automated reminders go out before the appointment, and the client can check themselves in by QR code on the day.

B. Front desk books in-app. From the Calendar, staff click a slot, pick the client, choose the service(s) and stylist, set the time, and configure a deposit if the policy requires one. Same result — a confirmed booking on the calendar.

C. Zoca's AI books outbound. Zoca's AI can work a list of leads (waitlist, a marketing list) — reach out by call or text, offer an open slot, send a secure payment link, take the deposit, and book the appointment. The lead is then marked converted. This is a differentiator: the system doesn't just hold appointments, it can go fill them.

GOOD TO KNOW

Online checkout uses a short-lived cart (about 30 minutes) and a ~10-minute hold on the chosen slot, so a client has time to finish paying without the slot being grabbed by someone else — and an abandoned cart frees the slot back up.

What's captured on a booking

Client (name, email, phone), the service(s) and stylist, the start/end time, any notes, the deposit and the full price (plus any retail products added at checkout, once that launches — see Checkout / POS).

Booking statuses

A booking moves through states the team can see and act on: it starts as pending payment (created, deposit not yet charged) and becomes confirmed once the deposit is taken, then checked-in → in progress → completed, with branches for cancelled, rescheduled, and no-show (which itself splits into fee collected vs fee waived — see the No-shows doc). Some flows can also start as pending approval when the owner wants to approve incoming requests before they're locked in. (When there's no deposit — a "no payment required" booking — it's confirmed straight away.)

Editing

From a booking's detail panel the front desk can update the appointment — change the client, the service(s), the deposit configuration, and the time slot. Edits stay on the same booking.

Rescheduling

The fastest way to reschedule is to drag the appointment to a new time or stylist on the calendar. The system keeps a record of the reschedule, frees the original slot, and resets the client's attendance confirmation (so they reconfirm the new time).

Cancelling

A booking can be cancelled by the client (from their confirmation/portal) or by the front desk. When it's cancelled:

  • the system evaluates the cancellation policy and calculates any refund,
  • the refund is processed automatically (back to the original card),
  • the slot is released back onto the calendar,
  • the client and stylist are notified, and
  • the waitlist is checked — if someone's waiting, they're told the slot opened.
POLICY IS CONFIGURABLE

A common policy shape looks like: cancel more than 24h out → full refund; inside 24h → partial; inside 2h → no refund. Those exact windows and percentages are set by the salon, not fixed by Zoca — so present them as "you decide your policy," and use the 24h/partial/none example only to illustrate. When the salon cancels (e.g., a stylist is out sick), the client typically gets a full refund regardless of timing, plus an optional account credit as a goodwill gesture.

4. Where it lives + demo path

  1. Create (front desk): Calendar → click a slot → New Appointment → client, service, stylist, time → save.
  2. Create (online): open the salon's public booking page in another tab → walk a slot selection → contact → card → confirm, and show it appear on the calendar.
  3. Edit: click the appointment → open details → change a service or time.
  4. Reschedule: drag the appointment to a new time.
  5. Cancel: open the appointment → Cancel → show the policy/refund message.
  6. Approvals: point to the Pending Approvals list in the calendar sidebar for the "approve before it's locked" flow.
DEMO TIP

Booking the same appointment two ways — once from the calendar, once from the public booking page — and showing both land in one calendar is a strong "single source of truth" moment.

6. FAQ & objection handling

"Can clients book themselves, or does my staff have to enter everything?" Both. Clients self-book online; staff book in-app for calls and walk-ins. Everything lands on the same calendar.

"What happens to the money when someone cancels?" The system applies your cancellation policy and refunds accordingly — automatically, to the original card. You set the windows and amounts.

"If I have to cancel on a client, do they get charged?" No — salon-initiated cancellations refund the client in full, and you can add a goodwill credit.

"Do I have to accept every online booking, or can I approve them first?" You can run an approval step — incoming requests sit in a Pending Approvals list for you to approve or decline.

"Is rescheduling a hassle?" It's a drag-and-drop on the calendar. The old slot frees up and the client is asked to reconfirm the new time.

"What if a client starts booking online but doesn't finish?" The cart and slot hold expire on their own (about 30 minutes / ~10 minutes), so abandoned bookings free the slot back up automatically.

"Can the AI really book for me?" Yes — it can reach out to leads, offer a slot, take the deposit via a payment link, and book the appointment. (Scope of outbound campaigns is set up with your team.)

7. Key terms

  • Booking / appointment — a reserved service for a client at a time with a stylist.
  • Pending payment — created but the deposit hasn't been charged yet.
  • Confirmed — deposit taken, appointment locked in.
  • Pending approval — waiting for the owner to approve before it's confirmed.
  • Rescheduled — moved to a new time; original slot released, reschedule recorded.
  • Cart — the short-lived online checkout session (~30 min).
  • Hold — a ~10-minute reservation on a slot during checkout.
  • Lead → converted — an outbound prospect that the AI (or staff) turned into a booking.
