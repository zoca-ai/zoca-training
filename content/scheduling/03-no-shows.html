TL;DR

A no-show is a client who simply doesn't turn up. For a salon, every no-show is an empty chair that can't be re-sold. Zoca detects no-shows automatically, lets the salon charge a no-show fee (or forfeit the deposit), waive it for a good reason, and immediately offer the open slot to the waitlist. Pitch it as: "Stop eating the cost of no-shows."

1. What it is

A no-show is when a client fails to arrive for a confirmed appointment and the appointment time passes without them checking in. The system detects this automatically after a short grace period (15 minutes by default, and the salon can change it).

A no-show is different from a cancellation:

  • A cancellation is when the client (or salon) explicitly calls off the appointment before the time. It runs the cancellation policy — typically with a charge window (e.g., "charge if cancelled within 24 hours").
  • A no-show is silence — the client neither shows nor cancels. It's detected after the grace period and runs the no-show policy.

The two no-show outcomes are tracked distinctly: no-show with fee collected and no-show, fee waived.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"If a client cancels, that's the cancellation policy. If they just ghost you, that's a no-show — and the system catches it and protects you."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

No-shows are one of the biggest silent revenue leaks in a salon. A two-hour color appointment that ghosts is two hours the chair earns nothing — and it's usually too late to fill.

Zoca attacks the problem from three sides:

  • Deterrence up front — deposits and attendance confirmations make clients think twice about ghosting.
  • Recovery after the fact — a no-show fee (or a forfeited deposit) means a ghosted slot still earns something.
  • Re-selling the slot — the moment a no-show is logged, the waitlist is pinged so someone else can grab the time.
VALUE LINE

"You can't make every client show up. But you can make sure a no-show doesn't cost you the whole appointment — and that someone else can take that slot."

3. How it works

Detection. When an appointment's time passes and the grace period (default 15 minutes) elapses without a check-in, the system flags it. Front desk can also mark a no-show manually from the booking's detail panel, and a no-show can be undone if it was a mistake.

The fee. If the salon has a no-show policy with a fee turned on, the system applies it. The fee is configurable and can be:

  • a percentage of the deposit (commonly 100% — i.e., the deposit is simply kept),
  • a fixed amount (e.g., a flat $50), or
  • a percentage of the service price.

When a deposit was already collected at booking, the most common setup is deposit forfeiture: the deposit the client already paid converts from refundable to the no-show fee — so there's nothing extra to charge.

POLICY IS CONFIGURABLE

The 15-minute grace period and the fee amounts above are examples and defaults — the salon sets these. Cancellation and no-show policies are configured separately and can even be set at different levels (a specific service, a stylist, the location, or the whole business), with the most specific rule winning. Present it as: "you decide the grace period and the fee, down to the service if you want."

Waiving. The owner stays in control. If a client had a real emergency, staff can waive the fee in one action; if money was already taken, it's refunded, and the client gets a "we understand, fee waived" message.

Disputes. If a client disputes a no-show, the salon can review and resolve it (waive, partially refund, or uphold the charge), and the whole thing is logged.

Attendance confirmation (deterrence). Salons can require clients to confirm they're coming before the appointment. The client taps to confirm; the stylist is notified. Note this is a signal, not a guarantee — it nudges clients and surfaces flakiness early, but the no-show detection still does the real enforcement. If a client reschedules, their confirmation resets so they reconfirm the new time.

Notifications around a no-show. The client gets a no-show notice (with the fee, if any), the stylist gets an alert, and the waitlist gets a "slot opened" text. If the fee is waived, the client is told.

6. FAQ & objection handling

"My clients won't like being charged for no-shows." You set the policy, and you can waive any fee for a genuine emergency in one tap. Most owners find a clear, fair no-show policy actually reduces no-shows because clients take the booking seriously.

"How does it know someone didn't show? My front desk is busy." It auto-detects after a grace period you set (15 minutes by default) — no babysitting required. Staff can also mark or undo a no-show manually.

"What's the difference between a cancellation and a no-show?" A cancellation is the client calling it off ahead of time (cancellation policy applies). A no-show is them not showing and not telling you (no-show policy applies). They're handled by separate rules you control.

"Can I charge different no-show fees for different services?" Yes — policies can be set per service, per stylist, per location, or for the whole business, and the most specific one wins.

"What if I just want a deposit kept, not a separate charge?" That's the most common setup: the deposit they already paid simply converts into the no-show fee.

"A client says it was a mistake / emergency." Waive the fee in one action (refunding if needed), and they get a friendly "fee waived" message. You can also resolve disputes and keep an audit trail.

7. Key terms

  • No-show — client didn't arrive and didn't cancel; detected after the grace period.
  • Grace period — minutes after the start time before a no-show is logged (15 min default, configurable).
  • No-show fee — the charge for a no-show: % of deposit, a fixed amount, or % of service.
  • Deposit forfeiture — the already-paid deposit becomes the no-show fee (nothing extra to charge).
  • Fee collected vs fee waived — the two no-show outcomes the system tracks.
  • Cancellation window — for cancellations, the hours-before-appointment threshold where a charge applies (separate from no-shows).
  • Attendance confirmation — an optional "confirm you're coming" step that nudges clients and flags flakiness early.
