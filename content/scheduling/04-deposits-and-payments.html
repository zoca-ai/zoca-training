TL;DR

Zoca collects a deposit when a client books (so they have skin in the game), takes the balance at the appointment, and supports cash, card, saved card-on-file, and in-person card reader / tap-to-pay — all tied to the booking. Refunds run automatically off the cancellation policy. Pitch it as: "Get a deposit up front, get the rest at checkout, and never chase a payment."

1. What it is

This is the money layer of scheduling. Two ideas to anchor:

  • Deposit — a partial payment taken at booking time to secure the appointment. The salon sets it as a flat amount or a percentage of the service price, and can turn it on or off per their policy.
  • Balance — the rest of what's owed, collected at the appointment via checkout.

Every booking automatically gets a linked sale (the financial record), so payments, the deposit, the balance, tips, and refunds all roll up in one place per appointment.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"A little money up front to hold the chair, the rest when they're done — and it's all attached to the appointment automatically."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

  • Deposits change behavior. A client who's put money down shows up. Deposits are the single most effective lever against no-shows.
  • Cash flow. Money starts arriving at booking, not days later.
  • No awkward chasing. The balance is right there at checkout — cash, tap, or a card already on file — so the front desk isn't hunting down payment.
  • Refunds without drama. When a client cancels, the refund follows the policy automatically; staff don't calculate or argue.
VALUE LINE

"Deposits are how you stop no-shows before they happen, and how you turn a calendar full of appointments into cash in the bank."

3. How it works

Deposits

When a booking is created, the system can collect a deposit immediately. The salon decides:

  • Whether to require one (it's a policy toggle),
  • How much — a flat dollar amount or a percentage of the service price.
EXAMPLE, NOT A RULE

One common deposit pattern scales the percentage to the ticket size — for instance, larger deposits on smaller services and a smaller percentage on big-ticket services. These tiers are illustrative; each salon configures its own. Use them only to paint the picture, e.g.: "you might take 50% on a quick service and 25–30% on a big color job — totally up to you."

Payment methods

MethodHow it's used
CashRecorded instantly as paid; no card processing.
Card (new card)Client's card is entered and charged through Stripe. Card payments confirm in a moment via Stripe.
Card on fileA saved card the client previously added — charge it without re-entering details (great for deposits, balances, and no-show fees).
In-person card readerA physical Stripe reader at the desk — see Checkout / POS.
Tap-to-payContactless tap on a supported mobile/tablet device — see Checkout / POS.
Payment linkA secure link sent to the client (e.g., by SMS) so they can pay their card on their own phone — used for remote deposits and when Zoca's AI books outbound.
NEEDS STRIPE

Card payments (including readers and tap-to-pay) run through the salon's connected Stripe account, so card acceptance requires completing Stripe setup. Cash works with no setup. Be honest about this in a demo: "card payments need your Stripe connected, which is a quick onboarding step."

"No payment required" bookings

Not every salon wants to take money up front. There's a no-payment-required booking policy option, so a salon can let clients book without paying a deposit at all — useful for businesses that prefer to collect entirely at the chair, or for specific services.

Paying the balance

The remaining balance is collected at the appointment through checkout. Split payments are Coming Soon — for now a single ticket must be settled by one payment method (e.g., all cash OR all card). The sale still tracks as it moves from open → partially paid → paid, and tips can be added at checkout.

COMING SOON — SPLIT PAYMENTS

When split payments ship, you'll be able to settle one ticket across multiple methods (e.g., part cash and part card). Until then, don't promise it on a call — one ticket, one payment method.

Refunds

Refunds are handled automatically and can be full or partial:

  • Card refunds go back to the original card (clients typically see them in ~5–10 business days).
  • Cash is returned in person.
  • Cancellation-driven refunds follow the cancellation policy — e.g., a client who cancels well ahead gets a full refund, while a last-minute cancel may get partial or none, per the salon's settings. A salon-initiated cancellation typically refunds in full.

6. FAQ & objection handling

"Do I have to take deposits?" No — deposits are optional and configurable. You can require them, set a flat or percentage amount, or run a "no payment required" policy and collect everything at the chair.

"What payment types do you support?" Cash, card, saved card-on-file, in-person card reader, and tap-to-pay on supported devices.

"Do I need Stripe?" For card payments, yes — your Stripe account connects to Zoca (a quick onboarding step). Cash needs no setup.

"Can a client pay part cash, part card?" Coming Soon. Today a ticket has to be settled by a single payment method — all cash OR all card. When split payments ship, you'll be able to do part-cash / part-card on the same ticket.

"How do refunds work?" Automatically, per your cancellation policy. Card refunds go back to the original card (about 5–10 business days); cash is returned in person.

"Can I save a client's card for next time?" Yes — card-on-file lets you charge a saved card for deposits, balances, or no-show fees without re-entering details.

"When does the deposit actually get charged?" At booking time, as soon as the appointment is created.

7. Key terms

  • Deposit — partial upfront payment to secure the booking (flat or %).
  • Balance — the remaining amount, collected at checkout.
  • Sale — the financial record auto-created with each booking; everything (deposit, balance, tips, refunds) rolls up here.
  • Card on file — a saved card that can be charged again without re-entry.
  • Split payment — paying one ticket across multiple methods (e.g., cash + card). Coming Soon — not live today.
  • No-payment-required policy — a setting that lets clients book without paying up front.
  • Stripe (connected account) — the payment processor behind card acceptance; the salon links its own account.
