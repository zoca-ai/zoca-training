Pitch it as: "This is where you run your day."

1. What it is

The Calendar is the merchant-facing scheduling screen at /dashboard/calendar. It shows the business's appointments on a time grid and is the central hub for daily operations: viewing the schedule, booking and editing appointments, managing time off, approving requests, and collecting payment.

It's a real, interactive calendar engine — multi-staff columns, live availability — and offers Day, Compact Day, Week, and Compact Week views, plus a sidebar with a mini-calendar for jumping around dates, staff filtering, and quick lists for pending approvals.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"Think of it like the appointment book at the front desk — except it's live, it's shared across all your staff, and you can take payment right from it."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

Most small salons still run on a paper book, a wall calendar, or a patchwork of texts and DMs. That creates three expensive problems the Calendar solves:

  • Double-bookings and gaps. The grid shows every stylist's day side by side, and the system blocks a slot the moment it's taken — so two clients don't land on the same chair, and empty gaps are obvious at a glance.
  • Front-desk busywork. Booking, moving, confirming, and checking out a client are usually separate steps in separate tools. Here they're all one screen, so the front desk does more with less.
  • No single source of truth. When the owner, the stylists, and the online booking page all read from the same calendar, nobody is working off a stale copy.
VALUE LINE

"Every no-show, every gap, every double-booking is lost revenue. The calendar's job is to make your day full, accurate, and easy to run."

3. How it works

Views and layout

  • Day, Compact Day, Week, and Compact Week views — pick the planning horizon. Day view is the front-desk workhorse; Week is for planning; Compact views offer a streamlined look.
  • Staff filtering — show one stylist's column or the whole team. Multi-staff businesses see each provider's day next to each other.
  • Sidebar — a mini-calendar to jump to any date, plus quick lists for pending approvals.

Everything opens in a side sheet, not a new page. When you click into an appointment to edit it, check out, or block time, it slides open over the calendar so you never lose your place. Close it and you're right back where you were.

Availability is calculated, not guessed. When you (or a client online) pick a service and stylist, the system builds the list of open slots by combining:

  • the stylist's working hours (and any special/holiday hours),
  • their breaks (lunch, vacation, personal blocks),
  • appointments already on the books,
  • temporary holds placed during someone else's checkout (a slot is held for ~10 minutes while a client finishes paying, so it can't be grabbed twice),
  • and the business's booking rules — how far in advance a client must book (minimum notice) and how far into the future booking is allowed.
GOOD TO KNOW

Times are always anchored to the location's time zone, so a Los-Angeles salon's 2 PM is 2 PM on the calendar regardless of where the client or staff member happens to be.

Appointment statuses an appointment moves through (useful when a prospect asks "how do you track where a client is at?"): pending payment → confirmed → completed, with branches for cancelled, rescheduled, and no-show (which splits into fee-collected vs fee-waived). Some flows can also start as pending approval when the owner approves requests before they're locked in. (A refund is a payment outcome driven by your cancellation/no-show policy — not a separate appointment status. See Booking lifecycle for the full set.)

4. What you can do from the Calendar

Action What it does
New Appointment Create a booking — pick the client, service(s), stylist, time slot, and configure a deposit if required.
Booking details Open any appointment to see the summary, pricing, and notes, and take actions: No-show, Cancel, Refund, Resend Receipt.
Checkout Take payment right from the appointment — cash, card, saved card-on-file, in-person card reader/tap, or a payment link — and add a tip. (See Checkout / POS.)
Add / Edit Time-Off Block out a stylist's time (lunch, errands, vacation) so it stops showing as bookable.
Pending Approvals Review incoming booking requests and Approve or Decline them.

5. Where it lives + demo path

  1. Log into the dashboard → click Calendar in the left navigation (/dashboard/calendar).
  2. Toggle Day / Compact Day / Week / Compact Week at the top to show the different views.
  3. Use the staff filter in the sidebar to show one stylist, then the whole team — this lands well for multi-chair salons.
  4. Click an empty slot → New Appointment → walk through picking a client, service, and time.
  5. Click an appointment → show Booking details and the action buttons (Checkout, No-show, Cancel).
  6. Point out the Pending Approvals list in the sidebar.

7. FAQ & objection handling

"We already have a calendar / we use Google Calendar." Google Calendar can't take a deposit, enforce your cancellation policy, hold a slot during checkout, run your online booking page, or check a client out. This is a calendar and a front desk and a register in one.

"What if two people try to book the same slot?" The system places a short hold (~10 minutes) on a slot while a client is checking out, and a confirmed appointment blocks the slot outright — so you don't get two clients on one chair.

"Can each stylist see only their own schedule?" Yes — staff filtering lets you view one provider or the whole team.

"What about no-shows showing up on the calendar?" You can mark an appointment as a no-show directly from its detail panel; see the No-shows doc for how fees work.

"Does it handle different time zones?" Appointments are pinned to the location's time zone, so the schedule reads correctly no matter where you're logged in.

"Can I block off time when a stylist is out?" Yes — Add/Edit Time-Off blocks a stylist's hours so those times stop being bookable.

8. Key terms

  • Slot — a bookable start/end window for a service with a given stylist; calculated live from hours, breaks, and existing bookings.
  • Hold — a temporary (~10-minute) reservation on a slot while a client completes checkout, so it can't be double-booked.
  • Break / Time-off — a stylist's blocked time (lunch, vacation, personal). Not bookable.
  • Pending approval — an incoming booking request awaiting the owner's Approve/Decline.
  • Minimum notice / booking window — rules for how soon and how far ahead clients can book.
