1. What it is

Two closely related flows, one register:

  • Checkout — settling up a completed appointment. Staff open the booking, the system loads what was booked into a cart, they add any retail products or upsells, apply tax/discounts/tips, pick a payment method, and take payment. The booking is marked paid.
  • Quick-sale (POS) — Coming Soon. A sale that isn't tied to an appointment: a walk-in buying a retail product, or a service rung up on the spot. Staff will build the cart from scratch on the Sales screen and check out the same way. Not live today — walk-ins need a same-day booking first.

Both use the same cart (service line items + product line items), the same payment methods, and the same receipt.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"It's the cash register — except it already knows about the appointment, your products, and your prices, so checking someone out takes seconds."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

  • One system instead of two. Salons often pay for a booking tool and a separate POS (Square, Clover). Zoca is both — one login, one set of reports, one place the money lives.
  • Retail revenue at the chair (coming soon, ~2 weeks). The cart will make it natural to add the shampoo or styling product the stylist just recommended, right at checkout — that's high-margin add-on revenue most salons leave on the table. Pitch it as launching shortly.
  • Tips done right. Tipping is built into checkout, so staff get tipped and it's tracked.
  • Real in-person card acceptance. A Stripe card reader or tap-to-pay means clients pay how they expect to, in person, without a clunky hand-keyed terminal.
VALUE LINE

"You're already taking the booking here. Why pay for a second system to take the money? Booking and register in one means cleaner books and more retail sales."

3. How it works

The checkout sidebar

From a completed appointment, staff open checkout. A sidebar slides out showing the amount due. They can:

  • add extra services to the cart (and add retail products — coming soon, ~2 weeks),
  • apply tax, a discount/offer, and a tip,
  • choose a payment method, and
  • take the payment — the sidebar shows live status (waiting → processing → paid) and closes itself on success.

The cart

The cart holds service line items (what was booked or rung up) and, once retail products go live (~2 weeks), product line items (retail goods). As items are added or removed, the totals recalculate. (See Offers & discounts for how a discount applies to a line.)

Payment methods at checkout

  • Cash — recorded instantly.
  • Card — entered and charged via Stripe.
  • Card on file — charge a saved card.
  • Card reader (in person) — a physical Stripe smart reader (e.g., BBPOS WisePOS E, Stripe S700). The reader connects to Stripe over the salon's internet; staff hit "Send to reader," the client taps/inserts, and the result comes back to the screen. There's also a simulated reader for training/testing without hardware.
  • Tap-to-pay — contactless tap on a supported mobile/tablet device. (This is distinct from web checkout, which collects card payment through the server-driven card reader described above — not via device tap-to-pay.)
HOW THE READER WORKS

The card reader is "server-driven" — the salon's computer doesn't talk to the reader directly; Zoca's backend tells the reader (through Stripe) to collect payment, and the screen updates when it's done. Translation for the prospect: "plug the reader into your internet, pair it once, and it just works — no fragile Bluetooth pairing every morning."

Quick-sale (walk-ins & retail) — Coming Soon

On the Sales screen, staff will start a New Sale, add services to the cart (and can apply an offer right there), then check out with any payment method — no appointment needed. This is Coming Soon (~2 weeks). Today, walk-in services still need to go through the booking flow first, then checkout — the walk-in quick-sale path (build a cart and check out with no booking) is not live yet. Once retail products, inventory, and gift cards land alongside quick-sale, this will also be how you ring up product and sell gift cards over the counter.

Setup for in-person cards

Card reader and tap-to-pay run on the salon's connected Stripe account. Pairing a real reader is a one-time step in Settings → Card Readers: generate a short code on the device, enter it with a label like "Front desk," and it's connected.

VERIFY BEFORE PROMISING

Card-reader and tap-to-pay availability can depend on the account's setup and region. Don't promise a hardware demo on a call until you've confirmed it's enabled in the demo/account you're using — use the simulated reader to show the flow if hardware isn't handy.

6. FAQ & objection handling

"We already have Square / Clover for payments." Then you're running two systems and reconciling them. Zoca is booking and register in one — one login, one set of reports, and your retail sales tie straight to your appointments.

"Can I sell products, not just services?" That's launching in the next couple of weeks. When retail products go live (alongside walk-in quick-sale), you'll add them to any ticket at checkout or ring up a product-only walk-in. Today you cannot ring up walk-ins without a booking — book a same-day appointment first, then check out.

"Can I sell gift cards?" Gift cards are coming shortly (~2 weeks) and will be sold through quick-sale like any other item. Great to mention as launching soon; just don't demo it as live yet.

"Do I need special hardware?" You can take cash and cards without hardware. For in-person tap, a Stripe card reader or a tap-to-pay device gives the smoothest experience.

"How does the card reader connect?" It pairs once over your internet through Stripe — no daily Bluetooth fiddling. Pair it in Settings with a short code from the device.

"Can I take a tip?" Yes — tipping is part of checkout.

"What about a client with no appointment who just wants product or a quick service?" Coming Soon (~2 weeks). Both walk-in retail and walk-in quick-sale for services (build a cart → check out, no booking) are on the roadmap. For services today, book a same-day appointment first, then run checkout against it. For retail-only or gift-card walk-ins today — same story, coming with the retail launch.

"Can I demo the reader without owning one?" Yes — there's a simulated reader that shows the full flow for training and demos.

7. Key terms

  • Checkout — settling a completed appointment into a paid sale.
  • Quick-sale (POS) — a sale with no appointment attached (walk-in / retail).
  • Cart — the ticket: service line items + product line items, with live totals.
  • Card reader — a physical Stripe smart reader (e.g., WisePOS E, S700) for in-person cards.
  • Simulated reader — a no-hardware test reader for demos and training.
  • Tap-to-pay — contactless card tap on a supported device.
  • Send to reader — the action that pushes a payment to the card reader.
