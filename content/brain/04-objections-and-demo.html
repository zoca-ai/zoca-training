TL;DR

The Brain demos in five tight minutes: Canvas (the wow shot) → Validate (walk the four verbs, hand them the mouse, confirm one fact live) → Preview (ask the AI and watch their own fact come back, cited) → Memory History (the audit-trail close). Then you handle objections — and there are three things you must never say. Pitch it as: "You're in control of every fact your AI knows — and I can show you exactly what it'll say."

1. What it is

This is the AE playbook for the Brain: the exact path to walk in a demo, the full set of objections you'll hear (and the line that turns each into a sale), and the three over-promises that will burn you on a call. It's the practical companion to the other three docs — read them for the what, read this for the how you sell it.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"The whole demo is five minutes and the whole story is one sentence: your AI only says what's in your Brain, and you control every fact in it — and I can prove it to you live."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

Owners don't buy the Brain because it's clever — they buy it because it removes the single biggest fear about AI: that it'll say something wrong to a real client. Every part of this demo exists to dismantle that fear in order:

  • Canvas shows them the AI's knowledge is finite and legible — not a black box.
  • Validate shows them they are in control — every card is theirs to confirm, edit, or remove.
  • Preview shows them the proof — their own fact, coming out of the AI, cited.
  • Memory History shows them there's an undo for everything.
VALUE LINE

"You're not selling a feature, you're selling control. By the end of five minutes the owner has seen that they control every fact the AI can use — and that's what closes."

3. How it works — the strict 5-minute demo path

Run this exact sequence. Resist the urge to open every tab or explain how the engine works — that drags and dilutes.

StepTabTimeWhat you do
1Canvas~5sOpen here. "This is your Brain at a glance — everything your AI knows about you."
2Validate~60sWalk the four verbs: Confirm, Refine, Edit, Reject. Show the source pills.
3Validate~15sHand them the mouse. Let the owner confirm one real fact about their business.
4Preview~90sAsk the question that fact answers. Read the cited answer back to them.
5Memory History~30sShow the revision log + one-click restore. "Here's your full audit trail."
DEMO TIP

The single most powerful moment is steps 3 and 4 back-to-back: the owner confirms a fact with their own hand, then watches it come straight out of the AI with a citation. That's the click. Build the whole demo around getting to that moment fast.

Do:

  • Hand them the mouse on Validate — let them feel the control.
  • Pick a fact that's their actual business (their price, their hours), not a generic example.
  • Keep it to five minutes and four tabs.

Don't:

  • Open every tab or give the Question Bank a full tour.
  • Talk about how the engine works under the hood — owners don't care and it slows you down.
  • Ad-lib new flows you can't reliably reproduce.
  • Promise roadmap items as if they're live.
DEMO TIP

Before any live call, confirm the Brain is enabled in your demo account — it's off by default. Confirm the tab and button labels match what you'll say (they can vary by version), and make sure the account has recognizably "salon" facts so Canvas and Preview feel real.

#ObjectionYour answer

5"What if you go out of business — is my Brain locked in?""Your Brain is your data — versioned, exportable, auditable, with full revision history. You're never locked in."
6"Can I see what the AI is actually telling people?""Yes — the Preview tab. Type any question and see the exact grounded answer, with citations to the memory it used."
7"What if two facts conflict — old price vs. new?""We catch it automatically. You get a three-button choice: replace, keep both with context, or cancel. No silent overwrites — ever."
8"Is this expensive? Will it run up an AI bill?""The AI processing is capped at $50 a month per location. You can't accidentally overspend."
9"Can my staff edit this too?""Today the owner is the only one who confirms a fact — by design, so your voice stays consistent. Staff can save candidates with one click; you approve."
10"We already have Yelp, Google, our website — why another place?""You're not adding a place, you're connecting them. The Brain reads all of those automatically. It's the layer that makes your AI actually use what's already there instead of guessing."
OBJECTION

The two objections you'll hear most are #1 ("it'll make things up") and #15-style "the content sounds generic / not like me." Both have the same cure: feed the Brain your real context. #1 is answered by owner approval; "generic content" is answered by "the Brain is why your content sounds like you and not everyone else."

OBJECTION

"What if your AI tells my customers the wrong thing?" — Honesty + control. Every fact in the Brain has to be confirmed by you before it goes live. But yes — if a wrong fact gets approved, it WILL repeat across every Zoca tool that reads the Brain (Front Desk, Discovery content, GBP posts, Command Center answers). That's exactly why we built Memory History as a full audit trail with one-click restore: when you spot a wrong fact, you fix it once and every tool corrects on the next read. Confirming carefully matters; correcting fast is built in.

5. The NEVER-SAY traps

Three over-promises will come back to bite you. Learn the say-instead for each.

Never saySay instead
"The AI will never hallucinate.""It grounds against facts you've approved, and every response is scored against them behind the scenes to catch drift."
"Set it and forget it.""About five minutes a week of quick approvals keeps your Brain current."
"Your Brain is shared across the Zoca network.""Your Brain is private to your business. No other owner ever sees it."
NEVER SAY

"It learns automatically from every call." That over-promises and it's not how calls work. Say: "it pulls candidate facts from every call into your inbox for you to confirm." Calls produce questions; the owner's answers produce knowledge.

NEVER SAY

"It's just ChatGPT for your business." Say: "it's the memory layer that makes an AI accurate for your specific business." ChatGPT knows the world; the Brain knows this salon.

6. Roadmap honesty in the room

A few honest gaps will come up. Handle them as growth, not as flaws — and never commit a date.

ROADMAP

If a prospect asks about any of these, it's fine to say "coming soon," but do not demo it as live and do not promise a date:

- Time-bound offers — today an offer you store won't auto-expire when it ends; there's no validity-window control yet. - Per-surface control — you can't yet say "use this fact only on the phone, not in my blog." A fact is available to all tools today. - Bulk FAQ import — you add answers one at a time in the Question Bank; there's no paste-the-whole-list option yet. - Other languages — English-only today. Note the ask, don't commit a date. - Staff as fact-writers — owner-only confirmation today; staff can save candidates but not approve.

GOOD TO KNOW

"Owner-only writer" and "English-only" aren't always objections — sometimes they're reassurances. Owners often like that staff can't change what the AI says. Read the room and frame it as control when it helps.

7. FAQ & objection handling

"This sounds like a lot to set up." It's the opposite — it sets itself up. Day one it has starter facts; from there it learns from your calls, website, and Google profile and just asks you to approve what it found. Five minutes a week.

"Can you prove it won't say something wrong?" Yes, live — in the Preview tab. I'll ask it a question the way your client would and you'll see the exact answer, with a citation back to the fact behind it. If you don't like the fact, you reject it and it's gone.

"What if I want to start over?" We'd archive rather than wipe — your history is valuable and recoverable. If you ever truly need a reset, that's a conversation with our team, not a one-click delete.

"The AI said something embarrassing on a real call — now what?" That's exactly the kind of thing the audit trail is for. We can pull the conversation, see which fact drove the answer, and you can fix or reject that fact so it never happens again.

INTERNAL

Don't treat this as a self-serve fix on the call. Per the CX triage matrix this is an Escalate-tier event: pull the transcript + groundedness score, flag the memory chain that produced the answer, and loop in product. Reassure the owner there's a clear path, then route it — don't promise an instant one-click fix yourself.

"Does this work for my industry?" The Brain ships with starter questions tuned to the industry on day one, and it learns the specifics of your business from there. It's built for salons, spas, and beauty businesses.
