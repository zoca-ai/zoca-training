TL;DR

The Brain learns two ways: when your AI already knows an answer, it confirms it in your inbox; when it doesn't know, that becomes a question for you to answer. It's seeded once at onboarding from your website, Google profile, past calls and existing Zoca data, and then grows as you use Zoca — from your Command Center chats, the questions you answer, one-click saves, and a nightly scan that spots gaps and asks you about them. You're in control of everything it learns — review, edit, or remove any fact anytime; conflicts never silently overwrite; and the AI cost is capped so it can never run up a bill. Pitch it as: "It learns from your business and never forgets — and you're always in control."

1. What it is

"How it learns" is the engine behind the Brain: the way raw business activity — phone calls, your website, your Google listing, your chats — turns into approved, usable facts. Two simple paths, one inbox. Everything the Brain learns shows up there for you to confirm, edit, or remove — you stay in control of what your AI knows.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"Every time your AI bumps into something it doesn't know, that turns into a quick question for you. You answer it once, and it never gets it wrong again. Over a few weeks, it ends up knowing your business the way a receptionist would after months of training."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

Owners have two fears about a learning AI, and the way the Brain learns answers both:

  • "It'll learn the wrong thing and embarrass me." It can't run away from you — because you stay in control of what it knows. Everything it learns lands in your inbox to confirm, edit, or remove; anything it's unsure about waits for your yes, and even a fact it's confident enough to use, you can pull in one click.
  • "It'll get out of date the second my business changes." It won't — because it keeps up through how the owner uses Zoca. Command Center chats feed it continuously, the owner answers the questions it surfaces, and a nightly scan flags gaps to confirm. When something changes, the owner confirms it in seconds.

"Your business changes all the time — your prices, your hours, your team. The Brain keeps up because every time you tell Zoca something or answer one of its questions, that's a confirmed fact. You don't re-enter anything; you just confirm the changes as they come up."

Ongoing freshness comes from your usage (Command Center chats, answered questions, saves) plus a nightly gap-scan, not a nightly re-scrape.

3. How it works

GOOD TO KNOW

One-time seed vs ongoing freshness. At setup we read your recent call transcripts once to seed the Brain with initial facts (alongside your website, Google profile, and existing Zoca data). From then on, ongoing freshness comes from three sources: chats you have with Command Center, questions you answer from the inbox, and a nightly gap-scan that finds what's missing. Calls are NOT re-read continuously — so when an AE says "the Brain learns from your calls," that means the setup seed, not an ongoing crawl. Ongoing call-driven learning happens only in the sense that live calls can surface candidate facts + unanswered questions into your inbox for you to approve.

The two learning paths

When a customer asks something, the Brain asks itself one question: do I already know the answer?

  • Path A — KNOWN. The Front Desk already has the fact and uses it on the call. That conversation lands in your Validate inbox so you can confirm the answer was accurate. Confirm it, and it becomes a permanent, versioned, cited memory.
  • Path B — UNKNOWN. The AI hits something it can't answer. That question lands in your Question Bank. You answer it in about 30 seconds, and your answer becomes a permanent, versioned, cited memory.

Both paths end the same place: the fact shows up on Canvas, is testable in Preview, and is used by every Zoca AI from the next interaction forward.

GOOD TO KNOW

There's a nuance on calls worth getting right. The clean line to say to an owner is: "Calls produce questions; your answers produce knowledge." — keep that simple on a call. Internally, be accurate: the Brain does mine call transcripts for candidate facts (via the Retell-transcript extractor and the live propose-memory callback), the same way it does your website, Google profile and chats. Those candidates land in the owner's inbox to review — confirm, edit, or remove. So don't assert internally that "calls never produce facts"; the honest version is "calls produce both questions and reviewable candidate facts." (And per the auto-resolve nuance: high-confidence, non-conflicting candidates can go live automatically, but the owner can still edit or remove any of them.)

What does not feed back in

One deliberate boundary worth knowing: content the AI generates — the blog posts and social posts it writes — is not fed back into the Brain as new facts. That's on purpose. The Brain is built on the owner's confirmed facts, not on the AI's own output. It won't quietly start treating its own writing as truth.

Conflict resolution — no silent overwrites

When a new candidate contradicts a fact the Brain already has — "open Sundays" vs. "closed Sundays," an old price vs. a new one — the Brain doesn't silently overwrite. It flags the conflict and hands the owner a three-way choice:

  1. Replace the old fact with the new one.
  2. Keep both (with context, e.g., different seasons or locations).
  3. Cancel and leave things as they were.
VALUE LINE

"If two facts ever disagree — say an old price and a new one — the Brain catches it and asks you. Replace, keep both, or cancel. No silent overwrites — ever."

The cost cap

The AI work behind the Brain is metered, and there's a hard ceiling: AI costs are capped at $50 per month per location. An owner cannot accidentally run up an AI bill, no matter how much activity flows through.

GOOD TO KNOW

$50/month is the AI-processing cap behind the Brain — it's a safety ceiling, not the price of the product. Use it to kill the "will this run up a huge bill?" fear, not as a pricing line.

7. FAQ & objection handling

"My business changes all the time — won't this get out of date?" It stays current through how you use Zoca, not a stale one-time import. We seed it from your website, Google profile and past calls at setup; after that it grows from your Command Center chats, the questions you answer, and a nightly scan that flags gaps. When something changes, you confirm it in seconds. (Internally: there's no nightly website re-crawl — freshness is usage-driven.)

"Does it learn automatically from my calls?" It pulls candidate facts and unanswered questions from your calls into your inbox — but you confirm them. Calls produce the questions; your answers produce the knowledge.

"What happens when two facts conflict?" The Brain catches it and gives you a three-button choice: replace the old one, keep both with context, or cancel. It never silently overwrites.

"Will this run up a huge AI bill?" No — the AI processing is capped at $50 a month per location. You can't accidentally overspend.

"Does it feed its own blog posts back in as facts?" No, deliberately. The Brain is built on facts you confirm, not on content the AI wrote — so it never treats its own output as truth.

"How long until it's actually good?" It's useful day one with starter facts, and it compounds fast — within a week or two of light upkeep it knows your business the way a trained receptionist would, and it never forgets.
