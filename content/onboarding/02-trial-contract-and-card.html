TL;DR

Before a salon starts using Zoca, three things happen at the end of onboarding: they pick a plan, e-sign one contract per business, and put a card on file — which starts a 7-day trial. They're charged only after the trial ends, "like Netflix." Adding more agents later just augments the same contract (no re-signing, same card), and if a payment ever fails there's a short grace period before access closes. Pitch it as: "See it work first — pay only after the trial."

1. What it is

The "formalities" that close out onboarding: a plan, a signed contract, and a saved card that kicks off a free trial. None of it happens before the prospect has seen the product working on their own salon — demo always comes before payment. It's the commitment step, designed to be quick, familiar, and low-friction.

  • The trial — 7 days, free, with the first charge landing only after it ends (confirm the exact length before quoting — it's configurable).
  • The card on file — required to start the trial; card only.
  • The contract — one per business, e-signed before the payment screen.
SAY IT SIMPLY

"It works exactly like Netflix. You put a card down, you get a free week, and you're not charged until the week's up — and you can cancel."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

Owners are rightly wary of "free trials" that quietly bill them or contracts they can't read. Zoca's version is built to be the opposite:

  • No payment before proof. They watch the product run on their own business first. The card only comes up once they've decided they want in.
  • A familiar model. "Card on file, charged after a free week" is something everyone already understands from streaming services — no new mental model to sell.
  • One contract, not a stack. Sign once for the business. Add another agent down the road and it just extends the existing agreement — no new signature, no new card.
VALUE LINE

"You see it work on your own salon before you spend a dollar — and even then, you've got a free week. That's about as low-risk as software gets."

3. How it works

The card on file is mandatory — there's no path without it. A card is required to start the trial, full stop. It's validated for funds at the time it's added, so a card with insufficient funds or an authorization error is blocked. Card only — PayPal and Apple Pay are not supported.

The trial is 7 days, charged after it ends. There's no "premium without trial" — every new subscription starts with the trial. The first charge auto-bills once the trial completes, unless the business has cancelled.

If a payment fails, there's a grace period. Rather than cutting access off instantly, Zoca gives a short window (a few days) to fix the payment. If it's still unresolved at the end of that window, functionality closes.

The contract is one per business and e-signed up front. It's a service agreement signed via email in a familiar e-signature flow: agree → sign digitally → finish → a signed copy is emailed back. It must be signed before the payment screen. If the business adds more agents later, the contract is augmented automatically — no re-signing, and the same card stays on file.

Cancelling the trial isn't one click. Cancellation is available, but it requires booking an offboarding call rather than flipping a switch — so there's a human conversation before anyone walks away.

GOOD TO KNOW

Card details are stored by Zoca's billing provider, not held by Zoca directly — a point worth making if a security-minded owner asks where their card lives. Exact trial length, grace-period length, and per-product pricing are configurable and can change, so confirm the current numbers before quoting them on a call.

7. FAQ & objection handling

OBJECTION

"Why do you need my card before I've even seen results?" You have seen it — we demo on your own salon before this step, and you don't pay anything for a full week. The card is just how the free trial starts, the same way Netflix or any subscription works. You're charged only after the trial, and you can cancel. Honestly, it also helps us keep the trial for people who are seriously considering it.

OBJECTION

"Will switching to you be painful?" We do the heavy lifting on the onboarding call. Depending on your current platform, we either run alongside it or migrate you over to Zoca's bookings and payments — your choice. (See Platform journeys.)

"Can I pay with PayPal or Apple Pay?" Not today — it's card only. The card has to be valid and funded to start the trial.

"What if my card gets declined after the trial?" You get a short grace period to sort it out — we don't cut you off the moment a charge fails. If it's still unresolved after that window, access pauses until it's fixed.

"Do I have to sign a new contract every time I add something?" No. The contract is one per business. Add another agent later and it simply extends what you already signed — no new signature, same card.

"How do I cancel during the trial?" You can cancel, but it's not a one-click thing — we set up a quick offboarding call so we can help you wrap up properly. (Confirm the current process before promising specifics.)

8. Key terms

  • Trial — the 7-day free period that starts a subscription; first charge lands only after it ends (confirm length before quoting).
  • Card on file — the saved, validated card required to start the trial; card only (no PayPal / Apple Pay). Stored by the billing provider, not by Zoca directly.
  • Contract / service agreement — the one-per-business agreement, e-signed by email before payment; augments automatically when agents are added.
  • Grace period — the short window to fix a failed payment before access closes.
  • Offboarding call — the booked conversation required to cancel a trial or subscription (not a one-click cancel).
