1. What it is

Onboarding is the guided setup that takes a salon from "interested" to "live on Zoca." It's web-based (run in a browser) and assisted — the prospect doesn't stumble onto it and sign up alone. A new visitor to the website is pointed to "book a demo", and the AE shares the onboarding link directly so the two of you go through it together.

It's organized into three sections, and within the middle section it introduces Zoca's AI one agent at a time — each agent greets the prospect, walks them through its own setup, says goodbye, and hands off to the next. Think of it like walking into a physical Zoca store and being passed from one specialist to the next.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"We're not going to email you a login and wish you luck. We get on a call, share my screen, and set the whole thing up together — and by the time we hang up, you're live."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

Most salon owners have been burned by software that's a chore to set up — hours of typing in services, fighting with settings, and a half-finished account they never go back to. Zoca's onboarding solves that three ways:

  • It's done with them, not to them. An AE drives the session, so there's no "figure it out yourself" cliff. The owner watches their salon come to life on screen.
  • It's fast. The whole thing — website, bookings, and AI receptionist — comes together in well under an hour. They don't lose a working day to setup.
  • It pulls in what already exists. Services are imported automatically from the salon's current booking link rather than retyped, so the owner reviews and confirms instead of building from scratch.
VALUE LINE

"Every minute a salon owner spends fighting setup is a minute they're not behind the chair. We do the heavy lifting on one call so they don't have to."

COMMON MISCONCEPTION

Reps describe onboarding as "fill out the form, takes 10 minutes." Owners hear forms and check out. Reframe: "The agents set themselves up while you watch — you're answering three questions, not filling out a form."

3. The one hard requirement

RULE OF THUMB

There is exactly one non-negotiable in the whole sale: a laptop or iPad must be in the shop. Onboarding — and the live demo — cannot run without a real screen to share and a phone for the Test Call. Everything else (vertical, lead volume, current platform) flexes; this doesn't.

Desktop / iPad first. Onboarding is built to be run on a desktop or iPad. It's responsive on mobile for the rare case you must, but the first impression, the full view of the product, and the sense that this is a serious tool all land best on a bigger screen. Default to sharing your desktop screen. The mobile app is recommended, not required — web is the primary experience, so never frame an app download as a mandatory onboarding step.

4. How it works — the three sections

Section 1 — Platform setup. The session opens by choosing which agent(s) the prospect wants, then signs them in with Google, identifies their location and contact details, and runs an audit of their Google Business Profile (their "audit"). The audit honestly scores where they stand today — rank, profile completeness, reviews, and rating — which sets up the "here's the gap, here's how Zoca closes it" story.

Section 2 — Agent setup. Each chosen AI agent walks the prospect through its own quick setup, in a fixed order:

  • Discovery Agent — validates the salon's existing booking link, imports and confirms their services, builds the website, and shows a summary. This is the dramatically fast part.
  • Bookings — confirms services, prices, and durations so the salon can take appointments through Zoca.
  • Front Desk Agent (Zoe) — connects the relevant platform, sets up the phone number and voice, configures call forwarding, and runs a live Test Call.

Section 3 — Formalities. The wrap-up: pick a plan, e-sign the contract, put a card on file, and the trial begins. (Full detail in Trial, contract & card.)

GOOD TO KNOW

Services are imported from the prospect's current booking link automatically, then surfaced on a review-services screen so the owner confirms everything — especially price — before the website builds. Pricing is sensitive, so it always gets a human "yes" rather than being published sight-unseen.

DEMO TIP

The single most impressive moment in the build is Discovery building the website from an imported booking link in seconds — and the single most converting moment is the Front Desk Test Call (§5, Step 15). Let both land; don't rush past them.

5. Running onboarding as your demo — the Discovery Neo screen-by-screen

The newer Discovery Neo preview turns onboarding into a 19-screen demo script you can run from the very first call — taking a prospect from "send me a deck" to live customer in under 15 minutes. Use this as your screen-by-screen narration. (Validate the exact screen set against production before a live customer setup — see the SOURCE NOTE at top.)

The 19 screens fall into three arcs: A. Platform setup (0–5) · B. Agent setup (6–16) · C. Formalities + launch (17–18).

GOOD TO KNOW

Three drop-off points to watch — Step 1 (Google OAuth), Step 5 (booking platform), Step 15 (Test Call nerves) — each has a prepared recovery below. And don't count steps out loud; if asked "how many?", say "under 10 minutes — about as long as making coffee."

Arc A — Platform setup

  • Step 0 · See Your Gap. "Where do customers find your business?" — two fields (Business Name, City), one CTA. "Watch — I'm not pulling this from a deck. We're scanning your Google profile, reviews, AI visibility, live."
  • Step 1 · Connect Google. One "Sign in with Google" button, read-only. Drop-off #1: "my old marketing guy set it up" → keep moving with the public-data audit and connect later; "I'll do it later" → "you lose the audit, and the audit is the thing — take 90 seconds now."
  • Step 2 · Your Details. Name, phone, email. The phone is "the number I'll text you when there's something to approve."
  • Step 3 · Meet Discovery. The Discovery Agent introduces itself (GBP · Website · Content · Reviews). A vibe screen — let them read.
  • Step 4 · Your Audit. Three scores — Local SEO, Traditional SEO, AI SEO — mostly red/amber. Read the scores aloud, then go silent for five seconds. "Does this match what you thought, or worse?" Their answer tells you which leak to lead with. This is where urgency lands.
  • Step 5 · Booking Platform. A grid + Other + Skip. Drop-off #2: "not listed" → pick Other, add services manually once; "paper/phone" → Skip, the Front Desk Agent becomes the booking platform.

Arc B — Discovery, then Front Desk

  • Step 6 · Your Website → Step 7 · Building Website (an ~18-second animated build; read the stages aloud) → Step 8 · Your Content (blog/GBP/service-page preview cards with target keyword + volume — open one, ask "pick a keyword your customers would Google," type it in, show the volume) → Step 9 · Keyword Opportunity (a demand map: tracked keywords, monthly searches, current rank by tier).
  • Step 10 · Meet Front Desk → Step 11 · Setting Up (~6s transitional) → Step 12 · Front Desk Setup (existing number + forwarding or a new Zoca number; deposit toggle No/25%/50%/Full; cancellation policy) → Step 13 · Train Front Desk (three open questions: what makes you different, top customer questions, key policies — ~2 minutes) → Step 14 · Review Front Desk (pick a voice, edit the greeting, see a sample booking transcript).
  • Step 15 · Test Call · THE WOW MOMENT. The prospect's own number is shown big. Before they dial: "Pull out your phone, call it, try to break it — ask hard questions." During: stay completely silent, don't narrate. After: "What did you think?" and let them talk first.
FOR THE AE

If the agent stumbles on a niche service, don't apologize — "that's a real model talking; the team trains it daily. The point is you just heard an AI answer your phone and book an appointment." The Test Call is non-negotiable: if you're short on time, cut something else, never the call. Don't name any third-party voice vendor — the voice is built in-house.

  • Step 16 · Ready to Launch. A recap of what happens next (GBP posts start, blogs draft, calls route, reviews trigger, dashboard goes live).

Arc C — Formalities + launch

  • Step 17 · Your Plan. The 12-month Discovery Programme (Build / Attract / Accelerate / Dominate) with annual deliverables and an agency-stack cost compare. Sell the deliverables, not the price — this screen lands as confirmation, not pitch.
  • Step 18 · Go Live. One button, confetti, redirect to the Day-0 dashboard. Hand the tap to the prospect.

6. Where it lives + path

  1. The prospect lands on the website and is prompted to book a demo — they don't self-serve.
  2. You run a discovery/demo call on an account already loaded with their business (demo before payment — always). Increasingly, you run the onboarding screens themselves as that demo (§5).
  3. When they're ready, you share the onboarding link and run the sections together on a screen-share.
  4. Onboarding ends with the prospect live — website, bookings, and/or AI receptionist working on their own salon.
DEMO TIP

Always run onboarding on desktop or iPad and confirm you're on the current environment before a live customer setup — the newest version lives in the demo/sandbox environment and production can lag. When in doubt, check with the product team that morning.

8. FAQ & objection handling

"Can I just sign up myself and try it?" Onboarding is assisted on purpose — we run it with you so nothing's half-finished and you're actually live by the end. That's why new visitors are pointed to book a demo rather than a public sign-up.

"How long is this going to take? I'm slammed." Well under an hour for everything, and as a live demo, under 15 minutes — about as long as making coffee. You're answering three questions, not filling out a form.

"Do I have to retype all my services and prices?" No. We pull them in automatically from your current booking link. You review them on one screen and confirm — we never publish a price you haven't signed off on.

"Can I do this on my phone?" You can, but we strongly prefer desktop or iPad — you see the whole product clearly and it's a better first impression. And you'll need a phone in hand for the Test Call regardless. The app is recommended, not required.

"What happens after we hang up — am I on my own?" If you take more than one part of Zoca, a product specialist calls you within a day or two to get everything dialed in, plus weekly check-ins through your first month. (See "FDE" below.)

"I run several locations — can you set all of them up?" Today onboarding is built for solo and single-location businesses (multiple staff are fine — it's multiple locations that aren't yet). Multi-location onboarding is on the near-term roadmap.
