TL;DR

Zoca onboarding is an assisted, desktop-first setup you run with the prospect on a screen-share — not a public self-serve sign-up. It moves through three sections (platform setup → per-agent setup → formalities) and walks the prospect, one AI agent at a time, through Discovery, then Bookings, then Front Desk. It's fast — getting a salon live with a website, bookings, and an AI receptionist takes well under an hour. Pitch it as: "Three steps, one screen-share, and you're live."

1. What it is

Onboarding is the guided setup that takes a salon from "interested" to "live on Zoca." It's web-based (run in a browser) and assisted — the prospect doesn't stumble onto it and sign up alone. A new visitor to the website is pointed to "book a demo", and the AE shares the onboarding link directly so the two of you go through it together.

It's organized into three sections, and within the middle section it introduces Zoca's AI one agent at a time — each agent greets the prospect, walks them through its own setup, says goodbye, and hands off to the next. Think of it like walking into a physical Zoca store and being passed from one specialist to the next.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"We're not going to email you a login and wish you luck. We get on a call, share my screen, and set the whole thing up together — and by the time we hang up, you're live."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

Most salon owners have been burned by software that's a chore to set up — hours of typing in services, fighting with settings, and a half-finished account they never go back to. Zoca's onboarding solves that three ways:

  • It's done with them, not to them. An AE drives the session, so there's no "figure it out yourself" cliff. The owner watches their salon come to life on screen.
  • It's fast. The whole thing — website, bookings, and AI receptionist — comes together in well under an hour. They don't lose a working day to setup.
  • It pulls in what already exists. Services are imported automatically from the salon's current booking link rather than retyped, so the owner reviews and confirms instead of building from scratch.
VALUE LINE

"Every minute a salon owner spends fighting setup is a minute they're not behind the chair. We do the heavy lifting on one call so they don't have to."

3. How it works

Desktop / iPad first. Onboarding is built to be run on a desktop or iPad. It's responsive on mobile for the rare case you must, but the first impression, the full view of the product, and the sense that this is a serious tool all land best on a bigger screen. Default to sharing your desktop screen.

Section 1 — Platform setup. The session opens by choosing which agent(s) the prospect wants, then signs them in with Google, identifies their location and contact details, and runs an audit of their Google Business Profile (their "audit"). The audit honestly scores where they stand today — rank, profile completeness, reviews, and rating — which sets up the "here's the gap, here's how Zoca closes it" story.

Section 2 — Agent setup. Each chosen AI agent walks the prospect through its own quick setup, in a fixed order:

  • Discovery — validates the salon's existing booking link, imports and confirms their services, builds the website, and shows a summary. This is the dramatically fast part.
  • Bookings — confirms services, prices, and durations so the salon can take appointments through Zoca.
  • Front Desk — connects the relevant platform, sets up the phone number and voice, configures call forwarding, and runs a live test call.

Section 3 — Formalities. The wrap-up: pick a plan, e-sign the contract, put a card on file, and the 7-day trial begins. (Full detail in Trial, contract & card.)

GOOD TO KNOW

Services are imported from the prospect's current booking link automatically, then surfaced on a review-services screen so the owner confirms everything — especially price — before the website builds. Pricing is sensitive, so it always gets a human "yes" rather than being published sight-unseen.

DEMO TIP

The single most impressive moment is Discovery building the website from an imported booking link in seconds. On the old mobile-app flow, just setting up Discovery took several minutes; in the new web flow, onboarding all three agents takes less time than that. Let that speed land — don't rush past it.

5. Where it lives + path

  1. The prospect lands on the website and is prompted to book a demo — they don't self-serve.
  2. You run a discovery/demo call on an account already loaded with their business (demo before payment — always).
  3. When they're ready, you share the onboarding link and run the three sections together on a screen-share.
  4. Onboarding ends with the prospect live — website, bookings, and/or AI receptionist working on their own salon.
DEMO TIP

Always run onboarding on desktop or iPad and confirm you're on the current environment before a live customer setup — the newest version lives in the demo/sandbox environment and production can lag. When in doubt, check with the product team that morning.

7. FAQ & objection handling

"Can I just sign up myself and try it?" Onboarding is assisted on purpose — we run it with you so nothing's half-finished and you're actually live by the end. That's why new visitors are pointed to book a demo rather than a public sign-up.

"How long is this going to take? I'm slammed." Well under an hour for everything — website, bookings, and AI receptionist. The website piece, built from your existing services, comes together in seconds.

"Do I have to retype all my services and prices?" No. We pull them in automatically from your current booking link. You review them on one screen and confirm — we never publish a price you haven't signed off on.

"Can I do this on my phone?" You can, but we strongly prefer desktop or iPad — you see the whole product clearly and it's a better first impression. Phone is a fallback, not the default.

"What happens after we hang up — am I on my own?" If you take more than one part of Zoca, a product specialist calls you within a day or two to get everything dialed in, plus weekly check-ins through your first month. (See "FDE" below.)

"I run several locations — can you set all of them up?" Today onboarding is built for solo and single-location businesses (multiple staff are fine — it's multiple locations that aren't yet). Multi-location onboarding is coming soon, targeting around mid-June.

8. Key terms

  • Assisted onboarding — setup run with the prospect on a screen-share by an AE; not a public self-serve sign-up.
  • The three sections — platform setup, per-agent setup, and formalities (plan/contract/card/trial).
  • Discovery / Bookings / Front Desk — the three AI agents, introduced one at a time in that fixed order.
  • Service import — pulling the salon's services automatically from their current booking link, confirmed on a review screen before the website builds.
  • GBP audit — the honest score of the salon's Google Business Profile (rank, completeness, reviews, rating) run during platform setup.
  • FDE (white-glove follow-up) — the post-onboarding product-specialist call within 24–48h plus weekly first-month calls for multi-product accounts.
