TL;DR

Every salon already runs something — a booking platform, a phone setup, a website. Onboarding meets them where they are through one of five platform journeys, chosen by what they use today. Some platforms hand Zoca a direct connection; others are connected through a secure automated assistant that reads their data without changing their platform; and the flagship path migrates them fully onto Zoca's own bookings and payments. There's no price difference between journeys — only a difference in setup. Pitch it as: "We meet you wherever you are today."

1. What it is

A "platform journey" is the specific onboarding path Zoca uses based on the prospect's current booking platform. The destination is the same — a live salon on Zoca — but how we get their data and connect their agents differs. There are five journeys, and which one a prospect lands in is determined by what they're running now, not by what they pay.

The point of having five is simple: a salon shouldn't have to rip out their current setup just to try Zoca. We either plug into what they have, read it safely without disrupting it, or move them over cleanly — whatever fits.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"You don't have to tear anything down to try us. Depending on what you use today, we either connect straight in, read your info safely, or move you over — your call."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

"Switching costs" are the single biggest reason salons stay stuck on software they've outgrown. The platform journeys exist to make that fear go away:

  • No forced rip-and-replace. If they want to keep their current platform running for now, several journeys let Zoca work alongside it.
  • Their data isn't trapped. Onboarding pulls in what's needed to get them live, so they're not starting from a blank slate.
  • A clean path forward when they're ready. The flagship journey moves them fully onto Zoca's bookings and payments — the end state most salons want once they trust it.
VALUE LINE

"The reason most salons stay on bad software is the dread of switching. We take that off the table — we do the connecting and the moving, on the call, with you watching."

3. How it works

There are three shapes the connection can take, spread across the five journeys:

Direct connection. Some platforms provide a built-in way for Zoca to connect to them directly. When a prospect is on one of these, onboarding links the two systems cleanly and pulls in the data needed to set up their agents.

Secure automated assistant. When a platform doesn't offer a direct connection, Zoca uses a secure automated assistant that signs in on the salon's behalf to read their information. It's read-only on their platform — it brings their current business data (their services, staff, and hours) into Zoca, but it doesn't act inside or change their existing system. Don't overstate what transfers: it reads the current setup, not necessarily a full history of past clients (confirm the exact scope per platform). This is how Zoca supports salons on platforms that would otherwise be a dead end.

Flagship migration. The flagship path migrates the salon onto Zoca's own bookings and payments — the full end-state where Zoca is running the show, not just reading from someone else's system.

A connected platform is required for the relevant agents. The agents that depend on outside data (for example, importing services or routing calls) need the platform connection in place. No connection, no data for those agents to work from — so the journey is part of, not separate from, getting the agents live.

GOOD TO KNOW

There is no price difference between the journeys. A prospect doesn't pay more or less because of which platform they came from — the journey only changes the setup steps, not the bill.

CUSTOMER-SAFE LANGUAGE

Always describe the secure automated assistant in safe, professional terms — "a secure automated assistant that reads your information for you," "read-only on your side," "we don't touch your existing system." Never call it "hacky," "a workaround," or anything that sounds improvised or risky. The technology is deliberate; the language should sound deliberate too.

5. Where it lives + path

  1. During onboarding's platform-setup section, you identify what the prospect runs today.
  2. That selection routes them into the right journey — direct connection, secure automated assistant, or flagship migration.
  3. The connected platform feeds the relevant agents so they can set up with real data.
  4. Whichever journey they're on, the price is the same and they end up live on Zoca.
DEMO TIP

Lead with reassurance, not mechanics. "Whatever you're on now, we've got a path for it — and it costs the same either way" lands far better than walking through connection internals. Save the detail for prospects who ask.

7. FAQ & objection handling

OBJECTION

"Will switching off my current platform be painful?" No — that's exactly what the journeys are for. Depending on what you run, we either connect straight in, read your data safely without touching your platform, or migrate you over to Zoca fully. You pick the pace; we do the heavy lifting on the call.

"Do I have to give you my login?" For platforms without a direct connection, our secure automated assistant signs in on your behalf to read your information — read-only, so it never changes anything in your existing system. For platforms that connect directly, no assistant is needed.

"Does the price change depending on how you connect?" No. There's no price difference between any of the journeys — the connection method only changes the setup, not the cost.

"Can I keep my current platform running while I try Zoca?" Yes — several journeys let us work alongside it. When you're ready to consolidate, the flagship path moves you fully onto Zoca's bookings and payments.

"What about my AI receptionist — how does it connect to my platform?" Front Desk's platform connection has its own specifics, and they're being refined per platform. For the exact Front-Desk platform details, see the Front Desk docs rather than my quoting them here.

8. Key terms

  • Platform journey — the onboarding path chosen by the prospect's current booking platform; five total, no price difference between them.
  • Direct connection — a built-in link some platforms offer that lets Zoca connect to them directly.
  • Secure automated assistant — Zoca's read-only method of bringing in data from platforms without a direct connection; reads on the salon's behalf and never changes their existing system.
  • Flagship migration — the path that moves a salon fully onto Zoca's own bookings and payments.
  • Connected platform — the in-place platform connection that the data-dependent agents need in order to set up and work.
