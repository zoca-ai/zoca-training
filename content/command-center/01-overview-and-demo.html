TL;DR

Command Center is the AI co-pilot built into the Zoca dashboard — "Claude Code for salon owners." Instead of hunting through menus, the owner just says what they want in plain English, and Command Center either pulls the answer or makes the change for them. It's on every page (plus a Cmd+K shortcut), it remembers their preferences, and it works across all their locations in one conversation. Pitch it as: "Run the whole product by just telling it what you want."

1. What it is

Command Center is an in-dashboard AI assistant that performs actions on the salon's behalf in plain language — not just answers questions. That distinction is the whole point.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"Think about the difference between an AI that gives you advice and one that actually does the job for you. Command Center is the second kind. You tell it 'change my hours' and it changes your hours — you don't go find the setting yourself."

The internal framing the product team uses is "Claude Code for salon owners": where a regular chatbot might tell you how to do something, Command Center actually does it. The name is literal — it's a place where you give commands, not with a mouse and keyboard the traditional way, but in natural language.

It lives on every page of the web dashboard (a button in the top-right, plus a Cmd+K keyboard shortcut), it can expand to full screen, and it supports multiple separate chat threads. The same conversation follows the owner as they move between pages and between locations.

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

All-in-one platforms have one chronic problem: they're huge. A salon owner gets a calendar, a register, their Google Business Profile, reviews, posts, a website, billing, a Front Desk AI — and realistically learns a small slice of it. The rest sits unused.

Command Center solves three expensive versions of that problem:

  • The learning curve. Owners don't have to memorize where every setting lives. They describe the outcome — "change my hours," "reply to that review," "how's my week looking" — and Command Center navigates and executes for them.
  • Product change. When Zoca ships new features or moves a setting, the owner doesn't get lost. The product team puts it this way: "No matter what changes are happening, what product flows are getting changed, even without them knowing end to end, they can still get the job done."
  • Time spent digging. Anything the owner would normally click through five screens to find, they can just ask for — and get back to running the floor.
VALUE LINE

"The biggest barrier to getting value out of any software is learning it. Command Center removes that barrier — if you can describe what you want, you can do it."

3. How it works

Behind the simple chat box, every Command Center request draws on three things:

PieceWhat it isScope
Knowledge baseStatic know-how about Zoca and how the product works.Global — the same for every business.
Action toolkitThe set of things it can actually do or fetch across the product.Depends on which products are enabled.
Memory / BrainThe business's own context — preferences, policies, facts.Per business. See Trust, safety & memory.

In plain terms: it knows the product (knowledge base), it can act on the product (toolkit), and it knows your business specifically (memory). The first is identical for everyone; the last is unique to each owner.

When you ask for something, Command Center figures out which area of the product your request belongs to, takes the action (or fetches the data), and replies. If the request would change something impactful, it stops and shows you a confirmation first (more on that in doc 3).

GOOD TO KNOW

The same conversation persists as the owner moves around — between pages, and between locations. They never have to "switch context" and start over. Ask about one location, then ask about another, in the same chat.

5. Where it lives + demo path

BEFORE YOU DEMO

Command Center is off by default and turned on per business. Make sure it's enabled in your demo account, and that the account is fully loaded with real-looking data (bookings, reviews, clicks) so the answers feel alive. Confirm exact button labels in your own demo account before the call — labels can vary by version.

  1. Log into the dashboard. Point out the Command Center button in the top-right — it's on every page.
  2. Mention the Cmd+K shortcut to open it instantly from anywhere.
  3. Open it, then expand to full screen so the room can read it.
  4. Start with a read — type or say: "How many website clicks have I gotten?" It pulls a real number.
  5. Follow with another read: "How is my calendar looking this week?" It summarizes the week.
  6. Now the headline moment — a write: "Change my business hours to 9 to 5, Monday to Friday." It comes back with a confirmation card showing the proposed hours, day by day.
  7. Approve it so they see the change land. Point out: "Notice it asked me first — it never changes anything important without showing me exactly what it's about to do."
  8. Optional: show it works across locations in the same chat, and that you can start a new thread without losing the first.
DEMO TIP

The single most powerful moment is the write + confirmation card. The read questions get nods; the write that previews itself and waits for approval is what makes jaws drop and lands the "powerful AND safe" story in one beat.

7. FAQ & objection handling

"Is this just a chatbot? I've seen those — they only talk." This is the opposite. A normal chatbot gives you suggestions. Command Center actually does the action — it changes your hours, reschedules the appointment, replies to the review. That's why we call it "Claude Code for salon owners."

OBJECTION

"I'll never remember the right commands." — There are no commands to remember. You talk to it like a person: "make my Tuesdays start at 10," "how busy am I this weekend." It figures out what you mean.

"What if I have more than one location?" One conversation handles all of them. You don't switch locations and start over — you can ask about one, then another, in the same chat.

"Do I have to be at my computer?" It's on every page of the web dashboard and it's also on the mobile app, so you can run things from the floor. (Voice is on the roadmap — today it's text.)

"Where do I even find it?" Top-right corner of any screen, or just hit Cmd+K. It's always one click away.

"Will it make changes I didn't mean?" No — anything impactful is previewed in a confirmation card with a before/after, and it waits for your approval. See Trust, safety & memory.

OBJECTION

"Does it actually work, or is this a beta?" — Be honest: it's live and used today, and it's expanding fast. Scheduling and Google-profile/marketing actions are the most complete; Front Desk actions are still maturing. It will occasionally falter — and your feedback is what sharpens it.

8. Key terms

  • Command Center — the in-dashboard AI assistant that performs actions in plain language.
  • Read — a request that fetches information without changing anything.
  • Write — a request that changes something; impactful writes are confirmed first.
  • Confirmation card — the before/after preview shown before an impactful change.
  • Cmd+K — the keyboard shortcut to open Command Center from any page.
  • Thread — a separate chat conversation; you can keep several going.
  • Knowledge base / toolkit / memory — the three things behind every reply: product know-how, the actions it can take, and your business's own context.
