TL;DR

Command Center can do 230+ different actions across roughly 26 areas of the business — everything from the calendar and clients to the Google profile, posts, reviews, and billing. Some actions just read information for you; others make changes. You never have to know which "area" your request belongs to — Command Center routes it to the right specialist behind the scenes. Pitch it as: "Whatever you can do in Zoca, you can ask it to do."

1. What it is

This is the breadth story. Command Center isn't a narrow helper bolted onto one feature — it spans the whole product. The internal line is: "Whatever is possible in the product is also possible through the Command Center."

SAY IT SIMPLY

"Anything you'd normally click around the dashboard to do — checking numbers, editing settings, replying to reviews, moving an appointment — you can just ask Command Center to do instead."

The headline figure to use externally is 230+ actions across ~26 operational areas. That number is real and, if anything, conservative.

GOOD TO KNOW

When speaking externally, say "230+ actions across ~26 areas." Use the "~" — the action count is the hard, verified number; the "26 areas" is the product grouping. Both are honest; just don't present "26" as an exact, fixed list on a call.

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

Breadth is what turns Command Center from a gimmick into the way the owner runs the business. If it only did three things, they'd forget about it. Because it covers nearly everything, it becomes the first place they go for anything.

  • One door to the whole product. Instead of learning a dozen sub-tools, the owner learns one habit: ask Command Center.
  • The long tail gets used. Features that owners never discover on their own — ranking insights, content queues, profile edits — become reachable just by asking.
  • Less front-desk training. New staff don't need a tour of every menu; they describe what they need.
VALUE LINE

"You paid for the whole platform. Command Center makes sure you actually use the whole platform — not just the 10% you stumbled onto."

3. How it works — it routes you to the right specialist

You type one request. Behind the scenes, Command Center decides which area of the business it belongs to and hands it to the right specialist to carry out — then comes back to you with the result. You never pick the area yourself.

In plain terms, the specialists cover four broad domains:

DomainRoughly what it handles
SchedulingCalendar, appointments, clients, services, bundles, deposits, point-of-sale.
Discovery / marketingGoogle Business Profile, posts, photos, reviews, content queue, ranking, website, domains, Instagram.
Front DeskFront Desk analytics, training (FAQs, policies, instructions), agent settings.
SupportSupport and escalation, billing, and reading/writing your business facts (the Brain).
DEMO TIP

Don't explain the routing on a sales call — it's plumbing. The selling point is the opposite: the owner doesn't think about areas at all. Mention routing only if a technical buyer asks "how does it know what I mean?"

The ~26 areas it spans include: Calendar, Clients, Services, Bundles, Inventory, POS, Business Insights, Google Profile, Posts, Photos, Content Queue, Reviews, Invites, Ranking, Website, Domains, Instagram, Front Desk, Training, Inbox, Leads, Billing, Support, Custom Requests, Memory, and Account/Trial.

4. Read vs Write — and example commands

Every action is one of two kinds:

  • READ — pulls live data and tells you, without changing anything. Safe to fire off freely.
  • WRITE — makes a change. Impactful writes are previewed in a confirmation card first (see Trust, safety & memory).

Here are real example commands by area — great for matching to the business in the room:

AreaSay something like…Read or Write
Calendar"How is my calendar looking this week?"Read
Calendar"Reschedule Friday's 2pm to 10am and text the customer."Write
Bookings"Add a pedicure to this booking and send a deposit link."Write
Services"Switch her to a gel manicure" (it asks which variant).Write
Clients"Pull up everyone who hasn't booked in 90 days."Read
Google Profile"Change my business hours to 9 to 5, Monday to Friday."Write
Posts"Post an update about my new summer color package."Write
Reviews"Reply to my latest review."Write
Reviews / Ranking"How are my reviews trending?" / "Where do I rank for my services?"Read
Website"How many website clicks have I gotten?"Read
Billing"What's my active subscription and when's my next bill?"Read
GOOD TO KNOW

When you tell Command Center to change your business hours, it can also push that change to your Google Business Profile — so the hours on Google stay in sync. That kind of "do the obvious follow-up too" behavior is exactly what lands the "it actually does the work" pitch.

5. Where it lives + demo path

BEFORE YOU DEMO

Use a demo account that's fully loaded across areas — real bookings, reviews, clicks, a Google profile — so each example returns a believable answer. Confirm labels in your own account first.

  1. Open Command Center (top-right button or Cmd+K) and expand to full screen.
  2. Fire a read the business cares about — for a marketing-minded owner: "How many website clicks have I gotten?" For an operator: "How's my calendar this week?"
  3. Show breadth by jumping areas in one chat: ask a calendar question, then a reviews question, then a billing question — without switching screens.
  4. Land a write that matches their world — "reply to my latest review," or "change my hours to 9–5 Mon–Fri" — and let the confirmation card appear.
  5. Approve it. Reinforce: "One chat just touched my calendar, my reviews, and my Google hours. That's the whole product, by voice of text."
DEMO TIP

The "jump across areas in one conversation" move is what sells breadth. Three unrelated questions, one chat box, no menu-hunting — that's the demo beat that proves "whatever you can do in Zoca, you can ask it."

7. FAQ & objection handling

"Okay, but what can it actually do — give me the list." 230+ actions across about 26 areas of the business: calendar and bookings, clients, services and bundles, point-of-sale, business insights, your Google profile, posts and photos, reviews, ranking, your website and domains, Instagram, the Front Desk, billing, support, and your saved business facts. If it's in Zoca, it's almost certainly reachable by just asking.

OBJECTION

"That sounds like a lot — surely it's spread thin?" — Be straight: the depth isn't uniform yet. Scheduling and Google-profile/marketing actions are the most complete. Front Desk actions are newer and still maturing. Everything's improving fast, and the field is the feedback loop.

"Can it do bulk things, like message a whole client segment?" It spans clients, inbox, and leads, so segment-style requests are in scope — but lead with the safe framing: anything sweeping or destructive is gated behind confirmation. Confirm the exact bulk capability in your demo account before promising it live.

"Does it cover inventory and retail?" Inventory and POS are among the areas it spans. As with everything, demo it in your own loaded account first so you're showing what's actually wired up for that account.

OBJECTION

"What if it doesn't understand my request?" — It asks. When a request is ambiguous (e.g., "switch her to gel" — which gel?), it comes back with a clarifying question rather than guessing. That's a feature, not a failure.

8. Key terms

  • Action — one thing Command Center can do; there are 230+.
  • Read action — fetches information, changes nothing.
  • Write action — makes a change; impactful ones are confirmed first.
  • Area — a domain of the business (calendar, reviews, billing…); ~26 of them.
  • Specialist / routing — behind the scenes, your request is handed to the right area's specialist. You never see it; you just ask.
  • Brain — your business's saved facts, which Command Center can read and update (see doc 3).
