TL;DR

Local SEO is the first of Discovery's three pillars: making the salon show up on Google's local results and the map when someone nearby searches. Discovery audits the Google Business Profile at onboarding (rank, completeness, reviews, competitors), picks which keywords to chase based on real demand and opportunity, and tracks rank against competitors over time. Pitch it as: "Get found by the people who are already searching for you down the street."

1. What it is

Local SEO is about being found in local and map results — the "salons near me" moment. It's distinct from website SEO (ranking your own pages) and AI search (getting recommended by AI engines), and Discovery treats it as its own pillar.

It has three moving parts:

  • Demand — figuring out what people in the area are actually searching for, and how much.
  • Keywords — choosing which services and search terms to push (by opportunity, not a flat list), then tracking where the business ranks for them.
  • Competition — seeing who currently outranks the salon and by how much, so the gap is clear.

The whole thing is anchored on the Google Business Profile — the salon's Google listing — which is the backbone of local visibility.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"When someone in your neighborhood pulls out their phone and types 'gel manicure near me,' local SEO is what decides whether your salon is the one they see — or the one three blocks away."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

For a local service business, local search is the lead source. A few realities make this pillar matter:

  • Most beauty bookings start local. People search by neighborhood and by service. If the salon isn't in the local pack and on the map, it simply isn't in the running for those searches.
  • Owners can't see where they stand. Most have no idea what their Google rank is, how complete their profile is, or how far behind their competitors they are. The onboarding audit makes it concrete — often uncomfortably so, which is exactly what motivates the sale.
  • The competition gap is real and visible. In the demo data, the audited profile scored a 42 overall while one competitor had over 1,300 reviews and others had 200+. That gap is the work local SEO is built to close.
VALUE LINE

"You're already great at the haircut. The problem isn't your work — it's that the customer searching right now can't find you. That's what local SEO fixes."

3. How it works

The onboarding audit (honest by design)

At signup, Discovery runs a local / Google Business Profile audit: it searches the profile and reports rank for the primary category, profile completeness, reviews, and rating — scored high / medium / low — plus the competitor landscape.

The audit is deliberately honest. It is not nudged toward a flattering score, and it isn't a lead-prediction number. It shows the actual state and the work to be done. In the demo, the profile scored a 42 — "bad, and I'm hoping Discovery can fix that." That candor is a selling point, not a weakness.

GOOD TO KNOW

There are three audits in the broader Discovery story — this local/GBP audit, an AI-search audit, and a website (traditional SEO) audit. The local one is the one you'll most often demo first because it's the most familiar to owners.

Keyword selection by demand and opportunity

This is the part prospects most often probe, so get it right: Discovery does not chase a fixed, flat list of a handful of keywords. It looks at the salon's full service catalog, measures demand (what's actually being searched and how much), and qualifies services by opportunity — where there's the best chance to win. Then it pushes those.

VALUE LINE

"We qualify your services by the higher-opportunity ones — so we can optimize twenty services at once, not just four. That's where the 'ten times the output' comes from."

Primary category is chosen by impressions, not by assumption

A Google Business Profile can exist without an address, but not without a primary category — and that category is one of the things that influences what the business ranks on. The mistake reps make is assuming the category. A salon that does both hair and nails might seem like an obvious "hair salon," but the right call depends on the data: what impressions is it already getting, what services have more demand, and where is the competition lighter?

DON'T ASSUME

A hair-and-nails salon might rank best as a hair salon — but only because that's where its impressions, demand, and competition happen to favor it. You can't know that until you check. The numbers might just as easily point to a mani-pedi combo (a $200 ticket) over a $50 haircut. Pick the primary category from impressions, demand, and competition — never from a gut guess about what the business "is."

Relevant vs irrelevant keywords — rank on the right searches

Ranking isn't just about ranking — it's about ranking on keywords that are actually relevant to what the salon offers. Searches signal demand, but chasing demand for a service you don't even provide is wasted energy.

EXAMPLE

A coaching session looked up "salmon-sperm men's facial" — a real, in-demand service — and found a salon, Bodycraft, that did not offer it. There's clear demand there, but it would make no sense for Bodycraft to spend energy ranking on it: that traffic is irrelevant to them. Their energy belongs on services they actually provide (say, acne treatments). Relevant keywords = the right searches for this business; irrelevant keywords = real demand the business can't fulfill. Rank on the relevant ones.

Branded vs non-branded keywords

  • Branded keywords contain the business (or chain) name — "VLCC near me," "Green Trends near me." Search these and you mostly see that brand's locations, with rivals sometimes buying sponsored ads against the branded term.
  • Non-branded keywords describe the service, not the brand — "hair salon near me," "best facials near me." This is where a salon competes for new customers who don't yet know its name, and it's where the real discovery battle is won.

The 5-step keyword pipeline

Keyword selection isn't a flat list — it runs through five steps, in order:

  1. Searches — what terms people are actually typing.
  2. Demand — how much each term is searched (number of searches = demand for that service).
  3. Relevance — filter to the keywords that match the salon's intent and services (drop the irrelevant traffic).
  4. Competitor — weigh how hard each term is to win given who else is ranking.
  5. Selection — choose the right keywords to focus on.
GOOD TO KNOW

No human can hold all this — every search term, the demand behind it, relevance, and the competition — in their head and act on it consistently. That's the case for Discovery doing the keyword work at scale; the five steps are the strategy behind which searches the agent chases.

Local vs hyperlocal

  • Local — showing up for searches tied to your city or area generally ("hair salon Austin").
  • Hyperlocal — the "near me" layer: searches tied to the searcher's exact spot, where being on the map for a specific neighborhood or block wins the click.

Discovery works both, which matters because so much beauty search is "near me" on a phone.

Rank tracking over time

Once keywords are chosen, Discovery tracks rank across geographic areas on a rank map (the local-SEO "grid") so progress is visible over time, and runs competitor comparison so the owner can see who they're catching.

HONESTY NOTE

Google takes time to reflect changes — local improvements don't show up overnight. Set the expectation that local SEO is a compounding program over the 12-month plan, not a switch you flip.

ON THE SCREEN

The Keywords tab has four sub-views worth knowing by name: By Keyword (the flat ranked table — KPIs Tracked / Top 20 / Improved / Local Pack; each keyword paired with the service it maps to, sorted by a Revenue column = monthly searches × cost-per-click × estimated conversion, framed as opportunity sizing); By Service (the same keywords grouped into High / Medium / Low demand tiers); Service detail (one service group, with a revenue range rather than a single number); and Map View (a 7×7 geo-grid over ~3 miles showing your rank at each point plus the top competitors — dense scans are limited to ~3/day because each one is 49 live Google queries). The Revenue column is the number that lands; "#20+" in red is the opportunity colour, not a failure. Full walkthrough in the field guide.

7. FAQ & objection handling

"How are these keywords selected?"

OBJECTION

We don't use a fixed list of a few keywords. We look at your whole service catalog, measure real search demand, and qualify your services by the higher-opportunity ones — the searches where you have the best chance to win. That's why we can optimize twenty services at once instead of four.

"I already rank fine on Google." Maybe for your name — but do you rank for the services people search before they know your name? The audit will show you exactly where you stand for the searches that bring in new customers, not just the ones from people who already know you.

"How long until I see results?" Local SEO compounds. Google takes time to reflect changes — think weeks, not days, and real momentum over the year. That's why it's a 12-month program, not a one-time fix.

"Why is my competitor ahead of me?" Usually reviews and a more complete, more active profile. The audit shows the gap, and Discovery works it down — keeping your profile fresh, growing reviews, and chasing the right keywords.
