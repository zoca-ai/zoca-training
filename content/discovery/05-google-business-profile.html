TL;DR

The Google Business Profile (GBP) is the salon's Google listing — the map pin, hours, photos, reviews, and contact info — and it's the backbone of everything local. Discovery audits it honestly at onboarding, optimizes the profile fields, posts to it daily, manages photos, and replies to reviews with AI drafts, while surfacing insights. One hard rule: the profile must be verified, so qualify that early. Pitch it as: "Your Google listing, audited, optimized, and working 24/7."

1. What it is

When someone searches a salon's name or "salon near me," the box that pops up on the right with the map, photos, hours, and reviews is the Google Business Profile. It's the single most important asset in local search — and the one owners most often neglect.

Discovery connects to it, runs an honest audit, and then keeps it optimized and fresh: profile fields filled in correctly, photos managed, posts published, and reviews answered. It surfaces the metrics — impressions, clicks, calls — so the owner can see it working.

SAY IT SIMPLY

"That little Google box with your map, hours, photos, and reviews? That's your storefront for everyone searching online. Most owners set it up once and forget it. We treat it like the front door it is — and keep it polished every day."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

  • It's the highest-leverage thing in local search. A complete, active, well-reviewed profile shows up higher and converts better than a neglected one — and the gap between the two is enormous.
  • Reviews are make-or-break, and replying matters. Profiles with more reviews and active replies win trust and rank. Most owners don't reply to reviews at all. AI-drafted replies fix that without adding work.
  • Owners are flying blind. They have no idea how many impressions, clicks, or calls their listing drives. The insights make it concrete.
VALUE LINE

"Your Google listing is doing the work of a 24/7 salesperson — or it's sitting there half-empty, sending customers to your competitor. We make sure it's the first one."

3. How it works

Connection and the verification gate

At onboarding, Discovery searches the profile, connects to it through Google, and audits it. Connecting requires the owner to grant Google permissions to manage their business listing.

OBJECTION

GBP verification is a hard dependency — qualify it early. An unverified profile breaks the overview screen and disables features ("if it's unverified, certain features aren't live"). GBP creation and verification is a known pain point. Surface it in the first conversation: "Is your Google Business Profile claimed and verified?" If not, that's a step you'll need to work through before parts of Discovery light up — don't discover it on the day of go-live.

The audit / score

The onboarding audit shows clicks, rank for the primary category, a profile-completeness breakdown, contact and access details, impressions and keywords, and the competitor landscape — scored high / medium / low. In the demo, the profile scored a 42 overall ("bad"). The audit is honest — it isn't nudged toward a flattering number; it shows the real state and the work to be done.

On top of that, daily Google posts keep the listing active (these run through the content engine — see Website & content).

Review management — how reviews get collected and gated

Reviews are customer feedback, and they're what build the trust and reputation Google rewards. Discovery collects them two ways, and gates them so only strong reviews go public:

  • QR code at the salon — a code displayed in-store that a customer scans to leave a review on the spot.
  • Automated post-service invites — after a service, an invite goes to the customer asking for a rating.
HOW REVIEWS ARE GATED

Anything under 4 stars is held internally for the owner — it doesn't auto-publish, so the owner can address the unhappy customer privately. 5-star (and strong) reviews get published out to the website and the Google Business Profile. The result: public-facing review presence stays strong, and the owner still hears the critical feedback. (Pair this with the AI-drafted replies — collect, gate, then respond, all without the owner chasing it.)

GBP is now a content platform, not just a ranking platform

REFRAME

Stop thinking of the Google profile as only a ranking lever. It's now a content platform: the posts, the photos, the reviews, and the linked landing page are all content that the AI engines read to decide what to say about the business. So GBP work isn't just "rank higher on the map" — it's feeding the inputs that drive AI recommendations too. One observed nuance worth knowing: the website link on the GBP appears to carry more content weight than other links to the same site found elsewhere online — so the landing page linked from the profile, and the content on it, is one of the highest-priority things an AI picks up. (Framed as a field observation, not a Google-published rule.)

The reflection lag

HONESTY NOTE

Google takes time to reflect changes — edits to the profile "take time to show on Google." Don't promise instant changes. When you optimize a listing or fix the fields, tell the owner it'll catch up over the coming days, not seconds.

Command Center can edit it (with confirmation)

If the customer also uses Command Center, it can read GBP status and make profile edits — but every write requires a confirmation card, because some changes (like the website link on the profile) are consequential. You don't need this for a Discovery-only sale, but it's good to know.

Publishing the Front Desk number

GOOD TO KNOW

For customers who also have the Front Desk product, the Front Desk phone number can be published to the Google Business Profile at go-live, so calls from the listing route into the AI front desk. It's a clean cross-sell hook — the marketing (Discovery) drives the call, and the front desk (a separate product) answers it. See Front Desk.

ON THE SCREEN

Reviews is its own tab — one screen to get reviews (an auto-imported list of clients who visited in the last 7 days, each with an Ask for Review button that texts + emails them) and respond to them (AI-drafted replies shown inline; 4–5-star replies auto-publish, 1–3-star are drafted and held for the owner). The Google Profile tab then has four sub-tabs + three edit pages: Insights (defaults to a 3-month window because GBP compounds slowly), Manage Profile (the Verified chip and a completeness % out of 100; syncs with Google every ~6h), Posts (~12/month, short and hashtag-heavy because Google truncates them), Photos (three slots Google scores you on — Logo, Cover, Exterior), and the About / Hours / Location edit pages — where the primary category is the single biggest ranking signal, hours accuracy is the top trust signal, and service areas are the cheapest local-SEO lift. Full walkthrough in the field guide.

7. FAQ & objection handling

"My Google listing is fine." Maybe — let's find out with the audit. Most owners are surprised. And "fine" today isn't "active" — daily posts, fresh photos, and answered reviews are what actually move you up and build trust, and that's the part that almost never happens on its own.

"I don't have time to reply to reviews." That's exactly why the replies are AI-drafted in your voice. You approve them in seconds instead of writing them. Active review replies are a real ranking and trust signal — this gets you the benefit without the chore.

"My profile isn't verified / I never claimed it."

OBJECTION

Good to know now rather than later — verification is a real dependency. Several features only switch on once Google has verified the listing, and verification can take some back-and-forth with Google. Let's get that started early so everything's live when your trial begins.

"I made a change but nothing happened on Google." Google reflects changes on its own schedule — edits take time to appear, usually over the following days. It's working; Google just isn't instant.
