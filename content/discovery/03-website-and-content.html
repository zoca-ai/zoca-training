3. How it works

Generation

The website is built from services scraped from the salon's booking link plus the Google Business Profile, with AI filling the gaps. It creates a page for each service, an About section, and a team section.

Before the build, there's a "review services" step where the owner confirms their services and prices are correct. Price is sensitive — owners care a lot about it being right — so this validation happens up front.

Preview, publish, and the two CTAs

  • The owner gets a preview with desktop and mobile toggles.
  • Two paths: "Looks good" to publish, or "I want to customize" to flag what they'd like changed (then they use the editor in the dashboard).
  • The website goes live when the trial starts — not the instant it's generated.
  • Every site carries two calls to action: Call and Book — so a visitor can either ring the salon or book directly.
HONESTY NOTE

The website goes live when the trial starts, not the moment it's generated in the demo. Don't tell a prospect "your site is live right now" mid-demo — say "this is your site, and it goes live the day your trial begins."

Auto-rebuild on service change

When the salon's services change, the site rebuilds to match (a "website flip"). The owner never has to manually update pages when they add or drop a service — the site stays in sync with the catalog.

The editor

The in-dashboard editor lets the owner customize pages, sections, fonts, colors, the footer, the Call/Book buttons, structured data, domains, and forms. So it's auto-generated and fully customizable — the owner isn't locked into a template.

The content engine

  • Blogs — published on a schedule, ~2 per week by default (configurable); higher tiers can do more. They're formatted as questions and answers, with built-in FAQs, which is exactly the format that gets picked up for specific searches and AI answers.
  • Google posts — published regularly to keep the Google listing active and fresh.
GOOD TO KNOW

The Q&A/FAQ blog format isn't a style choice — it's strategic. Content written as "question → answer" is what both Google and AI engines pull from when someone asks a specific question. The same blogs that help website SEO also feed AI search (see AI search).

Context is what makes content non-generic (and AI-recommendable)

Content alone is table stakes — what separates a salon from the wax place down the road is context. The Brain stores the business-specific nuance that most owners keep only in their heads: "our wax is homemade," "it's low-pain," "it doesn't cause rashes," "we only do female bikini wax," "our Sunday agent's name is Zoe." That nuance is gold for content.

WHY IT MATTERS

When a customer asks ChatGPT, "I want a wax but I want it painless — who's good at that?", the engine can only recommend a salon if that detail exists in the salon's content somewhere online. Context fed by the Brain is what turns a generic "we offer waxing" page into "homemade, low-pain, no-rashes waxing" — and that's exactly the kind of specific, grounded content an AI matches against and recommends. Without context, the salon is just another generic blob of text on the net. (See Brain.)

Every line online matters — and it must be cohesive

GOOD TO KNOW

We don't have full visibility into how each engine weights each source — ChatGPT has pulled from Facebook, then Yelp, now Google directly — so the safe assumption is that every piece of content written online about the business matters. That makes cohesiveness across platforms essential: the website, the blogs, the Google posts, the listings should all say the same thing, in the same voice, positioning the business the same way. Scattered or contradictory content across platforms dilutes the signal; a cohesive story reinforces it everywhere an engine looks.

HONESTY NOTE

AI-written content isn't penalized just for being AI-written — generic content is. Search and AI engines don't distinguish human-vs-AI; they penalize thin, duplicate, context-free filler. AI content that's been written properly, is specific to the salon's services, and is grounded in the business's real facts (via the Brain) is the opposite of that. The grounding is what makes it rank and get recommended — so "AI-written" is fine; "generic" is the problem.

SEO assets (the plumbing)

Every generated site ships the technical SEO essentials, in plain terms:

  • A sitemap — a map of all the pages so search engines find everything.
  • Crawl instructions — telling search engines what to read.
  • Structured data — labels that help engines understand what each page is about.
  • An AI-readable summary file — a newer file specifically meant to help AI assistants understand the site (Zoca has shipped it on its sites for a while; honestly, AI engines still under-use it today, so it's a forward-looking nicety, not a magic bullet).
