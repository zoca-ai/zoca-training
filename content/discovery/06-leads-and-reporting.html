TL;DR

Discovery surfaces an enquiry/lead pipeline — each enquiry tagged Booked, Not booked, Unresponsive, or Duplicate — and it's also where abandoned leads from the Front Desk product land. But the most important thing in this doc isn't a feature; it's a reframe: don't sell a lead-prediction number. Sell the visible marketing work that produces leads. The audits and dashboards exist to show the work being done — honestly — not to promise a magic lead count. Pitch it as: "You can finally see the work — and the work is what gets you leads."

1. What it is

Two things, one philosophy.

The leads surface is a pipeline of enquiries the business has received, each with a status so the owner can see what's happening:

StatusWhat it means
BookedThe enquiry turned into a booking.
Not bookedA genuine enquiry that didn't convert (yet).
UnresponsiveThe person went quiet — no reply.
DuplicateA repeat of an enquiry already captured.

The reporting / impact framing is everything Discovery uses to show its work — the onboarding audits, the rank tracking, the content queue, the website, the AI-search dashboard. Taken together they answer the owner's real question: "what am I paying for, and is it doing anything?"

SAY IT SIMPLY

"Here are the people who reached out, and what happened to each — booked, didn't book, went quiet. And here's all the marketing work running for you behind it, so you can actually see what you're paying for."

2. Why it matters (the salon owner's view)

Owners have been burned by marketing before — usually by someone who promised "X leads a month," took their money, and delivered a vague report and a black box. So two things matter to them:

  • Trust through visibility. When the owner can see the audits, the pages, the posts, the rankings, and the enquiries — the actual work — they trust it. A number they can't verify does the opposite.
  • Knowing where enquiries stand. Seeing booked vs. not-booked vs. unresponsive tells the owner where attention is needed and proves the marketing is generating real interest.
VALUE LINE

"The reason marketing feels like a gamble is that you can never see what you're getting. Discovery flips that — every job it does is on the dashboard. You're buying visible work, not a promise."

3. How it works

The leads pipeline

Enquiries flow into the leads surface and get the status that fits — Booked, Not booked, Unresponsive, or Duplicate. The owner gets one place to see who reached out and what came of it, instead of scattered DMs, texts, and missed calls.

Where abandoned leads land

The Front Desk product (Zoca's conversational AI) has a lead-to-booking flow: when someone starts a conversation or a booking but drops off before finishing, that abandoned lead is captured rather than lost — and it surfaces here in the Discovery leads view. So a customer who has both products gets a single place where marketing-driven enquiries and recovered abandoned-booking leads come together.

GOOD TO KNOW

You don't need the Front Desk product for the leads surface to be useful — it works for Discovery on its own. But it's a clean cross-sell: "Discovery brings them to your door; Front Desk catches the ones who almost booked and trailed off." See Front Desk.

Reporting that shows the work

Discovery's reporting is built around showing the work, not predicting an outcome:

  • The onboarding audits (local/GBP, AI search, and website) show where the business stands and the gap to close — honestly scored, never nudged toward a flattering or alarming number.
  • The dashboards show the ongoing work: pages live, content published, posts going out, rank moving, AI-search mentions and citations.

This is deliberate. The product's own positioning is explicit about it.

4. The big reframe (the most important page in this set)

VALUE LINE

"We made a mistake selling leads as an outcome — nobody ends up selling just leads. You have to sell the work that gets you leads. And now that work is visible."

This is the heart of how to sell Discovery. Internalize it:

  • Don't promise a lead count. "We'll get you 30 leads a month" is the trap that has burned every owner you'll talk to, and it's not how Discovery is positioned.
  • Sell the comprehensive work — the website management, the content, the rank and visibility, the AI-search presence — bundled into one solution. That's the value.
  • "Buy the product, not us." The owner isn't hiring a person or a vague service; they're buying a product that does a stack of marketing jobs that would otherwise cost thousands a month.
  • Lead with the audit, not a forecast. Show where they stand and the work that closes the gap — not a lead-prediction number.

5. Where it lives + demo path

  1. Open the leads / enquiries surface in the Discovery dashboard — show the statuses (Booked, Not booked, Unresponsive, Duplicate).
  2. If the prospect is also looking at Front Desk, point out that abandoned booking leads land here too.
  3. Pivot to the work: the audits and dashboards. Frame them as "this is what you're actually buying — visible, ongoing marketing."
ON THE SCREEN

The Discovery Overview tab is the "morning coffee" screen — four KPI cards (Leads, Revenue attributed via the booking-system sync, Reviews consistency — the % of weeks with at least one new review, because Google rewards velocity not a lifetime total, and Additional business — directions + calls from the Google listing, i.e. people who chose them without ever visiting the website) and one plain-English coaching tip keyed to the weakest number. It deliberately has no charts and no buttons — it orients; the deep tabs are where you act. Note for week-1 demos: zero leads is expected early — numbers move in weeks 2–4. Full walkthrough in the field guide.

DEMO TIP

Spend more demo time on the work (audits, content queue, rankings, AI search) than on the leads list itself. The leads list proves interest; the work is the product. And never let the conversation settle on "how many leads" — keep steering back to visible work.

7. FAQ & objection handling

"How many leads will I get?"

OBJECTION

I won't give you a number, and I'd be skeptical of anyone who does — leads depend on your market, your services, and timing, and pretending otherwise is how owners get burned. What I will do is show you every piece of work we do and exactly where you stand, all visible on your dashboard. That work is what produces leads, and you'll see all of it.

"My last marketing company promised leads and delivered nothing."

OBJECTION

That's exactly the trap we don't fall into. We don't sell a lead number — we sell the actual marketing work, and we put all of it on a dashboard you can check any time. Audits, pages, posts, rankings, AI-search visibility. You're never staring at a black box wondering what you paid for.

"What's in the leads list — where do these come from?" Enquiries the business receives, tagged by what happened — booked, not booked, unresponsive, or duplicate. And if you also use our front desk, the people who started a booking and dropped off get captured here too, so they're not lost.

"Can you predict my ROI?" I can show you where you stand today and the work that closes the gap — honestly. I won't fabricate an ROI number. Marketing compounds over the year; the audits and dashboards let you watch it happen rather than take it on faith.
