# SEO & Content 101 — how search ranking really works

> [!TL;DR] SEO is making a website easy for search engines to find, understand, and trust so it shows up when people search. Google works in three stages — **crawl** (discover pages), **index** (understand and store them), **rank** (order them for a query). Ranking rests on three pillars: **on-page** (titles, headings, useful content matched to what people search), **technical** (sitemap, mobile, speed, structured data), and **off-page** (links and mentions that signal authority). Google rewards real **Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness (E-E-A-T)** and demotes thin, generic pages. For a salon: a clear page per service plus honest answers to the questions clients actually ask. SEO compounds but is slow — expect 4–12 months. AI-written content is _not_ penalized for being AI-written; it's penalized for being thin and unhelpful.

## 1. What SEO actually is

SEO — search engine optimization — is the work of helping a website earn **free ("organic")** placement in search results, as opposed to paid ads. When a salon owner says "I want to show up on Google," that's SEO.

> [!WHY IT MATTERS]
> Most people looking for a salon, a balayage specialist, or "gel manicure near me" start on a search engine. If a business isn't visible there, it's invisible to a huge share of new clients — no matter how good the work is.
>
> SEO is one of the few marketing channels where the traffic is _intent-driven_: the person is already looking for what the business offers. That makes it some of the highest-quality lead flow a small business can get.

This primer covers **general organic search and content**. Two sibling topics live in their own primers: **Local SEO** (Google Business Profile, maps, "near me") and **AEO/GEO** (showing up in AI answers and assistants). We'll reference them but won't repeat them here.

## 2. The pipeline: crawl → index → rank

Google is a fully automated system. Per Google's own documentation, it works in three stages:

| Stage            | What happens                                                                                    | Plain-English version                                           |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Crawl**        | Software "crawlers" (bots) discover pages by following links and reading submitted sitemaps.    | Google sends robots out to _find_ your pages.                   |
| **Index**        | Google analyzes each page's text, titles, images, and tags, then stores it in a giant database. | Google _reads and files_ your page so it knows what it's about. |
| **Rank (serve)** | For each query, Google searches its index and orders results using hundreds of signals.         | Google _decides who shows up first_ for a given search.         |

> [!HOW IT WORKS]
> A page can't rank if it was never indexed, and it can't be indexed if it was never crawled. So problems happen at different stages: a brand-new page nobody links to may never get _crawled_; a page Google reads but finds duplicative may get _indexed but never ranked_.
>
> This is why "technical health" matters before content cleverness — if the robots can't reach or understand the page, nothing downstream helps.

## 3. The three pillars of SEO

Almost everything in SEO sorts into three buckets. A strong strategy needs all three.

| Pillar        | What it covers                                                                                                                                     | Salon-relevant examples                                                                                    |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **On-page**   | Content and the words on each page: titles, headings (H1/H2), body copy, keyword/intent match, internal links.                                     | A dedicated "Balayage" page with a clear title, a description of the service, pricing context, and photos. |
| **Technical** | How the site is built so search engines can crawl and read it: XML sitemap, robots rules, mobile-friendliness, page speed, HTTPS, structured data. | The booking site loads fast on a phone and submits a sitemap so every service page gets found.             |
| **Off-page**  | Signals from _outside_ the site that build authority: links from other sites, and brand mentions.                                                  | A local "best salons" blog links to the shop; a press mention names the business.                          |

> [!KEY TERM] > **On-page SEO** = what's _on_ your page (you fully control it). **Technical SEO** = how the _site_ is built and served. **Off-page SEO** = reputation signals you _earn_ elsewhere. Per current 2025 guidance from Moz/Ahrefs-style sources, off-page is now about _quality and relevance_ of links, not raw quantity.

## 4. On-page: write for people _and_ the search

The heart of on-page SEO is **matching content to what people search and what they want**. That starts with keywords and _search intent_.

A **keyword** is simply the phrase a person types. But the same words can mean different things, so Google groups searches by **intent**. There are four classic types:

| Intent            | The searcher wants to…      | Typical signals            | Salon example                    |
| ----------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| **Informational** | Learn something             | "how," "what," "why"       | "how long does balayage last"    |
| **Navigational**  | Reach a specific site/brand | a brand or business name   | "Glow Salon Austin"              |
| **Commercial**    | Compare before buying       | "best," "vs," "reviews"    | "best keratin treatment near me" |
| **Transactional** | Act now                     | "book," "price," "near me" | "book gel manicure downtown"     |

> [!SALON EXAMPLE]
> A page titled "Our Services" that lumps everything together competes for _nothing_ clearly. Split it: a **page per service** — Balayage, Brazilian Blowout, Bridal Hair — each answering the questions a person searching that service has. A separate **FAQ / Q&A** section ("Does balayage damage hair?", "How much is a full set?") captures informational searches that lead to bookings.

A page-per-service approach works because each page can target one clear intent, earn its own ranking, and link to the booking flow. (Note: Google announced FAQ _rich results_ are being dropped in 2026 — but the _content_ still ranks and still answers real questions, so keep writing Q&A; just don't rely on the special FAQ snippet appearance.)

## 5. Technical SEO and structured data

Technical SEO makes the site readable by machines. The essentials: a submitted **XML sitemap**, sensible **robots** rules, **mobile-friendliness**, fast **page speed**, secure **HTTPS**, and **structured data**.

> [!KEY TERM] > **Structured data (schema markup)** is a standardized code format (Google recommends JSON-LD) that labels page content — "this is a business, these are its hours, this is a service, this is its price" — so search engines understand it precisely instead of guessing. The most relevant types for salons are **LocalBusiness** (covered in the Local SEO primer) and service/price markup.

## 6. E-E-A-T: why genuine expertise and trust win

Google's quality guidelines center on **E-E-A-T**: **Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness.** The "extra E" — Experience — was added in 2022 to reward content from someone who has _actually done the thing_.

> [!HOW IT WORKS]
> E-E-A-T isn't a single dial Google turns; it's a lens its human "quality raters" use to judge whether the algorithm is surfacing good results. Their data doesn't directly set rankings, but it trains and validates the systems that do. Trust is described as the most important member of the family.

> [!SALON EXAMPLE]
> A stylist with 12 years of color experience writing about "how to keep balayage from going brassy" — with real before/after photos, named credentials, and an honest tone — radiates Experience and Trust. A generic 300-word page that could describe any salon anywhere does not.

## 7. Content marketing: why consistency compounds

Content marketing is publishing genuinely useful content (service pages, guides, FAQs, posts) over time. Google's **helpful content** guidance frames it as **Who, How, Why**: who made it, how (including any AI use), and _why_ — and the "why" should be to help people, not just to rank.

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION]
> "AI-written content gets penalized." **False.** Google has stated it's not about whether content is made by a human or AI — it's about quality, originality, and value. The actual risk is **thin, generic, mass-produced ("scaled") content with little added value**, which raters are told to rate lowest. AI used to help a knowledgeable salon owner say something real and specific is fine; AI used to spray out filler is not.

> [!RULE OF THUMB]
> Consistency beats bursts. One genuinely useful page or post every week or two, over months, compounds: each indexed page is another doorway into the site and another chance to match a real search. Ten strong pages built over a quarter outperform fifty thin ones dumped in a weekend.

## 8. Realistic timelines

> [!FOR THE AE]
> SEO is **slow, then compounding**. Multiple 2025 sources — and Google's own experts — cite a **4–12 month** window for meaningful results. Technical fixes can show early wins in weeks; a brand-new site may need 8–12 months to build authority.
>
> Set this expectation up front with salon owners. Position SEO and content as a **durable asset that compounds**, not a faucet you turn on for instant bookings. If a prospect needs clients _this week_, that's a paid-ads/Local conversation; SEO is the long game that lowers their cost of getting found over time. Under-promising the timeline and over-delivering on the compounding payoff is the honest, winning pitch.

## Key terms

- **SEO (search engine optimization):** earning free/organic placement in search results.
- **Organic vs. paid:** organic = unpaid rankings; paid = ads.
- **Crawl / index / rank:** discover pages → understand & store them → order them for a query.
- **Keyword:** the phrase a searcher types.
- **Search intent:** what the searcher actually wants — informational, navigational, commercial, or transactional.
- **On-page / technical / off-page:** the three pillars — content, site-readability, and earned authority.
- **Structured data (schema):** code (usually JSON-LD) that labels page content for machines.
- **E-E-A-T:** Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness.
- **Helpful / people-first content:** Google's standard — content made primarily to help people.
- **Backlink:** a link from another site, treated as a vote of authority.
