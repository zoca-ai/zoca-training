# AEO & GEO 101 — getting recommended by AI search

> [!TL;DR] AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) and GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) are the practice of getting a business mentioned, recommended, and cited inside AI-generated answers — like ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, and Gemini — instead of chasing a blue-link ranking. The big mindset shift: there is no "position #1" to win. AI engines synthesize one answer from many trusted sources, so the goal is to be one of the sources they pull from. You earn that with clear, factual, structured content and a consistent reputation across the web (your site, reviews, directories, social). It's new, measured differently than SEO, and the results are still uncertain — but it matters because a growing share of searches now end without anyone clicking a website at all.

## 1. What changed: search became "answers"

For 20 years, search worked one way: type a question, get a list of links, click one. Businesses competed to rank near the top of that list. That's classic **SEO** (Search Engine Optimization).

That model is shrinking fast. When someone now asks Google, ChatGPT, or Perplexity a question, they increasingly get a written answer right on the screen — and never click through to any website. These are called **zero-click searches**.

> [!WHY IT MATTERS] If a salon owner's future customer asks an AI assistant a question and gets a complete answer without visiting a single website, then the only thing that matters is _whether the business showed up inside that answer_. Ranking #4 on a link list nobody reads is worthless. Being named in the AI's answer is everything.

A randomized field study by researchers at the Indian School of Business and Carnegie Mellon found Google's AI Overviews cut outbound organic clicks by **38%** on the queries where they appeared, and pushed zero-click searches from 54% to 72%. Semrush data showed AI Overviews appearing on **6.49% of queries in January 2025**, more than doubling to **13.14% by March 2025**. The direction is clear even if the exact numbers keep moving.

## 2. AEO vs GEO vs SEO — what the terms mean

These three acronyms get used loosely. Here's the honest version:

- **SEO** — optimizing to rank in the traditional link list.
- **AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)** — optimizing to _be the answer_: featured snippets, "people also ask," and AI summaries that directly answer a question.
- **GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)** — optimizing to be _cited and recommended by generative AI_ (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity) when it writes an answer.

> [!KEY TERM] In practice AEO and GEO overlap heavily, and many practitioners treat them as the same discipline under different names. You don't need to police the difference. The shared goal is simple: **show up inside AI-generated answers.** When talking to a salon owner, use whichever term lands — the concept is what counts.

## 3. How AI engines actually build an answer

Most AI answer engines use **retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)**: the engine first _retrieves_ relevant web pages, then the language model _generates_ a written answer grounded in those pages, often with citations linking back.

> [!HOW IT WORKS] Google calls part of this "query fan-out." One question — say, "best painless waxing salon near me" — gets split into several sub-searches behind the scenes: "Brazilian wax pain comparison," "hard wax vs soft wax," "top-rated waxing salons [city]," "wax allergy safety." The engine pulls sources for each, then weaves one answer. Your business has many small doors to be pulled through, not one big front door.

Engines lean on a few signals to decide what to trust: clearly stated, **specific facts**; **structure** (headings, lists, Q&A); **freshness**; **entity understanding** (does the engine recognize this business as a real, distinct thing?); and **corroboration** — does the same information show up consistently across multiple independent sources?

Different engines behave differently. Industry analyses report ChatGPT leans on consensus sources like Wikipedia and review/comparison sites; Perplexity leans heavily on Reddit and real-time sources; Google AI Overviews still overlap meaningfully with traditional organic rankings. One analysis of citations found only ~11% domain overlap between ChatGPT and Perplexity — meaning **you cannot treat "AI search" as one single thing to optimize for.**

## 4. The big reframe: there is no "position"

This is the single most important idea to internalize.

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] "We just need to rank #1 in ChatGPT."
>
> There is no #1. The AI writes _one_ answer and may name several businesses, cite a handful of sources, or recommend just one — and it can give a different customer a slightly different answer to the same question. You're not climbing a ranked ladder. You're trying to be _included in the answer_ and, ideally, _recommended favorably_.

Think of the AI like a knowledgeable friend giving a recommendation. You don't "rank" in your friend's brain. You become someone they bring up because they've heard good, consistent things about you from many places. That's the mental model.

## 5. What content earns citations

The academic anchor here is the Princeton-led **"GEO: Generative Engine Optimization"** paper (KDD 2024), which built a 10,000-query benchmark and tested which content changes increased AI visibility. Its headline findings:

- Adding **specific statistics** (numbers, percentages, dates, dollar amounts) boosted visibility up to **+40%**.
- Adding **credible source citations** and **direct quotations** from authorities also lifted visibility — with lower-ranked pages (around position 5) benefiting most (a reported ~115% relative lift), while already-top pages changed little.

Translated for a salon, the content that earns AI citations is:

- **Direct and factual** — "A Brazilian wax with hard wax typically takes 20–30 minutes and uses a low-temperature wax that adheres to hair, not skin."
- **Structured as Q&A / FAQ** — questions phrased the way real customers ask them, with clear answers underneath.
- **Specific** — prices, durations, what's included, aftercare, who it's for.
- **Consistent everywhere** — the same hours, services, and positioning on the website, Google Business Profile, Yelp, Instagram, and booking page.

> [!SALON EXAMPLE] A customer asks an AI: _"Who does painless Brazilian waxing near me?"_ The engine fans out, retrieves local results, reviews, and pages describing waxing techniques, then writes: _"For a gentler Brazilian wax, look for salons using hard wax and offering numbing options. Highly reviewed options near you include..."_ To be in that answer, the salon needs a page that explicitly says it uses hard wax and offers numbing — plus reviews and a Google Business Profile that confirm it. Vague "we offer waxing" pages get skipped.

## 6. Reviews and third-party mentions do heavy lifting

AI engines build confidence through **corroboration** — agreement across independent sources. A salon's own website is one voice. The engine trusts it far more when Google reviews, Yelp, local directories, Instagram, and maybe a local blog all say similar things.

> [!RULE OF THUMB] One thing said in five places beats five things said in one place. Encourage owners to keep their listings, reviews, and social presence consistent and current — that consistency is what makes an AI confident enough to recommend them by name.

## 7. How you'd even measure it

You can't check a "rank report." Instead, teams track **AI Share of Voice**: across a set of representative customer questions, how often does the business get mentioned or cited versus competitors?

|                  | Classic SEO                         | AEO / GEO                                                      |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Goal             | Rank high in the link list          | Be mentioned/cited in the AI's answer                          |
| Unit of success  | A ranking position (#1–#10)         | A mention, recommendation, or citation                         |
| What wins        | Keywords, backlinks, page authority | Clear facts, structure, entity trust, consensus across the web |
| Result delivered | A list of links to click            | One synthesized answer, often zero-click                       |
| Measured by      | Rank trackers, organic traffic      | Share of voice / citation tracking across engines              |
| Predictability   | Mostly stable, transparent          | Variable; same question can yield different answers            |

Specialized tools (Profound, Otterly.ai, Peec AI, Semrush's AI toolkit, HubSpot's AEO grader, and others) run many prompts across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews and report mention frequency, citation rate, and share of voice over time.

> [!FOR THE AE] Set honest expectations. AEO/GEO is **early, fast-changing, and uncertain** — engines update constantly, answers vary, and no one can promise a "position." Frame it as a _durable habit_: keep the business's facts clear, structured, reviewed, and consistent everywhere, and it becomes a likelier source for AI answers over time. Promise the practice, not a guaranteed placement.

## Key terms

- **SEO (Search Engine Optimization)** — getting a website to rank high in traditional search link results.
- **AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)** — optimizing content to _be the answer_ in snippets and AI summaries.
- **GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)** — optimizing to be cited/recommended by generative AI assistants; in practice largely synonymous with AEO.
- **Zero-click search** — a search that's answered on-screen, so the user never clicks through to a website.
- **AI Overviews** — Google's AI-generated answer shown above the regular results.
- **RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)** — the engine retrieves real web sources first, then generates an answer grounded in them.
- **Query fan-out** — one user question split into multiple background sub-searches.
- **Citation** — a linked source the AI used to support its answer.
- **Entity** — a recognized, distinct "thing" (a specific salon) the AI understands as real.
- **Corroboration / consensus** — the same facts confirmed across multiple independent sources, which builds AI trust.
- **AI Share of Voice** — how often a brand is mentioned/cited across a set of AI answers versus competitors.
