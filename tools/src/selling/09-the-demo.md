# The demo — canonical flow & dev controls

> [!TL;DR] The Zoca demo has exactly three jobs: show the prospect their **gap** in their real market with their real numbers, show the **four ways** Zoca closes that gap (anchored to the pain they named in qualifying), and walk out with a **written next step**. The canonical version runs ~20 minutes — GBP audit reveal → the four-promises walk → a Day 0 → Wk 3 toggle that sets honest expectations → a live **Test Call** to the **Front Desk Agent** → the pricing reveal at the very end → close. The **Dev panel** is what lets one rep show every state on one link without staging or "let me find the right login." Run it from muscle memory, stay honest about what's placeholder or coming-soon, and never bury the price.

---

## 1. What a Zoca demo actually is

A demo is not a product walkthrough. A product walkthrough is what you do when you're proud of what you built. A demo is what you do when you want a prospect to sign. If you spend 35 minutes on what the dashboard does, you've run a walkthrough and you won't close.

The three jobs, and only these three:

1. **Show the gap** — in their actual market, with their actual numbers.
2. **Show the four fixes** — one demo move per promise, led by the pain they named in qualifying.
3. **Get a written next step** — a signature link, a second call, or a handoff to the AM team. Never "I'll think about it," never "I'll send you something."

> [!SAY IT SIMPLY] Do those three things in 20 minutes and you've run the demo. The voice is peer-to-peer: concrete numbers, honest about what isn't built yet, slightly conspiratorial but never pushy. They're running a business. So are you.

## 2. The canonical 20-minute flow

Set a timer. Stay on it.

| Minute      | Move                | What you actually do                                            |
| ----------- | ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0:00–1:00   | Opening             | Set expectations, confirm time, restate their biggest pain      |
| 1:00–4:00   | Audit reveal        | Pull up their GBP, run a live keyword + AI search, name one gap |
| 4:00–12:00  | Four promises       | Each promise, one demo move, anchored to their pain             |
| 12:00–14:00 | Day 0 → Wk 3 reveal | Toggle the Dev panel — start vs. destination                    |
| 14:00–17:00 | Test Call           | Live Test Call to the Front Desk Agent, or the audio sample     |
| 17:00–18:00 | Pricing reveal      | One number, no flinch — saved for the end on purpose            |
| 18:00–20:00 | Close               | Annual close OR the recap-and-schedule handle                   |

> [!DEMO TIP] Run the **condensed 10-minute version** when the prospect has a hard stop (see §9). Switch to it silently — don't announce you're cutting it short.

---

## 3. Opening (1 minute)

Confirm you listened, set the time box, and give them permission to interrupt — engaged prospects don't zone out.

> [!TALK TRACK] "Quick recap before I share — you've got 2 locations in Charlotte, no agency right now, and the thing bleeding the most is the phone going unanswered on Saturdays. I'll spend about 20 minutes. Interrupt me whenever something doesn't track. End of this, you'll know if it's a fit and we'll figure out the next step. Sound right?"

If they correct anything in your recap, take it seriously and re-order the demo on the fly to lead with whatever they corrected.

---

## 4. The audit reveal (3 minutes)

This is the moment that flips the demo from "sales pitch" to "I need this." Don't editorialize and don't fix anything yet — just narrate the gap. The prospect has to _feel_ it before they care about the fix.

1. "Let me share my screen. Sharing now — tell me when you see it."
2. Open their actual Google Business Profile (you have it from the pre-call audit).
3. "This is you. 67 reviews, 4.9 stars — that's strong. So whatever's broken, it's not your reviews."
4. New tab. Search "balayage near me" with the location set to their city. "Top three — Salon X, Y, Z. You're not in the top three for the search that drives most of your new bookings."
5. New tab. Ask an AI chat assistant "best balayage salon in [city]." Read the answer. "It named two of those same competitors. Didn't name you."
6. Pause. "That's the gap. Now let me show you what we'd do about it."

> [!DEMO TIP] **Let the silence work.** After the reveal, sit quiet for 4–6 seconds. The prospect will say "yeah, that's bad" or "what does it cost to fix this?" — that sentence is the demo turning in your favor. Don't rush to the next screen.

## 5. The four promises (8 minutes — ~2 each)

Demo all four. The **order is set by the pain they named in qualifying** — lead with that one, touch the others quickly. Set the Dev panel to the destination state first: **Day State = Wk 3, Growth Band = Prime, Product State = Full**.

These are the four leaks Zoca closes:

| #   | Promise                                                | Demo move                                                                                                                                       | Anchor line                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 01  | **Fill new bookings** (visibility)                     | Discovery Agent → Keywords (demand map by service tier), then the Content Queue showing the next 4 weeks of blogs + GBP posts already scheduled | "We don't email you a strategy — we write and publish the content: 96 blog posts a year, 144 GBP posts, 20 service pages, aimed at the keywords your customers type into Google."                                                                                |
| 02  | **Answer every call** (missed calls)                   | Front Desk Agent → Activity. Read one inbound transcript aloud. Then Settings — greeting, voice options, deposit policy                         | "Voice AI receptionist, picks up in under a second, books and reschedules. You said 18 missed calls last Saturday — with this on, that's 18 confirmed bookings or 18 reasons you can see in this dashboard."                                                     |
| 03  | **Turn one-timers into regulars** (first-timer churn)  | Reviews → Recent (live now), then the Loyalty Agent (rebook windows, dormant-guest detection)                                                   | "The reviews piece is live now. The rebook nudges live in the Loyalty Agent — that one is rolling out and ships as a $49/mo add-on when you want it."                                                                                                            |
| 04  | **Win back every lost booking** (mid-booking drop-off) | Conversion Agent → Funnel. Show the drop-off steps and where bookings stall                                                                     | "We track every step, fire a voice + SMS recovery in minutes, and recover a chunk of every stalled booking. It's a $49/mo add-on plus 10% of recovered value, and it needs Bookings & Payments connected. Most start with the bundle and add this in month two." |

> [!FOR THE AE] If they ask "is it really AI?" — be honest. "Yeah, it's our **Front Desk Agent**. We built it in-house. We'll do a Test Call in a couple minutes and you'll hear it." Don't dodge — and don't name any third-party vendor; the voice is built in-house.

> [!COMPLIANCE NOTE] Never present a Coming-Soon feature as if it's live. The Loyalty Agent and Ads Agent are roadmap/add-on — show the destination state, label it honestly, and sell it as upside, never with a launch date. This is value 04: be honest, full stop.

## 6. Day 0 → Wk 3 reveal (2 minutes)

This is the single most important move in the demo **for managing expectations**. New customers don't get the Wk 3 dashboard on day one — they get an empty dashboard with setup checklists. Show them that first, then toggle to the destination.

1. Open the Dev panel. Toggle **Day State → Day 0**.
2. Show the empty dashboard: KPIs at zero, setup checklist, "Get your first reviews today." "This is your account the day you sign. It's empty — we earn the numbers."
3. Toggle back to **Day State: Wk 3 + Growth Band: Prime**. Stay silent 4 seconds while it re-renders.
4. "Same business, three weeks later. Calls handled, bookings booked, page-1 keywords climbing, the AI now naming your salon. That's the destination."
5. Close the Dev panel.

> [!WHY IT MATTERS] Skip this and you create churn risk: customers expect the Wk 3 numbers on Day 1, blame you when the dashboard is empty, and cancel before activation. Showing the empty Day 0 first is what makes the slow start feel honest instead of broken.

## 7. The Dev panel — your demo cockpit

The 🛠 **Dev panel** sits bottom-right of every dashboard page in the preview. Each toggle changes what the dashboard renders — KPIs, activity feed, unlocked agents, CTAs, copy. It is a **sales tool, not a real-customer surface**: it does not exist in production. It exists so one rep can demonstrate every state on a single shared link without staging.

> [!RULE OF THUMB] Open it twice per demo: once **before** the prospect joins (to pre-stage state), and once **mid-demo** when the toggle flip _is_ the move (the Day 0 → Wk 3 reveal). Otherwise: open it, set state, close it. Demoing with the panel visible the whole call is noise.

The toggles you'll touch most:

| Toggle                                      | What it changes                                   | When to use                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Entry Product** (Discovery / Voice AI)    | The default landing tile                          | Discovery for SEO-angle prospects; Voice AI for "I'm losing calls"   |
| **Organization** (Single / 2 Loc / 3 Loc)   | Location switcher + KPI rollup                    | Set before the demo opens if they're multi-location                  |
| **Day State** (Day 0 / Day 2 / Wk 1 / Wk 3) | Every KPI, the feed, checklists                   | The one you flip most — Day 0 → Wk 3 is your biggest move            |
| **Product State** (Disc / +Voice / Full)    | Which agents are unlocked                         | Almost always Full (shows the bundle)                                |
| **B&P** (Connected / not)                   | Wires Bookings & Payments                         | Connected for any Conversion Agent demo, or the funnel renders empty |
| **Calendar** (Connected / not)              | Populates the Calendar with synced appointments   | Always Connected for any Front Desk Activity demo                    |
| **Growth Band** (Spark / Rise / Prime)      | The growth ladder + bookings forecast             | Match the prospect's real review count                               |
| **Account ACV** (<$5K / >$5K)               | Email routing + AM behavior in MBR / Get in Touch | Set **before** opening those surfaces                                |

> [!GOOD TO KNOW] Three "standard combos" cover ~80% of demos: **Standard** (Single · Wk 3 · Full · B&P + Calendar Connected · Rise · ACV <$5K), **Multi-Loc** (3 Loc · Wk 3 · Full · ACV >$5K · Loyalty Active), and **Day 0 Reveal** (Single · Day 0 · Full · Spark · ACV <$5K). Memorize them.

> [!DEMO TIP] Never read toggle names aloud. "Now let me set Day State to Wk 3" — the prospect doesn't care about your tooling. Set it silently; narrate the change in the _dashboard_, not the toggle.

**When a toggle glitches** (the preview is alive — it happens):

- KPIs still show zeros after a Day State flip → hard refresh (Cmd+R), re-set the toggle.
- Conversion funnel page empty → check B&P = Connected and Day State ≥ Wk 1.
- Get in Touch routing didn't update → close and re-open the chooser; state reads on dialog open.
- Something genuinely breaks → don't stall. "Let me jump to the Front Desk Activity instead — you'll see the agent in motion." Recover by pivoting to a flow you know works.

## 8. The Test Call (3 minutes)

The live Test Call to the **Front Desk Agent** is the **single highest-converting moment in the demo** — and the highest reversal risk if you wing it. Don't wing it.

**Warm up the demo number ~5 minutes before the call:**

1. **Match the vertical.** We keep separate demo lines for salon, spa, med-spa, massage, and barber. A spa prospect hearing a hair-salon agent loses trust instantly — using the right line is the single biggest factor in call quality.
2. **Place a test ping call** from your own phone. Listen for pickup time (1–2 rings), voice clarity, response naturalness. If anything feels off, switch lines or plan to use a canned recording.
3. **Send a test text** to the same number to confirm SMS is alive (response in <10s).
4. **Test audio routing** into your Zoom/Meet — unmute your phone, hold it near your mic, verify the prospect can hear the agent. Audio drops are demo killers.
5. **Rehearse your opening line** out loud — realistic, not theatrical.

**The live call (~90 seconds):**

> [!TALK TRACK] **Setup:** "I'd rather you hear it than I describe it. Got your phone? I'll text you a number. Call it on speaker and try to book something. It's a training instance for a sample salon, so don't expect it to know _your_ services yet — but you'll get a feel for what your customers will hear."

Dial, put it on speaker, let the greeting complete (don't talk over it), make your booking ask, then add one challenging turn — "How much does it cost?" or "Can you do Saturday morning?" — and let the agent close. Hang up.

> [!FOR THE AE] **If the agent makes a mistake** — and it sometimes does — be honest, don't pretend it was perfect: "This is a training model, so it doesn't have a real booking system connected. On a real instance, it pulls live availability from your booking platform. What matters here is the voice, the pacing, and how it handles the question." Honesty keeps the trust intact.

**The follow-up math question** is what puts them inside the product:

> [!TALK TRACK] "So — if that were your actual phone ringing at 9pm on a Saturday when you're home with your kids, and a prospect called about a $450 service, that whole conversation just happened whether you picked up or not. How many of those calls do you think you're missing a week?"

After the call, let the prospect react first. If they're quiet for 5 seconds, ask one question: "What did you think?" Silence is them thinking — not awkwardness.

## 9. Demo accounts — canned vs. live

Two ways to show the Front Desk Agent. Know when to use which.

|          | **Live Test Call**                                          | **Canned recording**                                                                                |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Use when | Onboarding/line is warmed up and audio routing tested clean | The demo line is shaky, audio is poor, or a live call is going badly                                |
| Why      | Highest-converting moment — interactive, undeniable         | Keeps the wow moment without risking the deal                                                       |
| Prep     | The 5-minute warm-up above                                  | Keep **3+ vertical-matched recordings saved locally** (hair / spa / med-spa) so they play instantly |

> [!FOR THE AE] **Never let a bad live call play out.** Interrupt and pivot gracefully: "Okay — I'm going to stop that, the demo line has a glitch today. Let me play you a recorded call from a real customer instead." Then play a clean clip.

> [!COMPLIANCE NOTE] **Honesty rules for partial/placeholder data.** Some surfaces in the preview are partial — multi-location **rollup** pages can render **placeholder data** at 3 Loc. Never fake it and never pass placeholder screens off as a real customer's numbers. Pivot to a single-location deep dive and say it straight: _"This network rollup is in the next preview drop — happy to walk a real customer's network on a follow-up. Today, here's what each location's dashboard looks like."_ Honesty matters more than the demo.

> [!GOOD TO KNOW] Within 30 minutes of the demo, send the recording of the live call (with consent) — subject line "Your demo call — Zoca Front Desk Agent." It's replay-worthy and shareable to co-decision-makers, moving the deal without another live meeting.

## 10. The pricing reveal (1 minute)

This is where new AEs get nervous and bury the price. Don't. You held it to the end **on purpose** — they've now felt the gap and seen the fix.

> [!TALK TRACK] "Pricing is simple. **`[annual plan price]`/month** on a 1-year contract, or **`[monthly plan price]`/month** monthly. That's the bundle — Discovery Agent and Front Desk Agent together. Bookings & Payments is free plus a 2.7% transaction fee if you turn it on. Loyalty Agent is $49/mo. Conversion Agent is $49/mo plus 10% of recovered bookings. That's the menu."

> [!RULE OF THUMB] Say the number, then **stop**. Don't defend it, don't say "which is less than most agencies charge…", don't ask "what's your budget?" Whoever talks first after the price loses leverage. Wait for them to react.

> [!SOURCE NOTE] Pricing figures are **pending final confirmation** — keep them as placeholders and **reject any stale numbers** ($179 / $249 / $269 / $279 / $290 are all dead; never quote them). Yearly and monthly totals stay as `[annual plan total/yr]` / `[monthly plan total/yr]`.

> [!VALUE LINE] **There is a setup fee, and it's waived only on the annual plan** — the waiver is baked into annual pricing. Use it as a concrete reason to go yearly: "If you go annual, the setup fee comes off — that's part of why the yearly number lands where it does." Never say "no setup fee" (stale wording), and never state a setup-fee dollar amount — it's TBD.

## 11. Close to a written next step (2 minutes)

Two paths out of pricing:

- **Path A — they're ready.** Run the annual close and **send the signature link before the call ends.**
- **Path B — they want time.** Don't force it. Use the recap-and-schedule handle to end with a confirmed next step.

> [!TALK TRACK] "Want me to send the signature link now, or schedule 30 minutes with the AM team Tuesday?"

> [!OBJECTION] **"Send me a one-pager and I'll review it with my partner."** → "I can do this two ways — send a recap and lose two weeks on calendars, or give you 15 minutes right now and you walk away knowing whether it's a fit. Which one?"

Never end with "I'll send you something." Always end with a calendar invite or a signature link.

## 12. The condensed 10-minute version

When the prospect has a hard stop:

| Minute     | Move                                                                                                    |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0:00–0:30  | Restate their pain. "You said the phone is the leak — let me show you what we'd do about it."           |
| 0:30–2:30  | Audit reveal — one local search + AI search only, skip the full GBP review                              |
| 2:30–4:30  | Their pain promise only — "happy to walk the other three after, but the one you care about is this one" |
| 4:30–7:30  | Test Call — live or canned                                                                              |
| 7:30–8:30  | Pricing reveal                                                                                          |
| 8:30–10:00 | Close — signature link or scheduled AM call                                                             |

Skip the Day 0 → Wk 3 toggle in the 10-minute cut — move it into the second call.

> [!SALON EXAMPLE] Devon, a 3-chair barbershop owner, showed up 8 minutes late to a 20-minute slot. The rep silently switched to the 10-minute flow, parked the visibility gap ("we'll get to it — phone first"), ran the Test Call (Devon laughed when the agent asked "line-up included or razor finish?"), paused 6 seconds after, and Devon asked the price himself. Annual. Signed at minute 11 — because the rep refused to fight the clock.

## 13. Personalize throughout

Every demo uses the prospect's vertical, location, and service vocabulary. A barbershop hears "fade," not "balayage." A med-spa hears "RN" or "esthetician," not "stylist." The keyword search runs in _their_ city. Memorize the most common verticals and their top services so you're never reading from a script mid-demo.

> [!RULE OF THUMB] **ICP is best-fit, not a hard no.** A lower-fit prospect who wants to buy can buy — set honest expectations on timeline and stage; don't refuse them. The **one genuine hard requirement** is operational: a **laptop or iPad must be in the shop**, or the onboarding demo can't run. Everything else flexes.

## Key terms

- **The demo (vs. walkthrough)** — a 20-minute sequence to get a signature, not a tour of the dashboard. Three jobs: show the gap, show the four fixes, get a written next step.
- **Audit reveal** — the screen-share at minutes 1–4 where you show the prospect their real GBP, a live local-search ranking gap, and the AI-search gap. Narrate; don't fix.
- **The four promises / four leaks** — fill new bookings (visibility), answer every call (missed calls), turn one-timers into regulars (first-timer churn), win back lost bookings (mid-booking drop-off).
- **Day 0 → Wk 3 reveal** — the toggle that shows empty start vs. populated destination; the core expectation-setting move.
- **Dev panel** — the 🛠 toggle panel in the preview that renders any state on one link. Sales-only; not in production. Open it, set state, close it.
- **Test Call** — the live call to the **Front Desk Agent** on speaker so the prospect hears the voice and pacing. Highest-converting, highest-risk moment.
- **Canned recording** — a vertical-matched saved call clip; the safe fallback when the live line is shaky or a call goes badly.
- **Placeholder/partial data** — preview surfaces (esp. multi-location rollup) that aren't real yet; pivot to single-location and say the rollup is in the next drop. Never fake it.
- **Pricing reveal** — the late-demo, one-sentence price drop, then silence. Figures are placeholders pending final confirmation; setup fee is waived on annual.
