# The sales process, team & deal mechanics

> [!TL;DR] The process is built around one rule: a **mandatory discovery call comes first**, with the product shown fully loaded, and **no payment before the demo.** The SDR qualifies, the full-cycle AE closes. Onboarding follows one of five journeys based on the prospect's existing booking platform, a 7-day trial requires a card on file, and a one-per-business contract is e-signed before payment.

---

## 1. How the team is structured

> [!SOURCE NOTE] These details come from internal sales-org notes captured at a point in time (headcount and names will drift). Treat the _structure_ as durable and the specific numbers as a snapshot.

- **At recording:** 3 SDRs + 3 AEs (one AE in transition, accounts handed over).
- **Two separate teams** are being built:
  - **Solo / single-location team** — chases the large US base of solopreneurs and single-location businesses (including single-location-with-staff); training framed this as "all the ~350k solopreneurs in the US market."
  - **Multi-location team** — built separately, owns its own discovery, prospecting, conversion, and full customer lifecycle. "Requires a different level of sophistication and mindset."

---

## 2. Roles & model

> [!KEY TERM] **Full-cycle AE** — by default, one AE runs the deal end to end ("full cycle A will do full cycle A"). An SDR-only option may roll out later.

> [!RULE OF THUMB] **The SDR owns qualification; the AE does not re-qualify.** The AE closes. The main reason an AE wouldn't close is the customer rejecting the product — not a qualification miss.

---

## 3. The pipeline

> [!HOW IT WORKS] The flow is deliberate: qualify (SDR) → **mandatory discovery call** (AE) → outcome. The discovery call is _not_ a "conversion call" — it's where you show the product **fully loaded, with all agents active.** This is a required step.

- **No payment before the demo.** The prospect sees the product first.
- **Three post-discovery outcomes:** sign now / "come back to you" / not interested.
- **Six tiers of mandatory discovery questions** — understand the salon, its customer volume, and what's already working before you pitch.

> [!FOR THE AE] The discovery call is the heart of the motion. Don't shortcut it to rush a close — a fully-loaded demo is what earns the sign-now outcome. Playbooks (AE Playbook, CX Playbook, SOP) live in the owned internal doc ("the holy bible"); use them.

---

## 4. The five onboarding journeys (A–E)

Which onboarding journey a customer takes is triggered by the **booking platform they already use.**

| Journey trigger                | What's possible                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Platforms that give an API     | Deeper integration — read data and action the platform                                                               |
| No-API platforms (e.g. Fresha) | Browser automation: can read their data and do the booking, but **not** action their platform; no past customer data |
| Flagship path                  | Migrate the business to Zoca bookings & payments                                                                     |

> [!SOURCE NOTE] The sales-process framing named five API platforms ("Vergado/Boulevard, Zenoti, MindBody, Acuity"). For **Front Desk** platform specifics, trust the Front Desk product training instead — the verified integration reality there is: true API = Square + Mindbody; browser automation = Booksy / Fresha / GlossGenius / Vagaro; a Boulevard adapter is not built. The A–E list is the sales-process story, not the engineering source of truth.

> [!RULE OF THUMB] **No price difference between journeys.** Whether a customer integrates, runs on browser automation, or migrates fully to Zoca, pricing is the same — so guide them toward the path that serves them best, not a cheaper tier.

## 5. Support / CX model

The post-sale model is shifting away from traditional account management.

- **No dedicated account manager** for customers paying under $5k or not on bookings & payments.
- **Dedicated pod** only for customers over $5k or on the full suite.
- Account management is being replaced by **Command Center** plus an **AI account manager ("AIM")** that answers business questions; on escalation it creates a ticket → human support during working hours.

> [!FOR THE AE] If a prospect worries about "who'll look after me," the answer is Command Center + the AI AM for everyday questions, with human support on escalation — and a dedicated pod for larger/full-suite accounts. Set that expectation honestly during the sale.

---

## 6. Trial, contract & billing

> [!HOW IT WORKS] The deal mechanics: a **7-day trial**, a **card on file required to start**, and **auto-charge after 7 days.** Billing runs through Chargebee. The **contract is one per business**, e-signed before payment; adding more agents later augments the same contract — no re-signing.

> [!OBJECTION] "Why do you need my card before I've paid?" — The card starts the 7-day trial and the auto-charge after; it's standard trial mechanics, and the contract is signed up front so there are no surprises. You're not charged until the trial ends.

---

## 7. Key terms

- **Discovery call** — the mandatory, fully-loaded product demo that precedes any payment (not a "conversion call").
- **Full-cycle AE** — one AE runs the deal end to end; the SDR qualifies.
- **Onboarding journeys (A–E)** — the five paths determined by the prospect's current booking platform.
- **AIM (AI account manager)** — the AI that answers business questions post-sale, replacing dedicated account management below the pod threshold.
- **Pod** — a dedicated support team for customers over $5k or on the full suite.
- **Trial mechanics** — 7-day trial, card on file, auto-charge after 7 days, billed via Chargebee.
