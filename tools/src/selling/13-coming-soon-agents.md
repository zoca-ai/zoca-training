# The Conversion Agent, the coming-soon agents & the flywheel

> [!TL;DR] Beyond the bundled core (Discovery Agent + Front Desk Agent = **Discovery Neo**), Zoca has one more agent you can sell today and three that are on the roadmap. The **Conversion Agent — "the Black Box"** — recovers mid-booking drop-offs, the leak no other tool measures; it's the only agent that pays for itself in a single recovered booking. **Booking, Loyalty, and Ads** are coming-soon agents you position as **upside, never with launch dates**. All five share **one context layer** — the flywheel — and **Ashley**, the AI account manager, runs a recorded Monthly Business Review (MBR) every month so the owner always knows what the agents did. Sell what's live, frame the rest honestly, and never overpromise.

## 2. The Conversion Agent — "the Black Box"

This is the most leveraged agent in the lineup. Owners measure traffic and they measure bookings. They do **not** measure the customers who started booking and walked away. That gap is the Black Box — and it's where the P&L lives.

> [!VALUE LINE] "The most important number you're not measuring." (Sales Deck Slide 10.) That single line is the entire frame.

Three numbers carry the pitch:

- **31%** of bookings stall mid-flow. Industry baseline. (Sales Deck Slides 03 + 10.)
- **42–58%** of every _contactable_ stalled booking is recoverable when an agent reaches out at the right moment. (Sales Deck Slide 10.)
- The intent already happened. The customer chose this shop, started booking, hit something they didn't like, and left. **The hardest part of the sale — them deciding — is already done.**

> [!TALK TRACK] Lead with the question, not the chart: _"Quick question — when a customer goes to your website to book and bails halfway through, where do you see that?"_ (Pause.) _"You don't. Nobody does. That's a third of your bookings, gone, every month — and not a single tool in the industry is built to catch them. We built one. It's called Conversion Agent."_

## 3. Why mid-booking is the leak nobody fixes

Owners spend on three layers to fill chairs: **top of funnel** (SEO, ads, social — awareness), **mid-funnel** (website, booking flow, deposits — the moment of decision), and **bottom of funnel** (confirmations, reminders — showing up). The middle layer is the one nobody fixes, because fixing it means knowing _which_ customer left, _why_, and reaching them within minutes — not 48 hours later.

Zoca instruments every step of the booking flow, sees the exact stage and reason each customer dropped, then fires Voice AI + smart SMS/email at the right moment.

> [!KEY TERM] **The Black Box** — Promise 04 of the Sales Deck. The mid-funnel drop-off most owners can't measure. It's the third of the four leaks in the curriculum (visibility, missed calls, **mid-booking drop-off**, first-timer churn). The pitch is **recovery, not analytics** — charts are table stakes; the agent reaching out is the wedge.

## 4. The Funnel Detail page — the one surface that's built

Navigate to the Conversion → Funnel Detail page. Set Day State = **Wk 3** and Product State = **Full** before you demo — Day 0 is empty because the agent hasn't watched any sessions yet.

A vertical funnel chart shows conversion at each of seven steps (illustrative preview defaults):

| #   | Step              | Healthy    | Why drops happen                                                         |
| --- | ----------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | Service selection | ~91.7%     | Service not listed, or not labeled how Google labels it                  |
| 2   | Date              | ~90.2%     | Specific day blocked, no next-best surfaced                              |
| 3   | Time              | ~89.9%     | Exact hour gone, no alternative suggested                                |
| 4   | Staff             | ~93.1%     | Preferred stylist absent                                                 |
| 5   | Details form      | ~73.1%     | Form too long; asks too much before showing price                        |
| 6   | **Deposit**       | **~58.2%** | **The killer — card hesitation, deposit confusion, comparison shopping** |
| 7   | Login / Confirm   | ~91.3%     | Account-creation friction or last-second cold feet                       |

> [!DEMO TIP] The deposit step is where the money is. **~18% of the original session walks at deposit** — twice the drop of any other step. (Sales Deck Slide 10.) Always make the prospect look at this row: _"These are people who picked a service, a stylist, a time — they want it — and then hit the deposit page and walked. We bring them back."_

**Trend states** color-code each step on week-over-week movement: **Healthy** (green, keep watching) · **Monitor** (blue, flag only) · **Investigate** (amber, surfaces an AI Recommendation card) · **Action Needed** (red, top of the queue, urgent badge). The owner doesn't read 47 dashboards — the agent does.

## 5. The three drop-off buckets

A "Who dropped off" pane segments drop-offs into three behavioral buckets — and the bucket determines what the recovery message sounds like.

| Bucket                  | Signal                                           | Recovery move                                                                             |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Price shock**         | Landed on deposit page, bounced instantly        | $0-deposit experiment for new customers, or a payment-plan option — don't argue the price |
| **Comparison shopping** | Sat 30+ seconds, maybe opened a new tab, bounced | Voice call within minutes — stylist match, time-window option, a reason _this_ salon      |
| **UX friction**         | Clicked into the payment form, started, bailed   | SMS deep-link back to a pre-filled form, or a one-click alternate payment route           |

> [!SAY IT SIMPLY] _"Three reasons a customer leaves — sticker shock, comparison, or the form fought them. Three different recovery messages. The agent figures out which one and sends the right move."_

## 6. The follow-up sequence — honest about what's recoverable

Below the buckets, the "Conversion Agent follow-up" pane shows the live recovery work: _of N drop-offs this week — X had phone/email and got contacted · Y were anonymous (couldn't reach) · Z recovered · W still in sequence · V cold (won't recover)._

> [!TALK TRACK] _"This week, 47 sessions dropped at deposit. 38 had phone or email — agent reached out. 9 were anonymous, those are gone. Of the 38, agent recovered 17 — that's 17 bookings that would've been zero. Industry recovery is 42 to 58%."_

Recovery stays honest:

- **Anonymous drop-offs can't be recovered** — no contact info. ~20% of drop-offs are anonymous. Never promise 100%.
- **Cold drop-offs** were reached but didn't engage. Two attempts, then we stop. We don't spam.
- **Recovered + still-in-sequence** is the live number that pays for the agent.

> [!OBJECTION] **"How is this different from Shopify cart abandonment?"** — "Three things. One — it's a service, not a product, so timing matters: the customer wants Saturday, not 'sometime.' Two — we use voice; SMS alone recovers ~12%, voice gets past 50%. Three — the message is tuned to the _reason_ they left. Generic 'you left your cart' doesn't cut it for a haircut."

## 7. AI Recommendation cards — analytics into action

Toward the bottom of the page, the agent surfaces **AI Recommendation cards** — one specific experiment each:

> _"33 people dropped at the deposit step this week — a 40% increase week-over-week. Current deposit is $50. Run a 2-week experiment: $0 deposit for new customers."_
> Buttons: **Run Experiment · Tell My AM · Dismiss**

- **Run Experiment** — agent applies the change for the window, measures the lift, reports back.
- **Tell My AM** — routes the recommendation to a human conversation (the AM at >$5K ACV, the CX pod at <$5K).
- **Dismiss** — owner says no, agent moves on.

> [!DEMO TIP] Demo at least one card on every Conversion pitch — it's the experiment loop, the thing that turns analytics into action. _"Most tools tell you the number is bad. We tell you the number is bad, here's why, and here's the experiment. The agent's the consultant — while you're doing color."_

## 9. The coming-soon agents — sold as upside, never with dates

These three are short on purpose: they're in the sidebar today but not fully built. We name them, position them as upside, and **never give a launch date.** Marketing or RevOps publishes a date when there is one; until then, "on the roadmap."

> [!COMPLIANCE NOTE] We've already had to walk back "loyalty ships next quarter" commitments more than once, and the compliance audit is watching. Reps who overpromise dates get customers who feel lied to in month two and churn loudly. The discipline is honesty, not vapor.

**Booking Agent** — once shipped: branded **confirmations**, **reminders** timed to the customer's no-show history, **no-show recovery** with one-click rebook, and **waitlist** management. _Today:_ a coming-soon stub in the sidebar; the owner's existing platform (Vagaro, Fresha, Square) handles basic reminders, and the Front Desk Agent picks up reschedule calls. It ships _after_ Conversion's full launch — the two share the same outreach engine, so we build it once in Conversion and plug Booking on top.

**Loyalty Agent** — once shipped: **post-visit concierge** (a brand-voice voice call within 48 hours of a first visit), **rebook nudges** at each customer's personal window, **dormant rescue** the day a lapsed client's window opens, and **seasonal campaigns** per location/service. _Today:_ the most real-looking of the three — a functional setup CTA at the Loyalty overview, though the campaign engine isn't yet wired to live data (be honest about that).

> [!VALUE LINE] **Loyalty Agent = $49/month**, includes **500 SMS + 100 AI calls** per month. Above that, usage-based overage at rates set at launch. Multi-location operators usually go above the included envelope — that's an enterprise conversation. Opt-in: it doesn't activate until the owner turns it on. (Source: Lovable Settings → Add-ons.)

The destination metric you can quote: 52% of first-time beauty guests never return; with Loyalty running, **first-time rebook moves from ~48% to 65–72% in 90 days** on the network. (Sales Deck Slides 04 + 09, Promise 03.) Quote it as the _destination_, not a promise of when the prospect hits it.

**Ads Agent** — once shipped: **Google Local Services Ads + Performance Max** and **Meta (Facebook + Instagram)** campaigns tuned per location. _Today:_ a connect-only stub where the OAuth connections work but the campaign-management layer isn't built.

> [!COMPLIANCE NOTE] **Where does ad spend go?** _"Directly to Google and Meta. Always. We're an authorized manager — campaigns run inside your accounts, the dollars never flow through us, and we don't mark anything up."_ This is non-negotiable framing. Owners burned by agencies who kept "rebates" don't trust ad vendors — we have to be cleaner than the cleanest agency.

> [!RULE OF THUMB] **Never quote an Ads Agent price.** Pricing is TBD. Anyone who asks gets: _"Pricing's still landing — likely a small monthly management fee plus the ad spend. We'll publish before launch, and bundled annual customers get the launch deal automatically."_ Don't improvise a number to look prepared.

## 11. The 5-agent flywheel — one context layer

The reason Zoca built five agents instead of one: they share a single context layer — the customer, their visit history, preferences, drop-offs, reviews, and search behavior. (Sales Deck Slide 12 — "AI-Native: one platform, one context layer.")

- **Discovery** brings the customer to the website (SEO + AI search + GBP).
- **Conversion** catches them if they bail mid-booking.
- **Front Desk** picks up if they call instead of booking online.
- **Booking** confirms, reminds, recovers no-shows.
- **Loyalty** brings them back for visit two, three, fifty.

> [!TALK TRACK] _"Most stacks have five tools that don't talk. Ours has five agents that share one brain. The Front Desk knows what your SEO is ranking for. The Loyalty Agent knows what your Front Desk booked. The Conversion Agent knows what your Booking Agent confirmed. Every customer interaction makes every other agent smarter. That's the moat."_

Use it when the prospect asks "why all five?" — not as a feature pile-up, and never to put coming-soon agents in the value math.

## 12. Ashley & the MBR — the AI account manager and the monthly cadence

**Ashley is an AI account manager — never a chatbot.** This is the single most-corrected mistake in the curriculum, and it changes how a prospect values everything else. A chatbot answers questions; Ashley _reads the account and acts on it._

> [!KEY TERM] **Ashley** — the AI account manager. She's trained on the customer's account (past MBRs, current KPIs, agent recommendations), she **acts** (runs a Conversion experiment, drafts a Loyalty campaign, books an MBR, escalates to a human AM when needed), and she replies in **under 30 seconds** on average. Say "AI account manager," "trained on your account," "she knows / she does / she suggests." Never "chatbot," "bot," or "AI assistant."

> [!OBJECTION] **"So it's a chatbot?"** — _"No — Ashley's an AI account manager. A chatbot answers questions. Ashley reads your account — every metric, every lead, every lever — and acts. She'll book your MBR, reframe a recommendation, draft a campaign, escalate to your human AM if it's past her wheelhouse. She's a teammate, not a search bar."_ Then prove it: have her pull a real number and offer a real action. The demo kills the chatbot frame, not the script.

**The MBR (Monthly Business Review)** is a recurring **30-minute strategic review** with Ashley, every month — not a support call. Each one is captured permanently (recording, AI summary, Highlights & Actions). _"Once a month you sit down with Ashley, walk through what the agents did — bookings, reviews, keywords, conversion — she points at what moved, suggests what to change, and you decide."_

| MBR mechanic         | The rule                                                                                                                             |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Booking window**   | Opens the 1st; schedulable slots **7th–15th** (weekdays); closes the 15th. Outside the window the Schedule CTA hides, history stays. |
| **Slots**            | Four per day: 9:00, 11:00, 14:00, 16:00 (local).                                                                                     |
| **Reminders**        | Day 25 email, Day 27 calendar; if missed, reactivates day 5 of next month.                                                           |
| **Signup timing**    | Sign up **before the 15th** → Month 1 MBR is open this cycle. **After the 15th** → Month 1 is skipped, cycle starts at Month 2.      |
| **Orientation Call** | A one-time onboarding session on **Day 8** (Welcome flag), separate from the recurring MBRs — same timeline, different agenda.       |
| **Missed MBR**       | No penalty; "Read written recap" opens the existing **Monthly Performance Report PDF** — it does **not** generate a new document.    |

**ACV-tier behavior** (internal only — the prospect never hears "ACV"): at **>$5K**, a dedicated human AM proactively reaches out to book MBRs alongside Ashley; at **<$5K**, the customer books reactively from the dashboard with automated reminders. The **Get in Touch** button (top-right of every page) opens a chooser with three routes — **Chat with Ashley** (recommended, <30s reply), **Hop on a call** (AI Live Call, 24/7), **Drop us a note** (email, routed by ACV to the Customer Success Pod or the customer's Account Manager).

> [!FOR THE AE] **"Do I have a human account manager?"** — _"At your ACV level, yes. Past $5K annual there's a dedicated human AM doing proactive outreach to book your MBRs; below that you book yourself from the dashboard. Either way Ashley's the first line and humans are the safety rail — the product, agents, and channels are identical."_ Never speak the "<$5K" label to a prospect.
