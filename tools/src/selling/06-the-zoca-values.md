# The Zoca values

> [!TL;DR] Zoca has six values, and they weren't copied from a workshop deck — they were written down after we'd already lived them. For an AE/AM they're not wall art; they're a **decision filter** for the moments no policy covers. Two carry the most weight in this job: **value 04 — be honest, full stop** (how you sell), and **value 05 — dream until it scares you** (the altitude you sell at: IPO, global, the top SMB company built from India). This lesson is also the home for our **compliance discipline** — the April 2026 SDR incident and the exact language we never use. Read it once for the values; come back to it whenever you're about to write outbound copy.

---

## 1. The six values, in order

These describe how we already work, not how we hope to. Recite them by name.

| #   | Value                         | The one-line read                                                                                               |
| --- | ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 01  | **WE GO FIRST**               | No playbook exists. Make one. Listen to the customer in front of you and build the response the moment demands. |
| 02  | **FIGURE IT OUT**             | Stuck is a starting point, not a destination. First-principle your way out before you ask.                      |
| 03  | **BET ON PEOPLE**             | Back people fully, give them ownership, get out of the way — your teammates _and_ your customers.               |
| 04  | **BE HONEST. FULL STOP**      | Sugar-coating is a kindness that kills. The most important value for sales and AM work.                         |
| 05  | **DREAM UNTIL IT SCARES YOU** | IPO. Global. Top SMB company built from India. Sell at that altitude.                                           |
| 06  | **SKIN IN THE GAME**          | We only win when the customer wins. The close is not the win.                                                   |

> [!KEY TERM] **The values filter** — six questions you run, in order, when no policy covers the situation. Most decisions resolve in the first two. The bad ones almost always fail at #1 (does this serve the customer winning?) or #2 (am I being completely honest?).

## 2. Value 01 — WE GO FIRST

Nobody handed us the map, so we made one. There is no senior comparable to copy, because half the deals in front of you are shapes we haven't seen yet. The instinct to ask "what's the playbook?" is the instinct to resist — usually there isn't one, so you build it and document it for the next rep.

> [!FOR THE AE] When a prospect asks for something we've never offered (a contract structure, a service category we haven't trained for), you don't say "we don't do that." You say _"let me come back tomorrow with a yes or a clean no."_ Then you go get the answer and write the FAQ entry that didn't exist yesterday.

> [!TALK TRACK] **Prospect asks something we've never handled** — "Honest answer — that specific situation is one we haven't run before. Let me come back to you tomorrow with a yes or a clean no, not a maybe. I want to talk to our team and give you the straight answer rather than guess."

---

## 3. Value 02 — FIGURE IT OUT

Pressure is the input; figured-out is the output, and you're paid to bridge the two. You'll hit walls in your first 30 days — a confused CRM, a product question you can't answer, a demo going sideways. The expected response isn't "I'm stuck, who can help me?" It's "here are the three things I tried, here's what I think the right move is, and here's the one question I still need answered."

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] Slacking the team five minutes into a problem. Don't. The sequence is: search the doc, search Slack history, form a hypothesis, _then_ ask — and lead with what you tried. "I'm stuck on X. I tried A, B, C. My best guess is D. The thing I can't resolve is Y." That ask earns help in 30 seconds. The bare question earns "did you check the doc?"

---

## 4. Value 03 — BET ON PEOPLE

This value runs in two directions. **Inside Zoca**, you'll get ownership earlier than you ever have — deals, customers, decisions, dollar figures — and nobody will micromanage how you get there (and nobody will rescue you, see value 02). **Outside Zoca**, it's how you treat customers: the salon owner is a real person taking real risk on you. Treat them like founders who happen to do hair instead of building software. Most of them are.

> [!FOR THE AE] A struggling customer asks for a non-standard concession. Evaluate the _customer_, not the policy. If they're the kind of operator worth backing, back them and figure out how to make it work. And when a teammate is running their first big multi-location deal — give them what you know, don't steal the deal, let them run it.

---

## 5. Value 04 — BE HONEST. FULL STOP

> [!WHY IT MATTERS] Every other value is about how you _build_. This one is about how you _sell_ — which makes it the most important value in your job. You'll be tempted daily: to oversell what the product does today, to promise a Coming Soon feature as if it ships next week, to push a lower-fit prospect across the line because quota is right there, to soften bad news because the conversation is awkward. Each temptation is exactly what this value exists to prevent.

**The hard rules that come out of value 04:**

- **Set fit expectations honestly.** If a prospect is a lower fit, you say so on the discovery call and set the real expectation — you don't oversell them across the line. The honest read today builds the referral tomorrow. (See _Our ICP — best-fit, not a hard no._)
- **Don't promise features that don't exist.** Discovery Agent and Front Desk Agent are fully live. Conversion Agent is partial. Booking, Loyalty, and Ads are visible in the sidebar so the owner can see where we're headed — they're **Coming Soon**, sold as upside, never with a launch date. "Coming Soon" means coming soon, not "if you sign today."
- **Bad news goes to the customer first, not last.** If a GBP reverted, a rebook number is down, a campaign underperformed — the customer hears it from you in plain English before they spot it themselves. The customer who hears bad news from you stays; the one who finds it themselves churns.
- **Use the right contract language.** The public phrasing is **"no long-term contracts."** It is **not** "no contracts" — we do sign service agreements at trial start, and the shorter line contradicts our actual practice.

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] Saying "no contracts" on a discovery call to make the offer feel friction-free. This is the exact phrasing the April compliance review flagged. Say **"no long-term contracts"** every time — the two extra words protect the company and keep you honest.

> [!TALK TRACK] **Contract phrasing** — "No long-term contracts — month-to-month at `[monthly plan price]`, or sign on the year at `[annual plan price]`. Either way the service agreement gets signed at trial start, like any other vendor relationship."

> [!TALK TRACK] **"Is feature X live today?"** — "Honest answer — Discovery Agent and Front Desk Agent are fully live today. Conversion Agent has the funnel detail page working but the rest is partial. Booking, Loyalty, and Ads are visible in the sidebar so you can see where we're headed, but they're on the roadmap, not in your dashboard yet. I won't tell you they ship next week. They don't."

> [!TALK TRACK] **Something we did caused a problem** — "That happened on our end. I'm not going to dress it up. Here's what I'm doing about it today — [specific] — here's what's changing this week — [specific] — and if you want to talk through whether the relationship is still working for you, I'll set that up tomorrow. No defensiveness."

## 6. Compliance discipline — the language we never use

This is the home for our outbound-messaging compliance rules. They all came out of one real incident, and value 04 is the reason it got documented and fixed instead of buried.

> [!COMPLIANCE NOTE] **The April 2026 SDR incident.** Team members reconciling missed-payment accounts found that a paid customer being chased on payment recovery had _also_ received a sales SMS. Investigation surfaced **34 sales SMS messages sent to currently-paid customers in April**. The copy contained Zoca branding, performance-shaming language, and "no contracts" phrasing. The export filter meant to exclude paid accounts hadn't been correctly applied. Several of those accounts subsequently went into payment trouble.

**What the team did, in order — and why it's structural, not "be more careful":**

1. Immediate moratorium on all cold-calling and outbound SDR SMS from the onboarding team until fixes landed.
2. SMS sends consolidated through one person — stopped depending on SDRs to filter paid accounts out of lists.
3. Built an **automated exclusion filter** on the sending system: paid accounts and active-trial accounts are hard-excluded **at send time**, not at list-prep time. Belt and suspenders.
4. Mandatory compliance training for anyone authoring SDR copy, before the next send.
5. All SDR copy reviewed for Zoca branding (removed in cold copy) and performance-shaming language (removed everywhere); public contract copy fixed.

**The rules that came out of it — non-negotiable:**

- **Paid customers are never in the SDR pool.** Active-trial accounts are never in the SDR pool. Do not send sales messaging to paid customers. If you're ever unsure whether someone is a prospect or a customer, **ask before you send.**
- **No performance-shaming language, anywhere, in any channel.** These are **banned word-for-word**: _"you are not ranking,"_ _"you have been MIA,"_ _"you ghosted us."_ You're talking to a person running a business, not a delinquent.
- **No "free demo / free trial to paid customers" framing.** Several paid customers got this exact line in April. It implies their current paid usage is inferior — actively harmful, banned.
- **Different cohorts, different rules.** "Demo-attended but not paid" copy _may_ mention Zoca branding. Paid-account copy cannot. Cold/unattended-prospect copy cannot.

> [!RULE OF THUMB] Read every rule in this section as the cost of one bad list export. The banned phrases are spelled out word-for-word so the next rep can't repeat them by accident. When in doubt about who's on the other end of a send — stop and ask.

## 7. Value 05 — DREAM UNTIL IT SCARES YOU

If the dream doesn't keep you up at night, it isn't big enough. The stated dream is explicit: **IPO. Global. The top-5 SMB company ever built from India.** Dream it first, earn it after. The dream isn't decoration; it's direction.

This shows up in your job two ways:

- **Match your ambition to the company's.** Every quarter the bar rises, every year the deal sizes grow, every cohort raises the floor. The people who thrive here personally want to build something they'll tell their kids about. A comfortable mid-tier sales job is the wrong room.
- **Let the dream show up in how you sell.** The customers you sign aren't buying "another vendor" — they're betting on a company that intends to be the operating system for beauty globally. The pitch is bigger than the price tag.

> [!TALK TRACK] **"Where will Zoca be in five years?"** — "IPO. Global. We want to be the operating system every beauty business in the world runs on. That's not a slide — that's what we're building toward. What it means for you: we'll still be here in five years, the product depth will keep widening, and the price you lock in this year is the floor."

## 8. Value 06 — SKIN IN THE GAME

We only win when the customer wins — that's the whole deal. This value draws the line between a **closed-won deal** and a **successful customer**, which are not the same thing. A close that doesn't turn into a growing customer is a loss; it just shows up two months later as a churn instead of a "no thanks" on the discovery call. The dollars are the same; the wasted trust is worse.

You're measured on what _stays_ signed and grows. That's why fit discipline matters, why honesty matters, and why the MBR cadence is part of the product — every month you account, face to face, for whether the customer is growing because of you.

> [!FOR THE AE] A paying customer hasn't opened the dashboard 30 days in. The day-4-no-open trigger fires a mandatory AE call — you don't skip it. A renewal is coming and the numbers are flat? You raise it first, in the MBR, and propose the fix. You don't wait for them to notice and churn quietly.

> [!TALK TRACK] **Your bonus is on the line and the customer isn't ready** — "I want to be straight with you — I could push you to sign for the second location this month. But I don't think you're ready. Your existing location's MBR numbers are flat two months in a row. Let's get those moving first. The right deal next quarter is worth more than the wrong deal this quarter. I'd rather wait."

> [!RULE OF THUMB] If your manager pushes back on waiting, push back. Skin in the game means you don't pull a deal forward at the customer's expense. Customers who succeed refer more, expand more, and renew higher — the math always works out for the rep who waits.

## 9. The values filter — using them when no policy exists

On Day 30, 60, 90 you'll hit situations no document covers. Run them through the six questions, in order:

1. **Does this serve the customer winning?** (06) — if no, stop.
2. **Am I being completely honest about what I'm offering?** (04) — if no, fix it before you continue.
3. **Have I tried to figure this out myself first?** (02) — if no, do that before escalating.
4. **Is this the boring safe move, or the move the moment demands?** (01) — if safe, push.
5. **Am I betting on the right people, including this customer?** (03)
6. **Am I dreaming big enough about what this could become?** (05)

> [!SALON EXAMPLE] **Sara, last Wednesday of the quarter.** One deal short. Mariana — a solo lash artist, single location, lower fit — is excited and would sign tonight, putting Sara at quota. But Sara reads it honestly: Mariana would pay for capacity she can't fill and likely churn at 60–90 days. Sara runs the filter. Q1: no, this doesn't serve Mariana winning. Q2: no, closing would mean softening the fit read she'd normally give. So Sara calls Mariana, levels with her — "save the budget, come back when you hire your first tech, I'll move fast for you" — and messages her manager naming the lost quota and the why. Three months later Mariana crosses six figures, comes back, _and_ refers a 3-location studio. Sara's commission on the two deals is several times the bad deal. The manager who was unhappy that quarter quotes Sara's example in the next team training.

> [!GOOD TO KNOW] Six questions; most decisions resolve in two. Sara didn't try to negotiate the fit rules _with herself_ — she ran the filter straight and told her manager before he noticed. Bad news first, even internally.
