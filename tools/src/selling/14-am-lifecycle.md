# The account-management lifecycle — handoff to renewal

> [!TL;DR] The sale is the start line, not the finish. Once a customer signs, the relationship moves from the AE to **account management** — and the first 30 days set whether they renew or churn. This lesson walks the post-sale timeline end to end: the **AE→AM Day-1 handoff** (what you inherit, the 4-hour and 24-hour clocks, red-flag triage), the **Day 8 orientation call**, the **early-warning signals** that mean pull CX in now, the **"owner isn't logging in" recovery play**, the **14-day check-in**, the **$5K ACV split** that decides who owns the account (under $5K = Customer Success Pod; $5K+ = a dedicated named AM), the **monthly engagement loop and the MBR cadence**, and finally the **churn-prevention play** — the human save conversation when a customer asks to cancel. Most first-month cancellations are expectation problems, not product problems. Your job across this whole arc is to keep the agreement intact and the value visible.

> [!SOURCE NOTE] The billing/subscription **mechanics** — the single consolidated subscription, upgrade/downgrade behavior, the dedicated cancellation flow order — are owned by the **Pricing, packaging, closing & billing** lesson. This lesson owns the **human retention play**: spotting churn risk and running the save conversation. Cross-references below point you to the mechanics; don't re-learn them here.

## 1. The lifecycle at a glance

| When                    | Touchpoint                                           | Who runs it   | Goal                                             |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Within 4 hrs of signing | Welcome / intro email                                | AM or Pod     | Customer hears from us — sets the tone forever   |
| Within 24 hrs           | 8-item confirmation list + "what to expect" briefing | AM or Pod     | Lock contact/billing, manage 30-day expectations |
| First 2 weeks           | Watch the early-escalation signals                   | AM + CX       | Catch the wall in week 1, not week 3             |
| Day 8                   | Orientation call (30 min, 6-item agenda)             | Lane owner    | Lock goal + priority services + webinars         |
| ~Day 14                 | Check-in call (a **different** voice)                | Different rep | First reality check; fix blockers on the call    |
| Monthly                 | Engagement loop + MBR (booking window 7th–15th)      | AM or Pod     | Keep value visible; review + plan                |
| On cancel request       | Churn-prevention call                                | CX Pod        | Listen, diagnose, run the right reframe          |

> [!KEY TERM] **AM (Account Manager)** — owns the customer relationship after the AE closes. **CX** — the customer-experience function pulled in on risk signals to run recovery plays. **Pod** — the shared Customer Success team that owns sub-$5K accounts collectively.

## 2. Day 1 — inheriting the deal

The AE did the heavy lift of selling. You inherit a customer who has **already agreed to spend money**. Your job is to keep that agreement intact through the first 30 days, **not to re-sell**.

**What you inherit — the handoff doc (6 items).** If any are missing, go back to the AE _before_ you take ownership:

1. **Deal terms** — annual or monthly (`[annual plan price]` / `[monthly plan price]`), add-ons (Bookings & Payments, Loyalty, Conversion), start date.
2. **Primary goal** — the ONE leak the AE promised to fix first (the prospect's biggest pain). This is your North Star for 30 days.
3. **Top 5 priority services** — what they want to rank for and answer calls about first.
4. **Agent activation milestones** — Discovery Agent + Front Desk Agent (the Discovery Neo bundle) activate in onboarding; Conversion/Loyalty add-ons run 2–3 weeks behind.
5. **Red flags** — anything fragile: an unsold partner, a past bad experience, a compliance worry, a pending legal review.
6. **Decision-maker contacts** — email + phone for the person who signs the bills (not the front desk).

> [!RULE OF THUMB] **The 4-hour clock and the 24-hour clock.** The customer must hear from you (or the pod) within **4 hours** of signing — even a one-line intro. Within **24 hours**, confirm the 8-item list: decision-maker email, decision-maker phone, billing email, payment method, Day 8 orientation calendared, preferred name + pronunciation, time zone (don't assume Eastern), red flags surfaced to your lane lead.

The "what to expect" briefing — sent end of day 1 — is the single most effective churn-prevention tool you have. It tells the customer the dashboard will look empty for 5–7 days (that's normal), first content posts around Day 4, first reviews around Day 6, first measurable lift around Days 21–28, MBR at month-end.

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] "Silence is fine, they just signed, they're happy." No. If a freshly-signed customer hears nothing for 48 hours, they assume Zoca is slow and start doubting the decision. Most 30-day cancellations are **expectation** failures seeded in the first 48 hours.

**Red-flag triage (do this in the first 14 days, don't wait for it to surface):**

| Red flag from AE                        | The risk                               | The AM mitigation                                                           |
| --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Legal review pending                    | Cancels if attorney pushes back        | Re-send the agreement in writing; offer a 15-min legal call                 |
| Partner/co-owner not sold               | Partner kills it at month 2            | Invite the partner to the Day 8 orientation; don't run it without both      |
| Bad past experience (agency/competitor) | Any friction reads as "same as before" | Name it explicitly; promise visible week-1 movement                         |
| State compliance worry                  | Trips a TCPA edge case                 | Schedule a compliance-lead call before they activate the review flow        |
| Pricing was a sticking point            | Cancels at month 2 without lift        | Lower the 14-day bar — confirm they see _content + calls_, not just numbers |

## 3. The first two weeks — signals CX should be pulled in early

Most customers roll through onboarding fine. A meaningful minority hit a wall. Spotting it in **week 1 instead of week 3 roughly doubles** your odds of saving the relationship.

- **Signal 1 — 48+ hours of silence after handoff.** No reply, no kickoff booking, no pickup. Intervene with a **phone call**, not another email.
- **Signal 2 — GBP verification stuck.** Without Google Business Profile access, the Discovery Agent can't start. CX has fast-track Google contacts — loop them in same-day.
- **Signal 3 — first Front Desk Agent draft rejected: "that's not how I'd say it."** High-leverage moment. Schedule a 20-min persona-tuning call same-day; edit 5–6 sample replies together to rebuild trust in the voice.
- **Signal 4 — a bad first inbound.** First real call the Front Desk Agent handles is a complaint or edge case and it fumbles (or the owner fears it did). Replay it together; fix or normalize.
- **Signal 5 — any legal/compliance question.** Goes straight to **compliance** — not CX, not you. Same-day.
- **Signal 6 — "my partner/co-owner isn't sold."** A co-decision-maker with veto power can kill the deal at month 1. Offer a 15-min call to re-run the value framing.
- **Signal 7 — "this is more complicated than I thought."** Verbatim overwhelm. Offer a concierge re-kickoff; simplify scope; rebuild momentum with small wins.

> [!FOR THE AE] When you flag a signal to CX, send a 4-part package: (1) the signal (quote it), (2) sale context CX doesn't have, (3) your honest gut call on whether it's save-worthy, (4) time-sensitivity (same-day vs this-week).

## 4. Day 8 — the orientation call

Day 8 is deliberate. Day 3 is too early — there's nothing to orient against. Day 14 is too late — habits are already formed. By Day 8 the customer has seen first GBP posts and first Front Desk calls, has questions, and hasn't yet decided the product is "for them." Orientation nudges that toward yes.

Run **6 items in 30 minutes** — you don't have to go in order, but hit all six:

1. **Confirm the primary goal** — re-confirm the AE's leak; if it's changed, recalibrate now (write it into the dashboard "Primary Goal" field live).
2. **Top 5 priority services** — 2–3 highest-revenue, 1–2 they want to grow, 1 underperformer. Type them in as they speak.
3. **Agent activation milestones** — confirm Discovery + Front Desk are live; name any blocker out loud; set honest timelines for add-ons.
4. **Webinar opt-in** — add the two weekly webinars to their calendar **by default**, let them decline. Tell them missed sessions get an emailed written summary so they don't feel guilt-tripped.
5. **Follow-up phone call decision** — decide _on the call_ whether one's needed; if yes, name the topic and calendar 15 min within 5 business days.
6. **MBR pointer** — open the MBR panel, show the empty state, explain the monthly cadence.

> [!TALK TRACK] **The opening.** "This is your Day 8 orientation — the same one every Zoca customer gets a week after activation. We'll confirm the goal, lock your priority services, walk through which agents activate when, get you opted into our webinars, and decide if we need a phone call after this. End of 30 minutes, everything's written in the dashboard so we're not relying on memory."

> [!RULE OF THUMB] **Type in front of the customer.** When the goal, the services, and the next steps land in the product live, orientation stops being "a meeting we'll forget" and becomes a working session. Recap email goes out within **2 hours**, always.

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] Running orientation as office hours — "what questions do you have?" at minute 1. The call drifts and you miss 4 of 6 items. The customer has 30 days for questions; orientation is for **orienting**. Bring a wanderer back to the agenda within 30 seconds.

## 5. The "owner isn't logging in" recovery play

Login activity is the **most predictive churn signal** we have. The pattern: Week 1 has 3+ logins (healthy exploring); Week 2 drops to 0–1; Week 3 still 0–1. When that trajectory fires, run the play.

**Why owners stop logging in — and the right response:**

| Reason                                  | Tell                                          | Recovery                                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| It's working, they're just not checking | Approvals firing, bookings coming in          | Good sign. Send a proactive weekly report — make value visible                             |
| "I thought it just runs by itself"      | Owner says exactly that                       | Re-orient on the approval queue; set notification preferences                              |
| Overwhelmed by options                  | Dashboard feels complex                       | Tour the 3 things that matter: approval queue, review stream, weekly report. Hide the rest |
| Buyer's remorse                         | Silence + defensive + no approvals            | Honest fit conversation; offer the off-ramp                                                |
| Running on assistant/staff              | Owner absent but front desk handles approvals | Neutral-to-positive. Confirm staff is empowered; send owner the weekly digest              |

> [!TALK TRACK] **The recovery call.** "I noticed you haven't been in the dashboard for about a week — totally normal, owners vary a lot. I just wanted to make sure nothing's blocking you, and show you one thing that might surprise you." Then show 1–2 wins they missed: _"Since last Thursday, Front Desk Agent handled 11 inquiries — 6 booked."_ / _"Your GBP climbed 3 spots."_ / _"8 new reviews this week vs. 2 last week."_

For time-starved owners, a **weekly email report** can replace the dashboard as the main surface (inquiries handled, bookings captured, reviews, GBP views, "things to review" count). If an owner says "just email me," enthusiastically say yes — don't force a login habit.

> [!GOOD TO KNOW] **The escalation threshold.** If, after the recovery call + 2 weekly reports, the owner is still silent at 0 logins: AE + CX join a call for an honest fit conversation, offering either (a) a free month to re-prove value or (b) a graceful exit. No guilt, no upsell. Counterintuitive truth: customers we let go gracefully at month 1–2 are the **most likely to return** in 6–12 months. Forced-to-stay customers never do.

## 6. Day 14 — the check-in

Day 8 was orientation. Day 14 is the **first reality check**, run by a **different person** on purpose — the customer hears two voices, which signals there's a team, and it gives the orientation notes a fresh second read.

Check three things: **dashboard engagement** (healthy = 3+ logins in 7 days across 3+ screens; risk = 0–1, which triggers the mandatory call), **blocker patterns** (usually 1–2 things, not 10), and **sentiment** (tone is data — resigned at Day 14 means churn at month 2 without intervention).

**The three recovery plays — run them _on the call_:**

- **Play 1 — Owner hasn't logged in (mandatory call).** Zero opens since onboarding = a call within 24 hours. Do a 10-minute screen-share, log in _with_ them, navigate the 3 screens that matter for their goal, bookmark the dashboard, set a daily reminder. Refusal to do the walkthrough is itself a risk signal — surface it to your lane lead.
- **Play 2 — "Calls coming in but not booking" (Front Desk audit).** Pull transcripts together. Check: calls where the AI couldn't pull live availability (fix the booking integration), wrong service answers (update the training prompts), escalations a human missed (set forwarding rules), after-hours calls (confirm voicemail + callback). Most fixes are 5-minute config edits.
- **Play 3 — "Reviews not flowing."** The review SMS only fires when the booking platform reports an appointment completed. Pull up the B&P integration; if it's broken, escalate to the platform team with account ID, booking platform, and date range — set the expectation that the fix lands in ~5 business days.

> [!SALON EXAMPLE] A barbershop owner is frustrated that the Front Desk Agent "doesn't know what a fade is." The AM doesn't defend the AI — she pulls a transcript, finds the service catalog has only "haircut / kids cut / beard trim," and adds the fade variants (skin/mid/high/low/taper, line-up, hot towel) **right there on the call** as the owner dictates prices. Eight minutes, frustrated → engaged. The 14-day call is a working session, not a status meeting.

## 7. Who owns the account — the $5K ACV split

Zoca's customer base splits into two lanes by **Annual Contract Value (ACV)**, decided automatically at the moment of payment success — **before** orientation, which determines who runs it.

|                    | **Under $5K ACV — Customer Success Pod**                 | **$5K ACV and above — Dedicated AM**              |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Ownership          | Shared inbox; pod-based routing                          | One named AM (name + direct work phone)           |
| MBR booking        | **Reactive** — customer books from the dashboard         | **Proactive** — AM reaches out to book each month |
| Email "From" field | "Support" → sign off "the Customer Success team"         | The AM's name → sign off as yourself              |
| Point of contact   | Whoever picks up the ticket (account history pre-loaded) | Single named owner, end-to-end                    |

> [!RULE OF THUMB] **What stays the same in BOTH lanes:** all the Get in Touch channels (Ashley the AI account manager / ticketing / Command Center / email), the Day 8 orientation, the 14-day check-in, the monthly engagement loop, the MBR mechanism, and webinar opt-ins. The split is about **who is behind the channel, not which channels exist.** A sub-$5K customer does not get _less_ support — they get the same support, structured as a pod.

> [!TALK TRACK] **Lane confusion ("I thought I had an account manager?").** "Our dedicated AMs work with the larger multi-location accounts. You've got our Customer Success Pod — a small team sharing the inbox. Same product, same MBR cadence, same check-ins. Whoever picks up has your account loaded, so you don't repeat yourself, and response time is actually faster than one named person who might be on a call." Never apologize for the pod and never say "you don't qualify."

> [!GOOD TO KNOW] **ACV expansion handoff.** If a pod-managed account is upsold past $5K (e.g., adds Loyalty + Conversion), it moves to a dedicated AM. The pod flags it, a named AM is assigned within 5 business days, pod + new AM co-write the announcement email, and the new AM books a 30-min intro within 7 days — walking in with the account history loaded so the customer never feels the disruption.

> [!GOOD TO KNOW] **Outside working hours**, both lanes see the same thing: a few predefined callback slots for the next business day. Open scheduling builds up queues; the fixed-slot pattern keeps the calendar clean and response time predictable.

## 8. The monthly engagement loop and the MBR

**Monthly engagement ≠ MBR** — this is the most-confused topic in the AM track.

- **Monthly engagement loop** = the _ongoing_ rhythm: relationship check-ins, blocker follow-up, churn-risk handling, and webinars. Continuous, all month, every paid customer.
- **MBR (Monthly Business Review)** = a _discrete_ 30-minute event, once a month inside the booking window, with a recording, an AI summary, and a Highlights & Actions list.

> [!KEY TERM] **CX vs. Product communications — keep the streams separate.** You (AM/pod) run **relationship** comms — check-ins, blocker follow-up, churn-risk, account coordination. **Product/Marketing** runs feature announcements and in-product education. You should NOT be the channel feature releases reach the customer through; if you forget to mention a launch, the customer thinks Zoca is opaque. You own the relationship; the product channel owns the announcements.

**Cadence between MBRs** is light and phone-first (meetings are a heavy ask you earn through value): roughly one brief mid-month phone check-in if anything's worth surfacing, one webinar invite, email when there's a fix or heads-up, and one MBR booked between the 7th and 15th. Don't over-touch.

**MBR mechanics:** booking window opens the **1st**, schedulable slots run the **7th–15th**, closes the **15th**; four daily slots (9 / 11 / 2 / 4 local); Day 25 email reminder + Day 27 calendar reminder; a missed MBR reopens booking on the 5th of the next month. The MBR panel lives in permanent left-rail real estate, one row per month, in one of four states — **Completed** (recording + AI summary + Highlights & Actions), **Scheduled**, **Missed** (the existing Monthly Performance Report PDF stands in so the customer gets the data even without the conversation), or **Booking-open/closed**.

The **30-minute MBR** runs: opening + agenda (0–2), last month's numbers (2–10), what worked / what didn't (10–18), next month's actions (18–25), lock **Highlights & Actions** in writing (25–28), confirm next booking + close (28–30). Highlights & Actions is a rigid numbered list of 5–8 single-sentence items, each pairing a performance observation with an agreed action — so a future AM can _scan_ the history, not parse prose.

> [!VALUE LINE] The MBR panel is your renewal tool. Six months of numbers, side by side, is the single clearest answer to "is this worth it?" — which is exactly what you reach for in a churn-prevention call (Section 9, Reframe 2).

## 9. Churn prevention — the retention play

> [!KEY TERM] **What churn actually is.** A customer is churning only when they say **"I want to cancel"** (or on non-payment offboarding). A 14-day silence is not churn. A flat MBR is not churn. Those are issues to _fix_. Don't conflate an issue with a cancellation.

When a customer requests cancellation, a churn-prevention call comes first (the cancellation flow order and grace mechanics live in the **Pricing, packaging, closing & billing** lesson — this section is the human conversation). If they don't book the call, CX still attempts to reach them within 48 hours. We never let a customer go without offering a conversation.

**The first 5 minutes — listen, don't pitch.**

> [!TALK TRACK] **The open.** "Thanks for taking the call. I'm not here to pressure you to stay. I'm here to listen and help you make the right call for your business. Tell me what's driving the cancellation." Then say nothing for ~2 minutes — most customers spill all 1–3 reasons. Then play it back: _"Three things — let me make sure I heard you right: (1)… (2)… (3)… Did I miss anything?"_ This surfaces hidden reasons (the surface reason is "price" but the real one is "my partner pushed back").

**The four reframes — diagnose, then pick:**

| Reframe            | When                                                                      | The move                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Fix-it**      | A specific blocker (Front Desk fumble, broken integration, content drift) | "That's fixable — let me pull up your dashboard, we'll fix it in 10 minutes. If you still want to cancel after, we cancel, no pressure." Demonstrate the fix; don't just promise it                                                |
| **2. Value**       | They don't see the lift                                                   | Open the MBR panel: "Let me show you 6 months — [MBR #1 numbers] vs. [now]. The question is whether the lift justifies the cost for you. If the answer's no, we don't continue — but let's look together"                          |
| **3. Cash flow**   | They love it but can't pay now                                            | "Cancel today and we preserve your configuration — re-subscribe in 5 minutes when you're ready, no re-onboarding. Or keep it active and we revisit in 30 days. Which is right?"                                                    |
| **4. Let-them-go** | Wrong-fit ICP — the volume for real lift was never there                  | "Honest answer — at your stage the lift was never going to be huge. I'm going to recommend you cancel. When you're at your second chair or your next revenue threshold, call me. I'll note your file. No outbound in the meantime" |

> [!RULE OF THUMB] **Never discount to save churn.** Discounting trains both sides badly — the customer remembers they paid less forever, and at renewal when it expires they churn anyway, with bad blood. The honest "pause and come back" path works better. There is no discount mechanism in the product.

> [!FOR THE AE] **ICP is best-fit, not a hard no — and the let-them-go move is the proof.** Recommending a wrong-fit solo owner cancel costs you one save and builds years of trust. They tell peers "Zoca told me to cancel — that's a real company," and the referrals come back. This is Zoca Value 04: **be honest, full stop.** It's also what protects us from the month-3 churn-with-bad-blood pattern.

> [!SALON EXAMPLE] A lash-bar owner asks to cancel at month 5: "The product's fine, I just had to put my partner through college and I'm cash-strapped." The pod member diagnoses **cash flow, not value**, offers the preserve-and-re-subscribe path (no discount), and walks her through the cancellation page transparently. She re-subscribed ~10 weeks later and continued for many more months. A discount would have made her the resentful full-price customer who churns when it expires.

> [!RULE OF THUMB] **When you DO save a customer, book a 14-day follow-up.** A save without a follow-up re-churns at month 2. Restart the subscription on the backend so they see no payment glitch, then confirm the fix actually closed.

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] Hiding the cancellation page or making the customer chase through three handoffs. Don't. Show them the path transparently — customers who feel **trapped** cancel faster than customers who feel **free**. (The cancellation page UI and flow steps live in the billing lesson.)

## 10. The numbers you can quote (and the ones you can't)

> [!COMPLIANCE NOTE] **Pricing is pending final confirmation — never invent a figure.** Use the literal placeholders `[annual plan price]` / `[monthly plan price]`. The stale numbers $179 / $249 / $269 / $279 / $290 are dead — never quote them. There is a **setup fee, waived only on the yearly plan** (the waiver is baked into annual pricing) — frame it as a reason to go annual; never state the amount and never say "no setup fee." Add-on pricing that _is_ final: Bookings & Payments = free monthly + 2.7%/transaction; Loyalty Agent = $49/mo (incl. 500 SMS + 100 AI calls); Conversion Agent = $49/mo + 10% of recovered booking value (requires B&P connected). The one number in _this_ lesson that is fixed: the **$5K ACV split** between the Pod lane and the dedicated-AM lane.

## 11. Key terms

- **AM (Account Manager)** — owns the relationship post-close; keeps the agreement intact through the first 30 days and beyond.
- **The handoff doc (6 items)** — deal terms, primary goal, top 5 priority services, agent activation milestones, red flags, decision-maker contacts. Missing any → go back to the AE before taking ownership.
- **4-hour / 24-hour clocks** — customer hears from us within 4 hours of signing; the 8-item confirmation list is clean within 24.
- **Primary goal** — the ONE leak the customer wants fixed first; the 30-day North Star, tracked through to MBR.
- **The early-escalation signals** — 48h silence, GBP verification stuck, rejected first Front Desk draft, bad first inbound, legal question, unsold co-owner, "more complicated than I thought."
- **Day 8 orientation** — universal 30-min call, 6-item agenda, everything captured in writing.
- **14-day check-in** — first reality check, a different voice; recovery plays run on the call.
- **ACV (Annual Contract Value)** — monthly recurring + add-ons + transaction revenue, annualized. The $5K split is based on it.
- **Pod lane (under $5K)** — shared inbox, reactive MBR, "Support" From field. **Dedicated-AM lane ($5K+)** — named owner, proactive MBR, named From field.
- **ACV expansion handoff** — clean move from pod to dedicated AM when an account crosses $5K.
- **Monthly engagement loop vs. MBR** — the continuous rhythm vs. the discrete monthly 30-min review.
- **MBR** — Monthly Business Review; booking window 7th–15th, 4 slots, recording + AI summary + Highlights & Actions.
- **The 4 reframes** — fix-it, value, cash-flow, let-them-go.
- **Wrong-fit ICP** — a customer who never should have been sold; the honest move is to recommend cancellation and leave the door open.
- **Ashley** — the AI account manager behind Get in Touch (same across both lanes).
