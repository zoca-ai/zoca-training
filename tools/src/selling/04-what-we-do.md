# What we do — Discovery Neo & the agent architecture

> [!TL;DR] **Discovery Neo is the product** — and it's _one_ product, not six. Every new customer gets two agents bundled together: the **Discovery Agent** (the marketing brain — gets the business found) and the **Front Desk Agent** (the voice brain — answers every call and books it). They aren't two tools stitched together; they share **one brain** — the same customer context — which is the whole moat against legacy "booking platform + AI plugin" setups. The way they work is **supervised autopilot**: the agents _act_ on the owner's behalf and surface the big calls for approval. The owner isn't pushing buttons — they're managing a team that keeps working while they're in a chair, asleep, or on vacation. Pitch it as a **team, not a tool**, and you win the demo.

---

## 1. The product is Discovery Neo

Discovery Neo is what every new logo gets. It replaces the older Zoca product entirely for new customers. You'll hear tenured folks say "Discovery," "the new product," or "Neo" — all the same thing. To a prospect, the name is **Discovery Neo** or just **Zoca**. Never introduce internal codenames in a customer conversation.

What sits inside it:

- **Discovery Agent** — the marketing brain. Owns SEO, content, Google Business Profile, reviews, AI-search visibility, and the website.
- **Front Desk Agent** — the voice brain. Owns inbound calls, bookings, reschedules, FAQs, and outbound follow-ups.

Plus two customer-facing surfaces that are _part of the product_, not separate add-ons:

- **Get in Touch / Ashley** — the AI account manager available inside the dashboard.
- **MBR (Monthly Business Reviews)** — the recurring 30-minute review that lives in the dashboard.

Two agents, two surfaces, one platform. That's the whole product.

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] "Zoca has six products." Wrong. **Zoca has one product.** The sidebar shows future agents (Booking, Conversion, Loyalty, Ads), but they're roadmap — not six things you buy. Anyone pitching "six products" is confusing the sidebar for the offer.

> [!SAY IT SIMPLY] "Zoca is one platform that handles your marketing and your phone. The Discovery Agent gets you found on Google and inside ChatGPT, automatically, every week. The Front Desk Agent picks up every call in under a second, books it, and calls back the people who dropped off mid-booking." Practice that until it's one breath.

## 2. The two agents

### Discovery Agent — the marketing brain

Everything that gets the business found — run by the agent on autopilot, with the owner approving when something needs a human call.

- **Google Business Profile.** Audits it on day one, fixes what's missing, posts 3×/week, syncs hours and contact info, manages categories. The owner stops logging into Google.
- **Website.** Builds a fully SEO-optimized site on a custom subdomain — service pages, hero, booking CTAs, blog, schema markup — editable in a visual editor.
- **Keywords.** Tracks rankings per service, per trade area; prioritizes by volume and opportunity.
- **Content.** Pre-generates a 90-day content calendar; publishes blog posts, GBP posts, and social-ready content tuned to the local trade area and the actual services sold.
- **Reviews.** Triggers a review request after every checkout; AI-drafts the response to new reviews; owner approves or auto-posts depending on settings.
- **AI-search visibility.** Writes per-location, per-service content in the structured format ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews actually cite.

> [!SOURCE NOTE] The "41% of beauty searches now run through AI search" figure is an internal benchmark — use the _shape_ of the trend confidently, and flag it as confirm-before-quoting if a prospect presses for a hard source.

### Front Desk Agent — the voice brain

The phone — inbound, outbound, and the website chat widget, all the same brain across all three channels.

- **Picks up in under one second. Every call. 24/7.** No IVR, no "press 1" — a conversation.
- **Books confirmed appointments in under two minutes.** Knows the services, stylists, pricing, policies, and rebook windows by name.
- **Handles reschedules, cancellations, deposits, directions, upsells** — without a human.
- **Multilingual from day one — 12 languages**; same concierge, in the customer's language.
- **Outbound rebook calls.** When a regular's rebook window opens and they haven't booked, it calls them.
- **Black Box recovery.** When a customer drops mid-booking on the web, it reaches out within minutes — not 48 hours later — with the right time, the right stylist, the right next-best option.

> [!VALUE LINE] Missed-call rate goes from an industry-typical **~22% down to ~3%** on a typical Zoca network, with sub-1-second pickup. (Internal benchmark — flag the source as needing an update if a prospect wants it documented.)

## 3. One brain — the part competitors can't copy

This matters more than it sounds. In every other tool the prospect has tried, the marketing system and the phone system are two separate things — different vendors, different data, different customer records.

In Zoca, both agents pull from the **same context layer**. The Discovery Agent knows what the Front Desk Agent said on the call yesterday. The Front Desk Agent knows the customer came in from a "balayage near me" search, has booked twice before, last visited Feb 14, and has a sensitive scalp on file.

> [!WHY IT MATTERS] Shared memory across phone, web, and chat is what makes the recovery rate, the rebook rate, and the conversion numbers _real_. No agency, no point tool, and no legacy "booking + AI plugin" stitches that together cleanly today. This is the moat — lead with it.

> [!OBJECTION] **"Can I just buy the Front Desk Agent?"** — "No, the two come together, and here's why that matters: they share one customer context layer. The Discovery Agent knows what the Front Desk said yesterday; the Front Desk knows the customer came in from a 'balayage near me' search. That shared brain is what makes the recovery rate work. Splitting them breaks the product."

## 4. Supervised autopilot — how the agents actually work

Most software is a tool: the owner pushes buttons, it does what it's told, and it stops the moment they stop. **Zoca is a team of agents.** They draft, suggest, schedule, and act on the owner's behalf — and they keep working when the owner is busy, asleep, or away. The owner reviews and confirms only when something needs a human in the loop. That's supervised autopilot.

> [!KEY TERM] **Supervised autopilot** — the agent acts on the owner's behalf; the owner reviews where it matters. It's the core UX of the product, and it's why we frame Zoca as a _team_, not software.

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] Pitching Zoca as "AI-powered software." Owners hear _another tool I have to learn_. The frame that wins is **"a team of agents that work for you."** Same product — one phrasing wins demos, the other loses to agencies.

## 5. The three operating modes — the human-in-the-loop model

The owner picks one of three modes, and they can set a **different mode per agent**. This is the answer to the "do I lose control?" objection — diagnose the owner, don't lecture.

| Mode                       | What happens                                                                                                         | Who picks it                                                           |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Approve**                | Every action is gated — nothing goes live until the owner taps approve in the **Content Queue**.                     | Cautious owners. Many new customers start here for the first ~14 days. |
| **Supervised** _(default)_ | The agent acts on its own and surfaces the important decisions in the Content Queue; the owner reviews periodically. | Most customers. Busy owners who want oversight without micromanaging.  |
| **Auto**                   | Full autopilot — the agent acts and reports; the owner sees results, not approvals.                                  | Experienced customers, usually past Day 90.                            |

Default is **Supervised**, and most customers stay there forever — agency-level output with founder-level transparency.

> [!KEY TERM] **Content Queue** — the dashboard inbox where blog posts, GBP posts, review responses, and service pages wait for the owner's approval before going live. It's the literal "human in the loop."

> [!TALK TRACK] _Owner: "I want to see everything before it goes out."_ — "Then you want **Approve** mode. Every blog, every reply, every post sits in your Content Queue; you tap approve, it goes live. About 90 seconds a day. Most owners switch to Supervised within two weeks because the approvals start feeling redundant — but you stay on Approve as long as you want."

> [!TALK TRACK] _Owner: "What if it says something wrong?"_ — "Two things. One, in Supervised and Approve the only fully-automated pieces are GBP posts and review responses, and you can flip review responses to approve-first in one click. Two, every draft uses _your_ business voice, _your_ service names, _your_ policies — it's not a generic chatbot. The brain is trained on your account."

## 6. Why the agents speak in first person

Throughout the dashboard, every agent uses "I": _"I'm setting up your Google Business Profile." "I published 3 posts today." "I found a keyword opportunity." "I'm ready to take calls 24/7."_

This is intentional and does two things:

1. **Frames the agent as a working partner, not a tool.** The relationship becomes coaching, not configuring.
2. **Builds a daily habit loop.** Opening the dashboard feels like reading a Slack standup from a teammate — what the team did overnight — not pulling a report from a database.

> [!DEMO TIP] Lean into this on a demo. Read the copy out loud: _"This line — 'I published 3 GBP posts this week' — that's the Discovery Agent talking to you. It's not a chart, it's a teammate telling you what it did. You're managing a team, not running software."_ That reframe lands deeper than any feature walk.

## 7. The progressive dashboard — Day 0 → Wk 3

The dashboard doesn't look the same on Day 0 as it does in Week 3. Most products show an empty skeleton and new customers feel like nothing's happening; Zoca uses **progressive disclosure** — the dashboard reveals more as data accumulates. Four states, switchable in the **Dev panel** for training and demos.

| State     | What the owner sees                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Day 0** | KPIs zeroed; setup checklists visible (forward calls, upload photos, check website); activity feed says "Front Desk is live and ready," "I'm optimizing your GBP"; Growth Band shows starting position. |
| **Day 2** | First GBP posts published; first review invites sent; 1–2 reviews trickling in; feed shifts to "I published / I scheduled / I responded."                                                               |
| **Wk 1**  | Measurable website traffic; first keywords on page 2–3; content pipeline active; 6+ reviews stacked.                                                                                                    |
| **Wk 3**  | Full KPI dashboard with ↑ deltas; Month 1 Report ready; "ChatGPT now recommends your salon"; feed shows real wins ("Front Desk recovered Lisa M. after a 67-day gap").                                  |

> [!KEY TERM] **Growth Band** — the customer's size tier by review count: **Spark (0–14) · Rise (15–39) · Prime (40+)**. It drives the bookings-per-month forecast. **Dev panel** — the bottom-right toggle in the Lovable preview reps use to switch day state, growth band, and ACV tier on a single demo account.

> [!DEMO TIP] The **Day 0 → Wk 3 reveal** is the single most powerful demo move you have. Set Day State to Wk 3 _before_ the prospect sees the dashboard, or run the reveal so the contrast lands — show the start and the destination together and they connect the dots themselves. Never leave them looking at zeros and silence.

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] Demoing from Day 0 only "because that's what loads by default." The prospect sees zeros, hears "this is your dashboard," and thinks _why am I paying for an empty page?_ Always set the contrast up first.

## 10. Ashley and the MBR — what happens after they sign

When a prospect asks "what happens after I sign?", the answer isn't "you log into a dashboard and figure it out."

- **Ashley (Get in Touch).** Every dashboard has a **Get in Touch** button in the header. One click opens a chooser: _Chat with Ashley_ (recommended), _Hop on a call_, or _Drop us a note_ (email). Ashley is the **AI account manager** — online 24/7, first reply under 30 seconds, trained on the customer's account end-to-end. Capability pills: Performance audit · Growth ideas · Instant fixes.
- **MBR (Monthly Business Review).** Every paid customer gets a recurring 30-minute strategic check-in with Ashley — not a support call, a performance review with the numbers, the wins, the problems, and the next 30 days agreed in writing. It has permanent left-panel real estate; the scheduling window opens the **7th** and closes the **15th** each month; past MBRs keep their recording, AI summary, and agreed actions.

Behind Ashley sits a real human team, sized to account value: **under $5K ACV** is led by the Customer Success pod; **$5K and over** gets a dedicated, named AM who proactively books the MBR.

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] Pitching Ashley as "our chatbot." Two problems: the product positions her as an **AI account manager**, and "chatbot" connotes 2018 dropdown menus. If "AI account manager" feels heavy, say "AI AM" — but never "chatbot."

> [!WHY IT MATTERS] The MBR is the single biggest retention lever in the product. When you sell, you sell **the platform plus the MBR cadence** — not just the software.

> [!GOOD TO KNOW] **One subscription, one billing cycle.** Adding an agent joins the _same_ subscription on the _same_ cycle — there are no separate per-agent subscriptions, and renewals consolidate everything active. ## 12. Best-fit framing (keep it soft)

Discovery Neo is sharpest for **beauty & wellness** — it knows the services, the vocabulary, and the rebook windows, so in-vertical shops get the most tailored results. That's a fit _signal_, not a wall: an out-of-vertical owner who wants in is still sellable — be honest the product is tuned for beauty and some category intelligence won't apply, then let them decide. The only genuine hard requirement anywhere is **a laptop or iPad in the shop** (onboarding and daily use depend on it). Everything else flexes — see the dedicated ICP lesson for how to read fit on a call.
