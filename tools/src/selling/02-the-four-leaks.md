# The four leaks

> [!TL;DR] Every beauty and wellness business has the same four places revenue leaks out every day: **new bookings** they don't show up for, **inbound calls** they can't answer, **bookings that stall mid-way**, and **first-timers who never come back**. The owner doesn't have a demand problem — they have a **capture problem**. Your job on a discovery call isn't to pitch features; it's to figure out which of the four leaks is biggest for this owner, name it back with a number tied to their dollars, and let the math do the selling. Memorize the four numbers — 37%, 22%, 31%, 52% — so you can cite them without looking down.

---

## 1. The setting — demand exists, capture doesn't

Every owner you talk to runs a business where demand already exists in their trade area today. People in their zip code want haircuts, color, lashes, facials, microneedling, fades — and they're searching for it on Google, Maps, ChatGPT, and Gemini right now. The owner does **not** have a demand problem. The owner has a **capture problem**. Demand is real, money is moving; the only question is who shows up when the customer goes looking. If the owner shows up, they get the booking. If they don't, the salon three doors down does. There is no third option.

> [!KEY TERM] **Capacity problem (vs. demand problem)** — the owner isn't short on demand; they're short on the time and skill to capture it. Every pitch comes back to this. They're working _in_ the chair, not _on_ the business.

## 3. Leak 01 — New bookings (the visibility gap)

The owner doesn't rank on Google, Maps, ChatGPT, or Gemini for the services they actually sell. Demand exists; they don't show up for it. The visibility gap vs. the top-ranked competitor in their trade area runs around **−37%** — a third of the new-customer pipeline gone before the customer even knows the salon exists.

> [!SOURCE NOTE] Sales Deck slide 03 — "−37% visibility gap vs. top-ranked competitor."

**Closes it:** the **Discovery Agent** — it ranks per location, per trade area, for non-branded service searches.

> [!KEY TERM] **Trade area** — the geographic radius where a location's customers actually come from, usually 3–5 miles in dense markets, not the whole city. **Non-branded search** — a customer Googling a service ("balayage near me") with no specific business in mind; the opposite of a branded search ("Maven Studio").

## 4. Leak 02 — Inbound calls

Roughly **1 in 4 calls (22%)** goes unanswered or abandoned during peak hours. Lunchtime, after work, Saturday morning — the exact times people decide to book — are the times the front desk can't pick up. Every missed call is a booking that walked next door.

> [!SOURCE NOTE] Sales Deck slide 03 — "22% of peak calls missed or abandoned." Case-study data (slide 17): a 22% missed-call rate before Zoca, dropped to **3% inside 90 days**.

**Closes it:** the **Front Desk Agent** — a voice concierge that picks up in under a second, knows the customer's history, books the appointment, takes the deposit, and logs it under the right location. Every dial tone becomes a confirmed booking.

> [!WHY IT MATTERS] This is why the product is **marketing + voice**, not marketing alone. The marketing brings the customer to the door; voice closes the booking when they pick up the phone. Either one alone leaves money on the table. A human front desk can't deliver a sub-second pickup at 9pm on a Sunday — not at one location, definitely not at twenty.

## 5. Leak 03 — Mid-booking drop-off (the "Black Box")

**31%** of booking flows stall mid-way. The customer landed on the website or called, started picking a service or a stylist or a date — then bounced. Wrong time, wrong stylist, wrong price. Nobody calls them back. The intent already happened; the money was halfway out of the wallet, and it never closed. This is the **single most leveraged leak** — the customer already chose the business. They just didn't finish.

> [!SOURCE NOTE] Sales Deck slide 10 — "31% of booking flows stall mid-way." Drop-off by step: Selecting Service 11% · Choosing Stylist 14% · Picking Date 9% · Picking Time 12% · **Deposit / Card Entry 18%** (the largest single step). Recovered at 42–58% on Zoca.

**Closes it:** the **Conversion Agent** (add-on) — it sees the stalled flow and follows up so the half-finished booking gets completed.

> [!KEY TERM] **The Black Box** — Leak 03, the mid-booking drop. The most leveraged leak because the intent already happened; the customer just didn't finish.

## 6. Leak 04 — First-timers who never return

**52%** of first-time guests never come back. No follow-up call, no rebook nudge, no reminder when their next service is due. The most expensive customer the owner will ever acquire — gone after one visit, often forever.

> [!SOURCE NOTE] Sales Deck slide 09 — "52% industry first-timer no-return rate today." Network rebook rate moves **48% → 65–72% within 90 days** on Zoca (same slide).

The math most owners have never done: if a salon spends $40 acquiring a first-timer and 52% never return, the **actual cost per retained customer is roughly $83**.

**Closes it:** the **Loyalty Agent** (add-on) — the follow-up, rebook nudge, and "your next service is due" reminder the owner never has time to send.

## 7. Name the leak, don't pitch the fix

The move that earns trust: name a **number**, tie it to what the prospect already said, tag it as a numbered leak, and tell them there are more. Specificity earns trust. "We can help you grow" earns nothing.

> [!TALK TRACK] **Naming the leak back** — "So just to play it back to you — you said the new location is dead. Industry-wide, when a single location opens fresh, the visibility gap vs. the top-ranked competitor in that trade area is around 37%. That's a third of new bookings gone before the customer even knows you exist. That's leak number one. We're going to look at the other three with you too — but I want to make sure I'm hearing the right one first."

> [!TALK TRACK] **Converting an industry stat into the owner's dollars** — "Industry-wide, 52% of first-time guests don't come back. So if you saw — call it 60 first-timers last quarter — and your full ticket is around $120 with the rebook, that's roughly $40K in lifetime revenue you walked past in 90 days. That number alone pays for the platform many times over."

> [!TALK TRACK] **"I don't have a marketing problem, I have a [whatever] problem"** — "Honestly, that might be true. Let me ask three quick numbers — what's your missed-call rate, what's your first-timer rebook, and what's the trend on new bookings. If those three are healthy, you don't have a marketing problem and we shouldn't be on this call. If one of them is leaking, that's where we'd start."

## 8. Why the owner can't fix this themselves

New reps get this wrong on the first call: they think the problem is **information** — that if they just explain SEO well enough, the owner will solve it. The problem isn't information. It's **capacity**. Beauty owners are working in the chair: doing color, training stylists, ordering product, fixing the schedule, dealing with no-shows, doing payroll. The owner who ranks on Google is the exception, and that exception almost always has a marketing agency behind them.

So the owner has two real options: **live with the leak** (most do), or **hire someone to fix it** — and that second option is usually where things get worse.

---

## 9. Why agencies fail this customer — and how to reframe it

Marketing and SEO agencies serving local beauty businesses charge **$800–$3,000/month**, sometimes more. Here's what the owner actually gets:

- **Junior staff** — the senior person sells the deal; the day-to-day goes to a 22-year-old account manager running 30 other accounts.
- **Slow turnaround** — weeks per market, per campaign; everything moves at the speed of one human writer with a queue.
- **No connection to bookings** — the agency runs ads or writes blogs; they never see the calendar and can't tell the owner whether the work produced a booking.
- **Manual repetition** — every new location is a new onboarding, keyword map, and content calendar; cost scales linearly with locations.
- **Opaque reports** — a monthly PDF of vanity metrics, no real-time dashboard.
- **Lock-in** — 3–6 month contracts, hard to cancel even when the work is bad.

The owner pays $1,500/month, gets four blog posts and a report, sees no change in chairs filled, cancels after six months — and tells the next vendor that "marketing doesn't work for our business." Assume every prospect has been burned before. They have.

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] **Bashing the agency on the call.** Don't. The owner picked that agency, often after researching it, and bashing the choice insults the owner. Reframe instead: "Yeah, an agency by design moves at the speed of one human writer. They're not built to do this in real time across multiple locations. That's a different kind of system — and that's the one we built." You're not attacking their judgment; you're explaining why a good decision two years ago no longer fits.

## 10. Why this is a _now_ problem, not a someday problem

Beauty has lived through demand-channel shifts: Yellow Pages → Google search → Yelp → Instagram → Google Maps. Each took years. Owners who moved early won; owners who waited got squeezed. The current shift is **faster and bigger** than any of those.

**~41%** of beauty searches now start in an AI chat — ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews — projected to hit **70%+** of local service searches by 2027. It's already happening, not a forecast.

> [!SOURCE NOTE] Sales Deck slide 06 — "41% of beauty searches now start in an AI chat. By 2027, projected 70%+ of local service searches will start in AI."

What's different about AI search: the answer returns **three names, not ten links**. There's no page 2, no "scroll down." If the brand isn't named in the answer, it's not losing rank — it's **invisible**. The brands cited over the next 18–24 months will own beauty discovery for the decade.

> [!TALK TRACK] **The AI search frame** — "Here's what's actually changing right now. When someone asks ChatGPT 'best balayage in Austin,' it doesn't return ten blue links — it returns three names. There's no page two. If your salon isn't one of those three, the customer doesn't know you exist. Today around 41% of beauty searches start in an AI chat. By 2027 that'll be 70%-plus. The brands cited in those answers in the next 18 months own the trade area for the decade. The brands that aren't will spend the decade trying to catch up. That's the shift."

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] **Performance-shaming the prospect about their AI visibility** — "You're not ranking on ChatGPT. You're invisible." Banned. The April 2026 compliance review specifically called out language like "you are not ranking," "you have been MIA," "you ghosted us" as language we never use, in any channel. The frame is _the market is shifting and we want to make sure you're in the answer set_ — not _you are failing_. (See **The Zoca values**.)

> [!DEMO TIP] Don't oversell this. Read the room. If the owner already knows about AI search, you don't need to teach. If they don't, this is the frame that moves the conversation from "do I need marketing?" to "how do I not get left behind?"

## 11. Putting it in dollars

Use this math when an owner asks "what is this actually costing me?" — for a single-location salon doing **$400K** in revenue:

| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Visibility gap (Leak 01)    | ~$40–60K in new bookings not captured      |
| Missed calls (Leak 02)      | 22% × call volume × ticket ≈ $20–35K       |
| Mid-booking drop (Leak 03)  | 31% of started bookings × ticket ≈ $30–50K |

## 13. Practice — name the leak back

> [!FOR THE AE] **Role-play (5 min, with a teammate).** Setup: a 2-location nail studio owner says she "just wants more bookings" and won't get specific — "I don't know SEO from a hole in the wall. I just need more bookings. Can you do that or not?" Your task: **don't pitch features.** Use the four leaks as a diagnostic. Ask 2–3 questions to find which leak is biggest, then name it back with a number tied to her revenue.
>
> **Scoring (5 points):**
>
> - [ ] Asked about missed-call rate, rebook rate, OR new-customer trend
> - [ ] Cited a real industry number (37%, 22%, 31%, 52%) — not "many" or "a lot"
> - [ ] Translated the percentage into a dollar figure using her revenue
> - [ ] Did **not** bash any tool or agency she mentioned
> - [ ] Closed with a clear next step (see the dashboard / pull a competitor scan / book a 20-min)
