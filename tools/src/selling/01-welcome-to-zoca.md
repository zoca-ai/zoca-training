# Welcome to Zoca & the dream

> [!TL;DR] Day one starts here. Zoca is the **marketing + voice platform for hyperlocal beauty and wellness businesses** — one system, one brain, one contract. We exist to do one thing: **fill the chair** at every location, every day, by closing the four leaks owners can't fix themselves. We lead with beauty & wellness because that's our sharpest fit, and we sell honestly to everyone else. We're aiming at an audacious target — IPO, global, a top-5 SMB company built from India — and your job inside that is simple to say and hard to master: tell the story in one breath, hear the owner's real problem, and be honest about fit. The only true prerequisite is operational — a **laptop or iPad in the shop**.

---

## 1. What Zoca is, in one line

> [!SAY IT SIMPLY] **"Zoca is the marketing + voice platform for hyperlocal beauty and wellness businesses."** Read it again. Every word is load-bearing.

- **Marketing + voice** — both, in one platform, sharing one brain. Not a marketing tool that bolted on voice. Not a voice tool that bolted on SEO. One system.
- **Hyperlocal** — every location of every brand gets its own intelligence. A salon in DTLA doesn't market the same way as a salon in Nashville, because their customers don't behave the same way. We treat them differently on purpose.
- **Beauty and wellness** — hair, med-spa, nails, lashes, brows, barbershops, wellness studios, and the in-vertical specialties (tattoo, piercing, permanent makeup). This is the vertical we're built for and the story we lead with. (Why the vertical is the moat comes later in the track.)

If a prospect asks "what is Zoca?" — that one line is the answer. Not "an AI agency." Not "an SMB SaaS." Not "the future of beauty." **That line.**

## 2. Say it cold, in one breath

This is the version you deliver when an owner picks up cold and asks what this is:

> [!TALK TRACK] "Zoca is the marketing + voice platform for hyperlocal beauty and wellness businesses. We get you ranked on Google and inside ChatGPT for what you actually sell, and we pick up every call your front desk misses. One platform, one contract."

Practice it out loud until it lands in **one breath**, with no commas where you stop to think.

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] Burying the one-liner under three sentences of warm-up — "so what we do is, basically, we're kind of like an AI thing for…" Cut all of it. Lead with the line. Owners decide in the first 10 seconds whether you're worth their next 20 minutes.

There's also a 25-words-or-fewer version for when "what does Zoca actually do for me?" comes mid-call:

> [!SAY IT SIMPLY] "We fill your chair. Every chair, every location. We handle Google, your phone, and your rebooks — automatically. You don't touch marketing again." (24 words.)

## 3. Why Zoca exists — fill the chair

Local beauty businesses run on **full chairs**. When a chair sits empty, the owner never gets that hour back. The whole company rolls up to closing **four leaks** that drain those chairs:

| #   | The leak                      | What it costs the owner                                                   |
| --- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **New bookings / visibility** | A new or quiet location nobody can find — great stylists, zero footprint. |
| 2   | **Missed calls**              | The phone rings, nobody picks up, the booking walks three doors down.     |
| 3   | **Mid-booking drop-off**      | Clients start to book and bail before they finish.                        |
| 4   | **First-timer churn**         | The most expensive customer they'll ever pay for is gone after one visit. |

Owners _feel_ every one of these. But they can't fix them — they're cutting hair, doing facials, training staff, running payroll. No time, no marketing skill, no budget for a $2,000-a-month agency that takes six weeks to publish a blog post. So they live with the leak: empty slots, missed calls, lost rebooks, quiet weeks they can't explain.

> [!WHY IT MATTERS] Zoca closes that leak — completely, automatically, with the owner learning nothing about marketing. That's the whole job. Every feature we ship, every call you take, every contract you sign rolls up to one outcome: **fill the chair, at every location, every day.**

> [!SOURCE NOTE] Sales Deck slide 04 — "Fill new bookings · Answer every call · Turn one-timers into regulars · Win back every lost booking" — the four promises rolled up to one outcome.

## 4. Who Zoca is for — best-fit, not a gate

Zoca is sharpest for a **beauty or wellness business that has crossed from "side hustle" into "real business."** Best-fit shows up when at least one of these is true:

- they run **more than one location**, or
- they **employ at least one person** besides the owner, or
- they do **more than $100K/year** in revenue.

That's the picture where the four leaks are biggest and Zoca closes them fastest — so it's where you spend your energy first and lead with the most confidence.

> [!RULE OF THUMB] Best-fit → move fast, lead with confidence. Lower-fit → still a real, sellable conversation; just be honest about what success looks like at their stage. We don't say "you don't qualify." We say "here's what Zoca will and won't move for a shop like yours right now."

The solo operator working from a spare room at $40K — no staff, one location — is **lower-fit, not a no.** The wins are smaller and slower, and you say so. The fit lifts the day they add a chair, a second location, or cross six figures. Beauty & wellness is where we're sharpest, but it isn't a wall either — an out-of-vertical owner who wants in can buy, as long as you're honest that the category-specific intelligence is tuned for beauty. (The full best-fit picture and how to read it on a call is the next lesson.)

> [!RULE OF THUMB] ⛔ **The one hard rule in the whole curriculum:** a **laptop or iPad must be in the shop.** Onboarding and the demo can't run without it. If there's no device, the move is "let's get a laptop or iPad at the front desk first — usually a quick fix — then we onboard." Everything else flexes.

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] Treating the threshold as a velvet rope. It isn't. It tells you where to aim and how to set expectations — not who to turn away.

## 6. The dream

This is not a lifestyle business. We're not building Zoca to be a comfortable mid-tier company that gets bought by a private equity firm and quietly absorbed. The dream — written down, said out loud, repeated at every all-hands — is:

> [!VALUE LINE] **IPO. Global. A top-5 SMB company built from India.**

> [!SOURCE NOTE] THE ZOCA VALUES, value 05 — "DREAM UNTIL IT SCARES YOU." Verbatim from the founder doc.

That's the ceiling we're aiming at. We hire people who want to build at that altitude and pass on people who don't. When a prospect asks where Zoca will be in five years, don't soften it:

> [!TALK TRACK] "Honest answer — IPO, global. We want to be the operating system every beauty business in the world runs on. That's not an investor slide; it's what we're building toward. What it means for you: we'll still be here in five years, the product depth keeps widening, and the price you lock in this year is the floor — not the ceiling."

> [!FOR THE AE] You'll be tempted to soften that. Don't. Owners who want to bet on a company that outlasts them respond to ambition. Owners who want a comfortable mid-tier vendor were never our fit anyway. And note where you're joining: the product is six months old in its current form, there's no senior comparable in India to copy, and the customers we sign this quarter are setting the blueprint the next 100 reps will run. We're figuring it out as we go — that's the job, not a bug in it.

## 7. What the job really is, on day 1

You're an AE or an AM. Either way, you are the **surface area between Zoca and the customer.** The product is good, the market is real, the dream is big — and none of it matters if you can't do four things:

1. **Fill chairs.** The job is not selling software. It's putting bookings in the chair. Every conversation traces back to that outcome.
2. **Tell the story in one breath.** By the end of day 1 you can explain what Zoca is, who it's for, and why it works in under 60 seconds. Owners don't have time for a 40-slide deck.
3. **Hear the actual problem.** Owners say they want "more bookings." That's rarely the real issue — it's usually one of the four leaks. Your job is to find which one and **name it back to them.**
4. **Be honest about fit.** If we're not their sharpest fit, you say so — and you still sell them honestly if they want in. That's value 04: **be honest, full stop.** You'll lose deals by promising things that aren't true; you'll keep customers for years by being the rep who leveled with them.

> [!OBJECTION] **"Are you guys an agency?"** — "No, the opposite. Agencies sell you marketing labor — junior staff, monthly PDFs, $2,000 a month. We sell you the outcome: bookings in the chair. Different business."

## 8. Model call — Marisol, 3-location hair studio, San Diego

A typical first call. Watch where the rep moves and where she lets the owner run.

> [!SALON EXAMPLE] > **0:00** — "Hi, this is Marisol. I've got you on the calendar but honestly I don't remember signing up — what is Zoca?"
> **0:08** — Rep: "Totally fair. Zoca is the marketing + voice platform for hyperlocal beauty businesses. We get you ranked on Google and inside ChatGPT for the services your customers are actually searching, and we pick up every call your front desk misses. One platform. Three locations is right in the sweet spot for us. That's the 30-second version — what made you take the call?"
> **0:35** — "We have a marketing person but I think she's drowning. We opened the third location last quarter and the new one is dead. Phone barely rings."
> **0:55** — Rep: "That tracks. New location, no Google footprint yet — best stylists in the city and nobody knows you exist for the first 90 days. What's your missed-call rate look like across the three?"
> **1:30** — "No idea. Probably bad. We close at 7, customers call at 8."
> **1:42** — Rep: "Right — industry average is about 1 in 4 calls at peak going to whoever picks up next. That's leak number one for you. The new location's visibility gap is leak number two. There are a third and fourth too — I'll walk you through them in 10 if it's useful. Quick scope check first: three locations, one marketing person — are all three doing six figures, or is the new one still ramping?"
> **2:30** — "Two are high three-hundreds. New one's on track for ~$200K year one."
> **2:42** — Rep: "Right in our wheelhouse. So here's the move — let me show you what your dashboard looks like at week 3 with the agents running across all three. Then if it's a fit, we talk price. Sound good?"
> **3:00** — "Yeah. Show me."

> [!FOR THE AE] What worked: led with the one-liner cold, no buildup; named the leaks **by number** before pitching features; read fit inside three minutes without it feeling like an interrogation; and **closed to the demo, not the price.** Price comes after the dashboard.

## 9. Selling honestly when they're not your sharpest fit

Best-fit isn't a wall, and "lower-fit" isn't a brush-off. When an owner is below the threshold or outside the sharpest vertical, you don't refuse them — you tell the truth about what Zoca moves at their stage and let them decide.

> [!TALK TRACK] **Lower-fit, said cleanly:** "The platform really hits its stride once you've outgrown doing it all yourself — multiple locations, staff, missed calls piling up. At where you are right now, some of what you'd pay for is capacity you don't need yet. If you want in, I'll set you up straight and we'll be honest about the timeline — and the day you add a chair or cross six figures, the value steps up fast. If you'd rather wait, I'll keep your contact and we pick it up then."

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] Saying "you don't qualify" or "you're too small." Never. Frame everything as the product's fit and stage — "here's what it'll move for you right now" — not a verdict on the customer. (The full best-fit playbook, including out-of-vertical owners, is the next lesson.)
