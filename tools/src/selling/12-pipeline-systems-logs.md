# Pipeline, systems & the dashboards you own

> [!TL;DR] Your pipeline is the honest record of where every deal actually is — not where you hope it is. Zoca runs **6 deal stages**, each with an objective entry condition; a deal moves forward only when the next stage's condition is met, moves backward when reality says so, and goes to Closed Lost with a logged reason. Run a 5-minute end-of-day review, apply the **14-day stale-deal rule** without flinching, and forecast only the deals with a confirmed next step. The discipline is the job — the clicks are easy. The same discipline applies to the **dashboards you can read without engineering**: before you escalate to CX or eng, check the view that already has your answer. Pricing here stays as placeholders and is **pending final confirmation** — never quote a number off the cuff.

---

## 1. Why this lesson is short

This is intentionally a short module. We are **not** going to teach you how to click through the CRM — your manager does that on Day 5 in twenty minutes flat. This lesson is about the **discipline** of pipeline management and reading your own dashboards. The clicks are easy. The discipline is what separates a good AE from a great one.

> [!SOURCE NOTE] Use whichever CRM your team has provisioned — the stage definitions and the discipline below are identical regardless of tool. (Source material references both a HubSpot-based stage workflow and a Pipedrive pipeline view; the framework doesn't change.)

---

## 2. The 6 deal stages and what each one means

Zoca uses **6 deal stages**. Each has a specific definition and a specific, objective condition to enter it. Don't blur them — a deal lives in **exactly one stage at a time**.

| #   | Stage                        | What it means                                                           | What's required to enter                                               |
| --- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Lead**                     | Inbound or outbound contact, not yet qualified                          | Name, business, contact info                                           |
| 2   | **Qualified**                | Passed the 5-question qualifying call, in best-fit range                | Notes from qualifying call, fit confirmed                              |
| 3   | **Demo Scheduled**           | Demo is on the calendar                                                 | Calendar invite, demo time confirmed                                   |
| 4   | **Demo Completed**           | Demo happened **and** a written next step exists                        | Recap email sent, next action calendared                               |
| 5   | **Negotiation**              | Pricing accepted in principle; paperwork or partner alignment in motion | Signature link sent **OR** contract sent **OR** partner call scheduled |
| 6   | **Closed Won / Closed Lost** | Signed and onboarding scheduled, **or** explicitly lost                 | Signature confirmed / loss reason logged                               |

> [!RULE OF THUMB] If a deal has been in **Demo Scheduled** for 12 days because the prospect rescheduled three times, it is **still Demo Scheduled**. Until the demo actually happens, you do not move it to Demo Completed. The stage reflects what happened, not what you expect to happen.

---

## 3. Moving deals forward, backward, and to lost — honestly

**Forward.** Move a deal forward only when the **required entry condition of the next stage has been met** — not when the deal "feels" further along. The conditions are objective for exactly this reason: they take your optimism out of the equation.

**Backward.** Yes, deals go backward, and that's healthy. If a prospect was in Negotiation (signature link sent) and they cool off, reopen a question, or need a partner who's no longer aligned, move the deal **back** to the stage that matches reality. A deal sitting in Negotiation that has actually slid back to "still thinking" is a lie to your forecast.

**Lost.** Closed Lost needs a **logged loss reason** — not a blank "didn't work out." The loss reason is what lets the team see patterns (price, timing, integration gap, went with a competitor) and it's what makes a future re-open possible.

> [!FOR THE AE] Stage inflation is the most common pipeline sin. A pipeline full of deals parked one stage ahead of where they really are feels great on Friday and falls apart at quarter-end. Honest staging now saves you the painful re-forecast later.

---

## 4. The daily pipeline review + the 14-day stale-deal rule

End every day with a **5-minute pipeline review**. You're not redoing the work — you're asking three questions of your board:

1. Did any deal meet the entry condition for the next stage today? Move it forward.
2. Did any deal actually slip? Move it backward, honestly.
3. **Has any deal sat untouched for 14 days?** Apply the stale-deal rule.

> [!KEY TERM] **The 14-day stale-deal rule** — any open deal with no movement and no logged activity for 14 days gets a decision, not a shrug: re-engage with a concrete next step, or move it to Closed Lost with a reason. Stale deals don't get to live in your pipeline inflating your forecast.

> [!RULE OF THUMB] Apply the 14-day rule **without flinching**. A deal you're "keeping warm" with zero activity for two weeks isn't warm — it's cold and you're hiding it. Either give it a real next action today or close it lost.

## 5. Forecast discipline

Your forecast is a promise to the team, so it's built on evidence, not hope.

> [!RULE OF THUMB] A deal is only **"likely close"** if it has a **confirmed next step** — a calendared call, a signature link sent, a contract out for review with a follow-up booked. No confirmed next step → it is **not** in your likely-close number, no matter how good the call felt.

The cleanest forecasts come from clean staging (Section 2) plus the daily review (Section 4). If your stages are honest, your forecast writes itself.

## 6. The recap email — mechanics that protect the deal

Every completed demo gets a **recap email within 4 hours** (a deal cannot legitimately sit in **Demo Completed** without one — that's the stage's entry condition). Keep it to **4 lines, plain text**. It names the pain, names the deal in writing, names the next step, and asks for anything missing.

> [!TALK TRACK] **Recap email body** — "Recap from today. (1) The leak we're fixing first — [pain]. (2) `[annual plan price]` annual / `[monthly plan price]` monthly, Discovery Neo bundle. (3) Next step — [signature link sent / call on date]. (4) Anything I missed?"

The non-negotiable mechanic: the recap **must contain the exact pricing the prospect agreed to verbally**. If you said the annual number on the call and the recap just says "Zoca pricing — let me know if you have questions," you have **not** named the deal. That gap is where the "I thought we said monthly" disputes, refund disputes, and legal disputes are born at month 2.

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] "The recap is a second pitch." No. The recap is a record, not a sales doc. What does **not** go in it: marketing copy, a second pitch, promises about Coming Soon features, "no contracts" wording, or **any pricing different from what was said on the call**.

> [!FOR THE AE] Loop in your senior AE / Sales lead by Slack **within an hour** of a notable call — don't sit on it. And when a deal advances, send the post-demo recording within 30 minutes: replay-worthy, shareable, advances the deal.

## 7. Pricing in the pipeline — say it cleanly, never invent it

The pipeline is where pricing discipline shows up in writing, so two rules carry straight through from the pricing lesson.

> [!COMPLIANCE NOTE] **Pricing is pending final confirmation.** Carry `[annual plan price]` and `[monthly plan price]` as placeholders in every recap and deal note until the final numbers are confirmed. **Reject stale numbers** — any figure floating around from an old deck ($179 / $249 / $269 / $279 / $290) is wrong. The only price flex is **annual vs. monthly**; discounts are not on the table.

> [!OBJECTION] **"Can you do a lower price — every dollar counts for a small business?"** — Hold the price; don't discount. Reframe to the leak math: "I hear you, and the reason I won't move on price is what we just showed you — those missed calls last week are worth far more than the gap you're asking about. Let's get you on annual; the **setup fee is waived on the annual plan**, which is real money back in your pocket, and I'll send the signature link before we hang up." Discounting unprompted trains this prospect — and the next five who hear about it — that the price is negotiable. It isn't.

> [!FOR THE AE] **5+-location prospect wants a number on the call?** Don't quote it. Multi-location is per-location at the base rate up to ~4 locations; **5+ is enterprise — escalate**. "For 5+ locations the standard pricing isn't the right shape. Let me get our enterprise lead on a 30-minute call this week — I'll send the invite tonight." Refusing to quote a number you'd have to walk back **builds** trust; an off-the-cuff "in the neighborhood" estimate destroys it.

## 8. The dashboards you can read without engineering

Most AEs escalate to engineering or CX the moment something looks off mid-demo. **About 80% of the time you don't need to** — the internal dashboards already answer the question. Know these by name, and **bookmark them today**.

| Dashboard                      | Owner (AE-readable)         | What you read it for                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Customer Health**            | CX-owned                    | Per-customer activation, login frequency, Agent usage, support tickets. 30 seconds here before a Day-7 check-in tells the full story.                                                                                 |
| **Sales Pipeline**             | AE-owned                    | Your CRM view with filters: deals stale >14 days, deals without notes, deals by stage. **Your weekly review starts here.**                                                                                            |
| **Agent Performance**          | per-customer, cross-team    | Live per-Agent metrics: Discovery Agent's GBP-views delta; Front Desk Agent's reply times + book-through rate; Loyalty Agent's send volume + review conversion. Answers "is my Agent working?" mid-Week-2 concretely. |
| **Front Desk Agent call logs** | engineering-owned           | Every call has a transcript + audio recording. If a prospect says "my demo call sounded weird," pull the transcript and verify or diagnose.                                                                           |
| **Weekly Team Revenue**        | manager-owned, all readable | Your team's new MRR, churn, activation rate, cycle time. Reps who check this weekly outperform reps who only check at quarter-end.                                                                                    |

> [!GOOD TO KNOW] Canonical Agent names appear across these dashboards: **Discovery Agent** and **Front Desk Agent** are the **Discovery Neo** core bundle every new logo gets; Loyalty, Conversion, Bookings & Payments, and Ads are add-ons / coming-soon and show up as separate lines where active.

## 9. Self-serving mid-demo — the polished path

A prospect on a live demo asks: _"How often does the Loyalty Agent send review requests?"_ Two paths:

- **Amateur path:** guess, or say "let me check with the team and get back to you." Momentum dies.
- **Polished path:** pull up the **canned account's Loyalty dashboard**, show actual send volumes and timing, answer concretely. The demo keeps moving.

The polished path only works if you've bookmarked the dashboards in advance.

> [!DEMO TIP] Build the weekly habit: spend **15 minutes once a week browsing the dashboards even with no specific question.** Over months you build a mental map of what "normal" looks like — which makes anomalies obvious at a glance, and makes you fast and credible when a prospect asks a live question.

---

## 10. When to still escalate

The dashboards don't answer everything. Still escalate when:

- The dashboard shows **abnormal numbers you can't explain** (potential bug or CX incident).
- A customer reports something the dashboard says **isn't happening** (likely a data-pipeline issue).
- **Legal or compliance questions** — always bypass AE debugging and go straight to compliance.
- A **deep technical integration question** you can't confidently answer. Honesty beats bluffing — "great question, let me get you a precise answer from the team" is the right move here, the opposite of how you handle a dashboard-answerable question.

> [!RULE OF THUMB] Dashboard-answerable → self-serve, keep momentum. Genuinely unknown or off-pattern → escalate honestly. The skill is knowing the line, and that comes from the weekly browse habit.

## Key terms

- **The 6 deal stages** — Lead → Qualified → Demo Scheduled → Demo Completed → Negotiation → Closed Won/Lost. Each has an objective entry condition; a deal sits in exactly one stage at a time.
- **Stage entry condition** — the concrete, observable thing that must be true to advance a deal (e.g., recap sent for Demo Completed, signature link sent for Negotiation). Forward moves are condition-based, not feeling-based.
- **14-day stale-deal rule** — any open deal untouched for 14 days gets re-engaged with a concrete next step or moved to Closed Lost with a logged reason.
- **Likely close** — forecast status reserved for deals with a **confirmed next step**; nothing without one counts.
- **The recap** — 4-line plain-text email within 4 hours of the demo that names the pain, names the deal in writing (exact agreed pricing), names the next step, and asks for missing info.
- **The dashboards** — Customer Health (CX), Sales Pipeline (AE), Agent Performance, Front Desk Agent call logs (eng), Weekly Team Revenue (manager). All AE-readable.
- **Polished path** — self-serving a mid-demo question from a dashboard instead of stalling with "let me check."
- **Discovery Neo** — the bundled core product (Discovery Agent + Front Desk Agent) every new logo gets.
