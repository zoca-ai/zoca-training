# The qualifying call

> [!TL;DR] The qualifying call is where you decide **who to demo**, not what to pitch. Run a fixed-order discovery frame — operational and shape questions first, revenue last — to read best-fit live, capture the prospect's **primary leak** and **top services**, and tee up a fully-loaded demo of **Discovery Neo** (Discovery Agent + Front Desk Agent). The product is shown before any payment. When a prospect isn't your sharpest fit, you don't turn them away — you set honest expectations and let them decide. And when the pricing question comes, you have **two numbers and a clean talk track** ready, anchored to the leak they just named.

---

## 1. Why qualifying beats pitching at this stage

Zoca is in the early innings of a hyperlocal beauty + wellness land grab, the deal cycle is short, and your competition (agencies, point tools) isn't waiting. The fastest way to fill a quarter is **not** to demo more — it's to demo the **right** people. Every minute spent demoing a lower-fit prospect is a minute not spent on a best-fit one.

> [!FOR THE AE] The qualifying call sits between SDR qualification and the mandatory discovery/demo. The SDR owns the first qualification pass; you don't re-qualify from scratch — you confirm the fit shape live, find the leak, and earn the demo. The discovery call itself is **fully loaded with all agents active** and comes **before any payment**.

> [!WHY IT MATTERS] Being straight about fit is Zoca Value 04 — _Be honest. Full stop._ A lower-fit owner you leveled with remembers you in two years when their single chair becomes four locations. Honesty at the top of the funnel is the long game.

## 2. The pre-call audit (4 minutes, before every call)

Before you dial, open three tabs and write down three numbers. These turn an ordinary discovery into a personalized one — the prospect feels seen.

1. **Their Google Business Profile.** Search the business name + city. Note review count, average rating, photo count, and categories. If the category is wrong (a med-spa filed as "wellness center"), flag it.
2. **A non-branded local search.** Pick one primary service and search "[service] near me" from their city. Where do they rank? Where's the #1 result? What's the gap?
3. **One AI search.** Ask an AI assistant "best [service] in [city]." Are they named? If not, that's your opening.

> [!RULE OF THUMB] Three numbers before you dial: **review count**, **rank for one service in one search**, and **whether AI search names them**. You'll use all three live on the screen-share bridge.

---

## 3. The five questions — in order

The frame is fixed and the order is fixed, because each answer informs how you ask the next. Lead with operational and shape questions; let revenue surface on its own.

> [!TALK TRACK] **Q1 — Locations & growth.** "Before I show you anything, I want to understand the business so I show you what's actually relevant. How many locations do you run today, and what does growth look like over the next 12 months?"

Reads the lead-shape layer. The moment there are two locations, the leak is structural (the owner can't be in both places) — that's **best-fit, any category**. "One location, opening two next quarter" → treat them as multi-location from here on.

> [!TALK TRACK] **Q2 — Team besides the owner.** "Got it. Besides yourself, how many people do you have — stylists, front desk, anyone running the marketing side?"

Tells you single-location-with-staff (strong fit, any category) vs. solo. It also reveals **who picks up the phone today**, which sets up the missed-call story in the demo.

> [!TALK TRACK] **Q3 — Revenue range.** "And just so I tailor this — are you under $100K a year, between $100K and $500K, or above $500K?"

Three buckets, never a specific number — owners answer ranges, not "what's your revenue." This is your size and ACV signal: a single-location solo shop is **strongest fit at $100K+**, and is still workable below with honest, modest expectations.

> [!TALK TRACK] **Q4 — Current marketing stack.** "Tell me what you're running today — an agency, point tools like a separate booking system plus a review tool plus a website host, or is most of this just you?"

Three buckets, each sets up a different demo:

- **Agency** → lean on the deliverable/cost comparison (see §7).
- **Point tools** → lean on the "one platform, one brain" frame — simplicity over a five-vendor stack.
- **Nothing** → lean on the audit reveal: show the gap before the fix.

> [!TALK TRACK] **Q5 — The single biggest pain.** "Last one. If you could fix one thing this quarter — visibility, the phone going unanswered, customers not rebooking, or stuff dropping mid-booking — which one would actually move your P&L?"

This maps them onto one of the **four leaks**. Whatever they name is the leak you lead the demo with — and the number you anchor pricing to later.

> [!KEY TERM] **The four leaks** — (1) new bookings / visibility, (2) missed calls, (3) mid-booking drop-off, (4) first-timer churn. Q5 makes the prospect pick their own.

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] Opening with the revenue question. Don't — owners get defensive when a stranger leads with "what's your revenue?" By Q3, the location and staff answers have already told you the size, so the range question feels like normal scoping.

## 4. Reading best-fit live (and capturing the leak)

While you run the frame, you're scoring fit in real time — softly. None of these is a verdict on the customer; they're signals for how fast and how big the wins will be.

| What you hear                                                                   | Fit read                     | What you do                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Multi-location (2+), any category                                               | **Best-fit**                 | Lead with confidence; structural leak, no revenue floor                           |
| Single location + staff, any category                                           | **Strong fit**               | Green light; move fast                                                            |
| Single location + solo, $100K+                                                  | **Strong-line fit**          | Sellable and solid; lead with the leak                                            |
| Single location + solo, under $100K                                             | **Workable, lower-fit**      | Still sellable — set modest, honest expectations                                  |
| Low-ticket single location (threading-only, nails-only, eyelash-only, braiders) | **Lower-fit, not excluded**  | Sell with honesty; fit lifts at a 2nd location / service line                     |
| Tattoo, piercing, permanent makeup                                              | **In-vertical, best-fit**    | Sell like any other beauty shop                                                   |
| Outside beauty & wellness (dental, gym, restaurant)                             | **Outside the sharpest fit** | Be honest the product is tuned for beauty; name what still works; let them decide |

> [!RULE OF THUMB] The lead-prediction signal for healthy demand is **≥60 leads over the next six months**. Thinner demand isn't a no — it means slower, smaller wins, and you say so out loud.

> [!FOR THE AE] **Capture two things on every call:** the prospect's **primary leak** (their Q5 answer) and their **top services** (from the audit and the call). The leak is what you lead the demo with; the top services are what the Discovery Agent will speak the language of and what you'll quote ranking gaps against.

## 5. The one hard rule — and the honest lower-fit move

> [!RULE OF THUMB] ⛔ **Hard rule — the only one.** A **laptop or iPad must be in the shop.** The onboarding demo and daily use depend on it. Everything else flexes. If there's no device: "let's get a laptop or iPad at the front desk first — usually a quick fix — then we onboard." Confirm it early: _"Quick scope question — is there a laptop or iPad at the front desk the team uses day to day?"_

When a prospect isn't your sharpest fit, you **never disqualify, decline, or say "you don't qualify."** You tell the truth about what Zoca will move at their stage and let them choose.

> [!TALK TRACK] **Lower-fit, in-vertical (said cleanly).** "You're in our world for sure. I want to be straight on fit: at your stage — [solo / lighter demand / brand-new GBP] — Zoca still helps, but the wins are smaller and slower than for a multi-chair shop. If that's worth it to you, I'll set you up right and we'll be honest about the timeline. And the day you add a second chair, open a second location, or your reviews build, the value steps up fast."

> [!TALK TRACK] **Outside beauty & wellness (honest, not a refusal).** "Fair warning so you go in eyes open — Zoca is built specifically for beauty and wellness, so a lot of the smarts — the service vocabulary, the rebook timing — are tuned for that world and won't map perfectly onto [dental / a restaurant / a gym]. The core — getting found, answering your calls — still works. If that's the part you want, I'm glad to set you up."

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] Saying "you're too small" or "you don't qualify." Never. Frame everything as the product's fit and stage — "here's what it'll move for you right now" — not a verdict on the person.

## 6. The bridge to demo

Once you've read the fit and named the leak, the move is to open their **own** Google Business Profile on a screen-share before transitioning to the demo. This is the bridge.

1. "Mind if I share my screen for one minute?" (Always ask. Always one minute, never longer.)
2. Pull up their actual GBP.
3. Show three things — review count vs. the top local competitor, their rank for one primary service on a "near me" search, and whether AI search names them for "best [service] in [city]."
4. **Don't fix anything yet** — just narrate the gap.
5. "Want me to show you what we'd do about this in a 15-minute walkthrough? Or do you have a hard stop?"

> [!TALK TRACK] **When they say "just send me something."** "I can do this two ways. I can send a recap and we lose two weeks figuring out calendars, or I can give you 15 minutes right now and you walk away knowing whether this is a fit. Which one?" Most pick the 15 minutes. The qualifying call ends with a **confirmed next step**, not a recap.

> [!TALK TRACK] **The "not yet" hold (right fit, wrong timing).** "You're 90 days early. Let me set a reminder and come back to you when your second chair is in — no outbound from us in the meantime; when you're ready, you'll hear from me directly." Mark it as "right fit, timing wrong — follow up [date]." Do **not** drop them back onto a cold outbound list.

## 7. The pricing & terms talk track (when they ask "what does this cost?")

If the prospect asks price during or right after the demo, quote it in **one clean sentence, no qualifiers**, then push annual and anchor to their leak.

> [!SOURCE NOTE] **Pricing is pending final confirmation.** Use the placeholders below, never a dollar figure. The historical numbers **$179 / $249 / $269 / $279 / $290 are all stale — reject them if a prospect or old doc quotes them.**

> [!TALK TRACK] **The reveal.** "It's `[annual plan price]`/month on annual, or `[monthly plan price]`/month month-to-month. That's the Discovery Agent and Front Desk Agent bundled together — same number per location."

> [!VALUE LINE] **Push annual with the setup-fee lever.** There **is** a setup fee, and it's **waived when you choose the yearly plan** — the waiver is baked into annual pricing. That's a concrete reason to go annual, on top of the locked rate. (Never quote a setup-fee number — it's pending.) "Switch to annual at any time — one click, apply at end of cycle or immediately, and you land right on your subscription page."

> [!TALK TRACK] **The add-on question ("what about all the other agents?").** Handle it honestly:
>
> - **Bookings & Payments (B&P)** — free monthly fee + 2.7% per transaction. Zoca's payment rail.
> - **Loyalty Agent** — $49/mo, includes 500 SMS + 100 AI calls. Off until you turn it on.
> - **Conversion Agent** — $49/mo + 10% of recovered booking value. Upside-only, and it **requires B&P connected** — quote that prerequisite every time.
> - **Ads Agent** — pricing TBD; **don't quote it.**
> - **Multi-location** — per-location at base rate up to ~4 locations; **5+ goes enterprise — escalate.**

> [!OBJECTION] **"Is this all in?"** — "Base bundle is `[annual plan price]` or `[monthly plan price]`. Loyalty is $49/mo when you turn it on, Conversion is $49 + 10% of recovered, both opt-in. Ads pricing is TBD. Bookings & Payments is free + 2.7% per transaction. Everything else is in the base."

> [!VALUE LINE] **The agency-cost anchor.** Against a roughly **$40,700/yr agency equivalent**, Discovery Neo runs `[annual plan total/yr]` on annual (`[monthly plan total/yr]` on monthly) — about a **91% saving**. But don't lead with the slogan; run the line items, then anchor to **one of the four leaks they named in Q5.** The math wins; the feature dump doesn't.

> [!COMMON MISCONCEPTION] Quoting per-chair, per-stylist, or per-call. We don't charge that way — **one number per location.** And the public-facing phrase is **"no long-term contracts,"** never "no contracts" (we do sign service agreements).

> [!GOOD TO KNOW] **One subscription, one billing cycle.** Adding agents later joins the same subscription on the same cycle — no separate per-agent subscriptions, no re-signing. Downgrades turn an agent off with no mid-cycle refund and no new subscription; cancellation lives in its own flow, handled distinctly for monthly vs. yearly.

---

## 8. Three failure modes to avoid

- **Pitching the agency math as a slogan.** "You save 91%" alone is air. The line items ("96 blog posts × $150 + …") make it real.
- **Defending the price with a feature list.** Anchor to one of the four leak-promise numbers tied to _their_ leak, not a feature dump.
- **Promising a price "forever" without the annual commitment.** The lock-in is the annual agreement. Monthly customers pay `[monthly plan price]` unless they upgrade.

## 9. Model scenario — Marisol, hair salon, Phoenix (~$420K)

Reyes Hair Studio, Phoenix AZ, one location, four stylists, ~$420K.

- **0:00 — Q1.** "Before I show you anything… how many locations, and what does growth look like over 12 months?" → One location; wants a second (Scottsdale) by end of next year. _You treat her as multi-location from here._
- **1:00 — Q2.** "Besides yourself, how many on the team?" → Four stylists, part-time front desk, no marketing. _Strong fit; front desk is part-time → missed-call leak is live._
- **1:45 — Q3.** "Under $100K, $100K–$500K, or above?" → Around $420K. _Comfortably above the strong line._
- **2:15 — Q4.** "What are you running today?" → On a local agency 11 months. _Demo leans on the deliverable/cost comparison._
- **Q5 — the leak.** Whatever she names becomes the demo's lead and the pricing anchor. _Capture it, plus her top services, before you bridge to the screen-share._

Read: **best-fit** (multi-location plan, staffed, well above $100K, in-vertical hair). Lead with confidence, bridge to the demo, and have the two-number pricing talk track ready.

## Key terms

- **Qualifying call** — the fixed-order discovery frame (operational → shape → revenue → stack → leak) that decides who to demo and reads best-fit live.
- **The five questions** — locations/growth, team, revenue range, marketing stack, single biggest pain — in that order, always.
- **The four leaks** — visibility, missed calls, mid-booking drop-off, first-timer churn; Q5 makes the prospect pick one.
- **Discovery Neo** — the bundled core product every new logo gets: Discovery Agent + Front Desk Agent, sold together.
- **Best-fit (not a hard no)** — fit is a targeting and expectation tool; the only hard rule is a laptop/iPad in the shop.
- **Lead signal** — ≥60 leads over the next six months marks healthy demand.
- **Add-ons** — B&P (free + 2.7%), Loyalty ($49/mo + 500 SMS / 100 AI calls), Conversion ($49/mo + 10% recovered, requires B&P), Ads (TBD — don't quote).
- **Setup fee** — exists; waived on the yearly plan; amount pending.
- **Agency-cost anchor** — ~$40,700/yr agency equivalent vs. `[annual plan total/yr]` on Zoca.
